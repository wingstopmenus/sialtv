A squad bowler's chance

Imran Mir's name appears in the Season 5 auction records as a Gulf Giants signing at $10,000, listed as a bowler. No profile of his game has been published beyond those lists: his batting and bowling styles are not on the public record, so this page leaves them blank rather than guessing. The opportunity itself is real enough — a squad place with the league's largest group, and daily training with Alzarri Joseph, Shamar Joseph, Blessing Muzarabani and Tabraiz Shamsi.

  • Role: Squad member
  • Team: Gulf Giants
  • Batting: TBC
  • Bowling: TBC

A season like this is won in the nets as much as in matches. Mir's job is to impress the coaches every day, working with the Giants' overseas attack and learning how the Joseph brothers plan a new-ball spell and how Shamsi reads a batter, so that when a place opens he is the name they reach for. The Giants play six of their ten league matches in Dubai, so any chance he gets is likely to come on pitches he knows.

The route into the eleven

Mir's path into the team runs through the bowling group. The Giants' attack is built on overseas pace, but ten league matches in 24 days is a heavy load, and the bowlers who stay fit decide the season. If the Giants rest a seamer, or if an injury opens a place, the squad bowlers are first in line — and the franchise has shown it values depth, building the largest squad in the league at 23 players.

His Season 5 is about being ready: ready to bowl, ready to field, and ready to take the chance when it comes. In a squad full of stars, the players who make themselves undroppable in training are the ones who get the call.

Depth that wins short tournaments

Ten league matches in 24 days are a squad's examination, not an eleven's. Players get injured, form dips, and the sides that stay in the top four are the ones whose 15th or 20th player can step in without the side falling apart. Imran Mir is part of that depth for the Giants — the group behind the stars that keeps the squad functioning through a long month.
