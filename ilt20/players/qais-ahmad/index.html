The Afghan leg-spinner

Qais is a right-arm leg-break bowler who bats right-handed, and he has been part of Afghanistan's white-ball plans since his teens. He was a member of the Afghanistan squad that reached the semi-finals of the 2024 T20 World Cup, and he has played in franchise leagues around the world.

His T20 game is built on variety: a big leg-break, a googly that is hard to pick, and a quicker ball that hurries batters. He has played in the Big Bash for Hobart Hurricanes, in the Caribbean Premier League, in the Lanka Premier League and in the ILT20, and his record in the format is consistently strong. The Knight Riders paid $38,000 for him — a low price for a bowler of his quality — and he is one of four Afghans in the squad, which matters with two required in every playing eleven.

Career so far

Qais made his Afghanistan debut in 2017, at 17, and has played more than 50 T20Is. He was part of the side that reached the 2024 T20 World Cup semi-finals in the Caribbean, and he has been a regular in Afghanistan's spin attack alongside Rashid Khan and Mujeeb Ur Rahman.

His franchise career has taken him to Hobart Hurricanes in the BBL, where he was a regular for three seasons, and to the CPL, the LPL and the ILT20. His googly has troubled some of the best batters in the world. At 26, he is entering his prime as a wrist-spinner — and the Knight Riders have signed him at the right time.

The spin group around him

Four frontline spinners is a luxury few T20 sides enjoy, and the Knight Riders have it: Narine's mystery, Gul's young wrist-spin, Tariq's off-spin — and Qais, the Afghan leg-spinner whose job is to be the second wrist-spinner — the one who bowls in the middle overs, takes wickets, and lets Narine control the game from the other end. Six of the Knight Riders' ten league games are in Abu Dhabi, where spin has often decided matches, and a leg-spinner who can also bat a little is exactly the profile this squad needs.
