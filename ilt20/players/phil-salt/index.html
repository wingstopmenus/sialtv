The powerplay destroyer

Phil Salt's game is simple to describe and hard to stop: he attacks from the first ball. His back-to-back T20 international hundreds against West Indies in December 2023 — 109 not out and 119 — announced him as an opener who can win a match in the powerplay, and his IPL season with Kolkata Knight Riders in 2024, where he opened with Sunil Narine and helped the side to the title, showed the same thing in franchise cricket. He has played for England in all three formats, but T20 is his home.

  • Role: Wicketkeeper
  • Team: Dubai Capitals
  • Batting: Right-handed
  • Born: 28 August 1996

The $80,000 fee looks like one of the bargains of the auction for a player of his record. The Capitals bought him to set the tempo: if Salt scores quickly in the first six overs, the middle order of Stoinis, Shanaka and Powell bats with freedom. If he fails, the innings has to be rebuilt. That is the deal with an opener like him, and the Capitals have made it gladly.

Keeper, opener, tone-setter

Salt's most likely role is opener and wicketkeeper, which solves two problems at once: it gives the Capitals their fastest scorer at the top and frees an overseas slot that a specialist keeper would use. He will keep wicket to Mustafizur and Noor Ahmad, which is not an easy job — standing up to the cutters and reading the wrist-spin — but he has done it for England and in the IPL. Tom Moores is the back-up keeper if the workload needs sharing.

His partnership with du Plessis is the one to watch. The captain is a placement batter who builds an innings; Salt is the player who takes it away from the opposition in six overs. Together they give the Capitals a powerplay that can produce 60 runs or lose two wickets — and the champions have decided the first outcome is worth the risk.

A top order that attacks from ball one

The Capitals' batting identity is attack, and Salt is the player who sets it. Du Plessis, Salt and Stoinis are three of the most destructive top-order batters in the league, and on Dubai's true pitches with a fast outfield, a powerplay of 60 or more is a realistic target every game. The middle order exists to finish what Salt starts.
