The best hitter of spin in the world

Heinrich Klaasen's reputation rests on a single skill: nobody in world cricket hits spin bowling as far or as cleanly as he does. His unbeaten 174 off 83 balls against Australia at Centurion in 2023 — 13 sixes, most of them against spin — is the innings that made the point, and his record since has confirmed it: in the IPL for Sunrisers Hyderabad, in the SA20, and for South Africa, he has been the batter opposition captains plan their spin around, and the plan usually fails. He was South Africa's star at the 2023 World Cup, where his hundreds against England and Bangladesh carried the side.

  • Role: Wicketkeeper
  • Team: Gulf Giants
  • Batting: Right-handed
  • Born: 30 July 1991

Klaasen was one of the names the Giants tied down before the auction, keeping him out of the bidding altogether — the clearest sign a side can give that it does not want to lose a player. In a batting order full of stars, Klaasen is the one who decides matches against spin — and in the UAE, where every side bowls spin, that is the most valuable skill in the league.

The middle-order destroyer

Klaasen bats at four or five for the Giants, behind Buttler and Gurbaz, and his job is to take the innings from a good start to a winning total. He is at his best when the spinners come on: he steps down the pitch, hits through the line, and clears the ropes with a still head and a full swing. His strike rate against spin is the best in the world, and on Dubai's pitches — where the ball grips and the spinners bowl in the middle overs — he is the batter every opposition fears most.

He also keeps wicket, which gives Jos Buttler options: Klaasen can take the gloves if the captain needs a rest from keeping, or if the side wants an extra bowler. His versatility is part of his value, but his batting is the reason he was retained.

A batting order built for spin

Gulf Giants' batting is the best in the league on paper, and Klaasen is the player who makes it work against spin. Buttler attacks the pace, Klaasen attacks the spin, Miller finishes — and Livingstone does all three. That is a batting order with no weakness: pace, spin, left-arm, right-arm, every kind of bowling has a batter who punishes it.
