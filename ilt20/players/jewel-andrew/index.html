The young keeper who broke in early

Jewel Andrew arrives at MI Emirates as a wicketkeeper-batter with a rare head start. He was signed in the days before the season opener, and at 19 he already carries two West Indies age records. For MI Emirates, he is the young keeping option in a squad that also leans on Kusal Perera behind the stumps. Andrew bats right-handed and is comfortable near the top of the order, where his quick hands and straight hitting give the side a fresh look. His job in Season 5 is simple: take the chances that come his way, keep the scoreboard moving, and make the most of every game he gets.

What to watch is how fast he settles. The opener against Desert Vipers in Dubai on Saturday 21 November is the biggest stage he has played on in franchise cricket, and the new ball will test his judgment early. Behind the stumps, the real examination comes when Rashid Khan is turning the ball past the bat. Andrew has kept to good spin before, and his footwork and clean hands are the reason the franchise moved for him so late. If he finds his rhythm in the first week, MI Emirates gain a keeper who can bat in the top four and grow through the season.

Career so far

Andrew's rise has been quick. He played the Under-19 World Cup in 2024, and by October that year he was in the West Indies one-day side. His ODI debut came against Sri Lanka at Pallekele on 26 October 2024, when he was 17, making him the youngest West Indies ODI debutant. The next year brought a Twenty20 international debut against Pakistan, and that too set a record: at 18 years and 236 days, he became the youngest West Indies T20I debutant. Few players reach both marks before their twentieth birthday.

At home he plays for the Leeward Islands, and in the Caribbean Premier League he turns out for Antigua and Barbuda Falcons. He has also appeared for Saint Lucia Kings and Guyana Amazon Warriors, picking up franchise experience across the region. His first-class record already includes a hundred, with a top score of 122, which shows the batting runs deeper than the keeping. ILT20 Season 5 is his next step: a full franchise season against the best bowling attacks the league can put together.

The batting around him

The batting around Andrew is full of power. Nicholas Pooran and Sherfane Rutherford hold the middle order, Kusal Perera brings experience at the top, and Muhammad Waseem and Ross Adair offer pace at the start of the innings. Behind them, captain Kieron Pollard and Romario Shepherd finish innings the way they have done for years. Andrew does not need to carry this batting line-up. He needs to fit into it, find his role in the order, and add runs where the game asks for them.

The bowling gives him just as much to work with. Rashid Khan leads the attack, Fazalhaq Farooqi takes the new ball, and Chris Jordan and Allah Ghazanfar cover the rest. Keeping to that group is a job in itself, and it is where Andrew can prove his worth even on a quiet day with the bat. MI Emirates open against Desert Vipers in Dubai on 21 November, then face Gulf Giants on 24 November and Abu Dhabi Knight Riders on 26 November. Three games in six days will tell the side plenty about its new keeper.
