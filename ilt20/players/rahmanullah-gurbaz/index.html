The World Cup's top scorer

Rahmanullah Gurbaz's 2024 was one of the great years in T20 cricket: he finished as the top run-scorer at the T20 World Cup, with 281 runs as Afghanistan reached the semi-finals for the first time, and then won the IPL with Kolkata Knight Riders, where his opening partnership set the tone for the title. He has scored a hundred on his one-day international debut, against Ireland in Abu Dhabi in 2021, and he has been Afghanistan's first-choice opener and keeper for years. His game is built on power and intent: he attacks from the first ball, hits through the off side, and scores quickly in the powerplay.

The $310,000 fee — paid through the Giants' right-to-match card — made him the second-most expensive player of the auction, behind only Liam Livingstone. The Giants broke the league's auction record twice in one afternoon, and Gurbaz was the second of those two signings. That is the scale of the franchise's ambition.

The opener who sets the tempo

Gurbaz's role is to open the batting with Ibrahim Zadran and set the tempo: attack the powerplay, score quickly, and give Buttler, Klaasen and Miller a platform to build on. His partnership with Zadran is an Afghan one — two batters who know each other's game from years of playing together for their country — and it is the opening pair the Giants have built their season around. He will also keep wicket, which gives Buttler the option of playing as a pure batter if the side needs it.

His family subplot writes itself: his younger brother Masood plays for Dubai Capitals, after switching his allegiance to the UAE. If both brothers play in the same match — Gulf Giants against Dubai Capitals on 28 November or 10 December — it will be one of the stories of the season.

A batting order with no end

Gulf Giants' batting is the strongest in the league on paper: Gurbaz and Zadran open, Buttler, Klaasen, Miller and Livingstone follow, and the side does not stop batting until the tail. Gurbaz is the player who starts that batting order — the opener whose powerplay scoring decides whether the middle order bats with freedom or under pressure. On Sharjah's small ground, where the Giants play twice early, his hitting is worth more than anywhere else in the league.

What’s next

The Giants open away from home, and their record opener gets his first outing in Sharjah: Sharjah Warriorz on Sunday 22 November, in the afternoon.
