From truck driver to international bowler

Matiullah Khan's journey to international cricket was anything but straightforward. He worked as a long-distance truck driver before his cricket took off, and he came to the game late, through company cricket in the UAE rather than an academy system. His breakthrough came in the Abu Dhabi T10 in 2022, where his seam bowling earned him a stint with Abu Dhabi Knight Riders in the ILT20, and he made his ODI debut for the UAE on a tour to Namibia in 2023. His T20 international debut followed on 17 May 2025, against Bangladesh in Sharjah.

  • Role: Bowler
  • Team: Gulf Giants
  • Batting: Right-handed
  • Bowling: Right-arm medium-fast
  • Born: 28 February 1993

He has played for Desert Vipers as well as the Knight Riders, and his favourite memory in the league is taking Kieron Pollard's wicket for the Vipers against MI Emirates. He has played only a handful of internationals — one ODI and five T20 internationals — but his story is the one the ILT20 was built for: a working cricketer who earned his place through performance, not privilege.

The third franchise in three seasons

Matiullah's move to Gulf Giants is his third ILT20 franchise, after the Knight Riders and the Vipers, and it says something about how the league values experience: the Giants have signed a bowler who knows the grounds, knows the conditions, and knows how to bowl in Dubai. His role is as the experienced local seamer — the bowler who can take the new ball if needed, bowl in the middle overs, and guide the younger quicks through a long month.

At 33, he is one of the oldest players in the squad, and his Season 5 is about showing that experience still counts. The Giants' pace attack is built on youth and speed; Matiullah is the bowler who knows when to bowl the slower ball, and when to keep it simple.

The local seamers' group

Gulf Giants' pace attack is built on the Joseph brothers, but the local seamers matter just as much: Matiullah Khan, Haider Rarraq and Awais Ahmad give the Giants cover across the attack, and the league's rules mean every eleven needs its UAE players. Matiullah is the senior member of that group — the one the younger bowlers look to, and the one the captain trusts when the game is tight.
