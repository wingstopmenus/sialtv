The UAE left-arm all-rounder

Keswani is a left-handed batter and slow left-arm orthodox bowler who plays as an all-rounder, and he is one of the UAE's most promising young cricketers. He represented his country at the 2022 Under-19 World Cup, scoring 39 runs and taking two wickets in the opening win over Canada, and he made his senior international debut in September 2023.

He has since played T20I and ODI cricket for the UAE, and he featured for Desert Vipers Development in the 2026 ILT20 Development Tournament — the pathway that earned him a place in the main squad. The Vipers bought him at the Season 5 auction, and he is one of four UAE players in the champions' squad — the local core of a title defence. His job is to be the left-arm spinner who can also bat — the kind of cricketer every T20 side wants.

Career so far

Keswani came through the UAE's age-group system, playing at the 2022 Under-19 World Cup, and made his senior debut in September 2023. He has played 12 T20Is for the UAE, taking 11 wickets at an economy of 7.03, and he has also played ODI cricket.

His domestic career has taken him to Dubai, Emirates Blues and Sharjah Warriorz, and his development-tournament performances for the Vipers in 2026 earned him a place in the main squad. His left-arm spin is his main weapon — he bowls with control and variation — and his left-handed batting gives the lower order depth. At 23, he is the future of UAE cricket — and the Vipers have signed him for the title defence.

The spin group around him

Behind Mujeeb's mystery, Shadab's leg-spin and Kharote's left-arm, the Vipers have a fourth spinner in Keswani — the strongest spin attack in the league.

Keswani's role is to be the fourth spinner — the UAE all-rounder who can bowl in the middle overs, take wickets, and give the captain a left-arm option. Six of the Vipers' ten league games are in Dubai, where spin has often decided matches, and a fourth spinner who is also a UAE all-rounder gives the side balance.
