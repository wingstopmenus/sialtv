The middle-order destroyer

Andre Russell is back where he belongs. The Knight Riders brought him back as their wild-card pick for Season 5, and he is the only franchise he has ever played for in the ILT20, with 38 appearances across the first four seasons. He bats in the middle order and finishes games, and he bowls fast at the death. In 38 ILT20 matches he has scored 675 runs at a strike rate of 166.66 and taken 23 wickets, numbers that say plainly what he does: he changes games in ten balls.

Season 4 gave him a milestone no other T20 cricketer had reached. His 500th T20 wicket, taken against Desert Vipers, made him the first player in the format's history to pass 5,000 runs, 500 wickets and 500 sixes. At 38 he is no longer the new-ball bowler he once was, but his value has not dropped. He will bat at five or six, bowl the hard overs, and stand in the outfield where the big catches come. The Knight Riders have chased a first title for four seasons, and Russell is the player they trust most when a game is on the line.

Career so far

Russell played for West Indies from 2010 to 2025 and retired from international cricket in July 2025, after 86 T20 internationals, 56 one-day internationals and a single Test. He was part of the West Indies sides that won the T20 World Cup in 2012 and 2016, and he was named in the team of the tournament in 2016. His one-day debut came at the 2011 World Cup against Ireland, and his T20 international debut followed a month later against Pakistan.

His franchise career is one of the longest in the short game. He has played for Kolkata Knight Riders since 2014, winning the Indian Premier League with them in 2014 and 2024, and his 510 runs at a strike rate above 200 in the 2019 season remain one of the great IPL campaigns. He has also played in the Big Bash, the Caribbean Premier League, the Hundred and Major League Cricket, passing 9,000 T20 runs and 480 T20 wickets along the way. In 2026 he became only the third batter in history to hit 800 sixes in T20 cricket.

The Knight Riders around him

Russell slots into a side built to win now. Jason Holder captains the team and leads the seam attack, Sunil Narine opens the bowling and bats in the top order, and Finn Allen, Colin Munro and Andre Fletcher give the batting its early pace. Moeen Ali and Gulbadin Naib add more all-round depth, so Russell does not have to carry the middle order alone the way he sometimes has in past seasons.

The local players matter just as much. Alishan Sharafu, the UAE opener bought for 110,000 dollars, and Ajay Kumar, the joint-highest wicket-taker last season, give the side a home-grown spine. The Knight Riders open against Dubai Capitals in Dubai on Sunday 22 November, then play six home games at Zayed Cricket Stadium. If Russell finds his range early, the Knight Riders' long wait for a first title starts to look very different.
