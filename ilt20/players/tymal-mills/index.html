England's left-arm quick for the hardest overs

Tymal Mills is the left-arm fast bowler Dubai Capitals turn to for the hardest overs in T20 cricket. He bowls at high pace from the left arm, and his best work comes with the new ball in the powerplay and at the death, where his yorker and his slower ball do the damage. He is a wicket-taker rather than a bowler who simply holds the score down, and his record shows it: 341 wickets in 277 T20 matches at an average of 23.56, with best figures of 4 for 13.

He has played for Dubai Capitals before, so the franchise and the conditions in the UAE are familiar to him. In a Capitals attack that also has Mustafizur Rahman and Luke Wood from the left arm, Mills still stands out for his raw pace and his record in big games. Watch for him in the final overs, when batters have to guess between the full, fast yorker and the slower ball that arrives much later than they expect.

Career so far

Mills was born in Dewsbury, West Yorkshire, on 12 August 1992. He made his first-class debut for Essex in 2011, then moved to Sussex in 2015. That same year he was found to have a congenital back condition, which ended his red-ball career almost before it began and turned him into a T20 specialist. The change suited him: he took 19 wickets at 18.84 in that season's T20 Blast, and his pace earned him an England T20I debut against Sri Lanka at Southampton on 5 July 2016.

Since then he has played 16 T20Is for England, taking 14 wickets, and he played at the 2021 T20 World Cup, where he took 3 for 27 against Bangladesh. In franchise cricket he has played in the IPL for Royal Challengers Bengaluru and Mumbai Indians, in the Big Bash for Brisbane Heat and Hobart Hurricanes, in the PSL for Quetta Gladiators, Karachi Kings and Peshawar Zalmi, and in the SA20 for Pretoria Capitals. He won the first Hundred title with Southern Brave in 2021 and has taken 51 wickets in The Hundred at 17.88. At Sussex he is the club's leading T20 wicket-taker, its T20 captain, and he signed a contract extension until the end of the 2027 season.

The pace attack around him

Mills joins a Dubai Capitals pace group with plenty of variety. Mustafizur Rahman brings left-arm swing and clever changes of pace, Luke Wood adds more left-arm speed, and Mark Adair offers right-arm pace and useful runs down the order. Noor Ahmad leads the spin attack, and the younger quicks in the squad, Nooristani Omarzai and Muhammad Arfan, give captain Sam Billings local and young options to rotate through a long season.

The batting gives the bowlers runs to defend. Phil Salt and Faf du Plessis open, Marcus Stoinis and Rovman Powell hit through the middle, and Dasun Shanaka and Karim Janat add all-round balance. The Capitals open their season against Abu Dhabi Knight Riders in Dubai on Sunday 22 November, then play Desert Vipers in Dubai on Wednesday 25 November. With coach Hemang Badani in charge, Mills has a clear role: bowl fast, take wickets, and close games out.
