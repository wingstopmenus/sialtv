The retained opener

Allen is a right-handed opener who attacks from the first ball, and he was one of only four players the Knight Riders kept before the Season 5 auction — alongside Jason Holder, Matthew Tromp and Sunil Narine. That tells you how the franchise values him.

His most famous innings came in January 2024, when he smashed 137 off 62 balls against Pakistan in Dunedin, the highest T20I score by a New Zealander at the time. He has also played in the Indian Premier League for Royal Challengers Bengaluru and in Major League Cricket for San Francisco Unicorns, where his 2025 season included one of the fastest hundreds in the league's history. In the ILT20, his job is simple: win the powerplay. If Allen gets going, the Knight Riders' total takes care of itself.

Career so far

Allen came through New Zealand's age-group system, playing at the 2018 Under-19 World Cup, and made his international debut in 2021. He has since played more than 50 T20Is for New Zealand, with a strike rate among the best of any opener in the world.

His franchise career has been just as fast-moving: Royal Challengers Bengaluru in the IPL, San Francisco Unicorns in MLC — where he hit 151 off 51 balls in June 2025, one of the great T20 innings — and the Knight Riders in both the Caribbean and Abu Dhabi. He bowls right-arm off-break on occasion, but his value is entirely with the bat: a powerplay batter who can end a game in six overs.

The batting around him

Everything in this batting line-up starts with the opener. Munro is the left-handed partner who can match Allen's powerplay hitting, and Fletcher and Pepper add depth behind them.

That structure only works if Allen fires. When he does, the middle order — Moeen Ali, Gulbadin Naib and Sunil Narine — can play with freedom and the total climbs past 180. When he falls early, the side leans on the middle order to rebuild. Allen's retention before the auction was the franchise's clearest statement: the batting starts with him.
