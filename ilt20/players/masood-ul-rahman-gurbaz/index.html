Season 5

The younger Gurbaz, choosing the UAE

Masood ul Rahman Gurbaz grew up in the shadow of his elder brother Rahmanullah, Afghanistan's wicketkeeper-batter and one of the most feared openers in world cricket. The brothers both live in Ajman, and Masood trained at the Sharjah Cricket Academy on a full scholarship, dreaming of following Rahmanullah into international cricket. In September 2026 he made a different choice: he declared his allegiance to the UAE, where he has lived for more than three years, and signed up for the ILT20 as a local player.

  • Role: Squad member
  • Team: Dubai Capitals
  • Batting: Right-handed
  • Bowling: TBC

His breakthrough came in the ILT20 Development Tournament, where he hit 74 off 44 balls for Abu Dhabi Knight Riders' development side against Gulf Giants. It was the innings that put him on the franchises' lists, and Dubai Capitals picked him up at the auction as a development signing — a wicketkeeper-batter with a famous surname and his own game to prove.

Season 5

What the Capitals bought

At $10,000, Masood is the cheapest signing in the Capitals squad, and the one with the longest runway. He is listed as an Under-19 player in the auction records, and his immediate job is to train with the senior squad, learn from Phil Salt and Tom Moores — two international wicketkeepers — and be ready if the champions need a second keeper or a young batter in the eleven. His qualification to play for the UAE national team is still being worked through, after time spent playing in Afghanistan, but his ILT20 place does not depend on it.

The family subplot writes itself: his brother Rahmanullah plays for Gulf Giants, the Capitals' rivals, after a $310,000 move. If both brothers play in the same match — Dubai Capitals against Gulf Giants on 28 November or 10 December — it will be one of the stories of the season. Masood has said it would be an honour to face his brother in international cricket one day; the ILT20 may give him the chance sooner.

Season 5

Two keepers ahead of him, and a long view

The Capitals already have Phil Salt and Tom Moores as wicketkeepers, so Masood's path into the eleven is not straightforward. But that is the point of a development signing: he is not bought for November, he is bought for the seasons after it. Training daily with Salt and Moores, keeping to Mustafizur and Noor Ahmad in the nets, and playing in the development competition is an education few young keepers get.
