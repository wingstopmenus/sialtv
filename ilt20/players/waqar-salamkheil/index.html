The wrist-spinner nobody can pick

Waqar's craft is the hardest to master in spin bowling: left-arm wrist-spin, the mirror image of the leg-spinner, turning the ball away from the right-hander with a flick of the wrist. Batters face it so rarely that even good players struggle to read it, and his wrong'un — the one that goes the other way — is the delivery that wins him wickets.

  • Role: Bowler
  • Team: Sharjah Warriorz
  • Batting: Right-handed
  • Bowling: Left-arm wrist-spin
  • Born: 2 October 2001, Nangarhar, Afghanistan

For the Warriorz he is the fifth spin option, and the most unusual one. When Rashid, Nabi, Raza and Linde have all bowled, Waqar is the bowler Vince can throw the ball to when a batter is set and comfortable — because comfort is the one thing a wrist-spinner does not allow.

From Nangarhar to the franchise circuit

Waqar comes from Nangarhar in Afghanistan and made his international debut as a teenager. He has played in the BPL for Comilla Victorians, in the CPL for Trinbago Knight Riders, and in the ILT20 before, and his white-ball record for Afghanistan is built on wickets rather than containment.

Left-arm wrist-spinners are so rare that teams build plans around the one they have — and the Warriorz have one of the best young ones in the world. Season 5 is his chance to show a wider audience what Afghan cricket already knows.
