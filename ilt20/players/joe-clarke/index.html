The English keeper-batter

Clarke is a right-handed wicketkeeper-batter who plays for Nottinghamshire, and he has been one of the most consistent white-ball batters in English domestic cricket for nearly a decade. He has scored hundreds in the T20 Blast and the One-Day Cup, and his strike rate in the middle order is among the best in the county game.

His game is built on clean striking and timing: he scores through the off side, hits the ball hard down the ground, and takes the game on from the first ball. The Vipers bought him at the Season 5 auction to add keeping depth behind Andries Gous and to give the middle order another power-hitter. If Gous is rested or injured, Clarke is the man who steps in — and he is good enough to hold a place on batting alone.

Career so far

Clarke came through Worcestershire's academy and made his debut as a teenager, scoring a hundred on his first-class debut. He moved to Nottinghamshire in 2019 and has since become one of the county's most important white-ball players.

He has played in The Hundred and has been part of England's white-ball squads, and his T20 record is strong: he scores quickly, hits sixes, and takes the game on. He has been a regular in the Nottinghamshire side that won the T20 Blast in 2020. The ILT20 is his first major overseas franchise league, and it is a natural step for a 30-year-old who has dominated English domestic cricket.

The keeping group around him

Two keepers share the Vipers' gloves: Gous is the first-choice keeper and a USA international, and Clarke is the English keeper-batter who can step in at any time.

His path to the eleven is through his batting: if the Vipers need a middle-order power-hitter, Clarke is the man. His keeping is good enough to hold the gloves for 20 overs, and his batting is good enough to bat at five. That combination is why the franchise bought him — and why he is never far from a game.
