The retained young all-rounder

Tromp is a right-handed batter and right-arm medium-fast bowler who plays as an all-rounder, and he was one of only four players the Knight Riders kept before the Season 5 auction. At 21, he is the youngest retained player in the squad — and the retention was earned, not given.

His 2026 was a breakthrough year. After a six-month stint in New Zealand club cricket, where he finished as the leading run-scorer in Christchurch premier cricket and was named Batsman of the Year, he returned to Major League Cricket with Los Angeles Knight Riders and scored 58 not out, 49, 50 and 44 in successive innings. He also played for Trinbago Knight Riders in the 2026 CPL. The Knight Riders family has watched him grow — and in Abu Dhabi, he gets his ILT20 chance.

Career so far

Tromp and his twin brother Joshua grew up in South Africa, representing Gauteng at Under-16 level, before the family moved to the United States in 2022. He played for Gujranwala Giants in the 2022 Pakistan Junior League and has since played Major League Cricket for Seattle Orcas and Los Angeles Knight Riders.

His T20 record is still young — 22 matches, 400 runs at an average of 26.66 with two fifties and a best of 58 not out — but the direction is clear. His 2026 MLC season was the breakout: a maiden fifty, four sixes in it, and a run of scores that made him one of the most talked-about young players in American cricket. He bowls right-arm medium-fast and can open the bowling, which makes him a genuine all-round option in a squad that values versatility.

Season 5

The all-round group around him

Tromp is learning from the best all-round group in the league: Holder captains it, Moeen is a World Cup winner, Narine is a great of the format, and Naib is a former international captain.

His path to the eleven is through his batting: if he can repeat his MLC form in the middle order, the Knight Riders have a 21-year-old who can bat at six and bowl four overs. That is the profile every T20 side wants, and the franchise's decision to retain him before the auction says they believe he is ready.
