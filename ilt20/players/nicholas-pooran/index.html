The captain and the finisher

Pooran's T20 game is built on clean, brutal hitting. A left-hander who picks length early and clears the ropes on both sides of the wicket, he has been one of the most expensive and most valuable players in franchise cricket for years — with Lucknow Super Giants in the IPL, and with MI Emirates, the side he led to the ILT20 title in 2024.

  • Role: Wicketkeeper
  • Team: MI Emirates
  • Batting: Left-handed
  • Bowling: Right-arm off break
  • Born: 2 October 1995, Trinidad

As captain he is calm and direct. He sets fields to attack, backs his bowlers to take wickets, and leads by example with the bat: when MI Emirates need 50 off the last four overs, Pooran is the man they want at the crease. His keeping gives the side balance too — he can take the gloves and free up a place for an extra batter.

From Trinidad to the top of franchise cricket

Pooran made his West Indies debut as a teenager and captained the side in white-ball cricket, leading them at the 2022 T20 World Cup. He has played in the IPL since 2019, in the CPL for Trinbago Knight Riders and the Guyana Amazon Warriors, and in the ILT20 since its first season — winning the title with MI Emirates in 2024.

His T20 record is built on volume and violence: thousands of runs at a strike rate that rarely drops below 140, and a habit of producing his best innings when the game is tightest. Last season MI Emirates finished runners-up; this season, with Pooran still captain and the squad strengthened, the target is the title again.

The MI Emirates around him

Pooran is the player this squad is built around: its captain, its wicketkeeper, and one of the most destructive left-handed batters in T20 cricket.

He led MI Emirates to the ILT20 title in 2024, and Season 5 is his chance to do it again, this time after finishing runners-up last season. He sets fields to attack, backs his bowlers to take wickets, and leads by example with the bat, which is why the side looks to him first when a chase gets tight.
