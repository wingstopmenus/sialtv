Two countries, one career

David Wiese's career is unusual: he played for South Africa, then switched to Namibia, the country of his father's birth, and became the side's most important player. He was Namibia's star at the 2021 T20 World Cup, where his all-round performances took the side to the Super 12s for the first time, and he has been their go-to player since — batting in the middle order, bowling at the death, and captaining the side when needed. He has played in the IPL, the Big Bash, the Hundred and the Caribbean Premier League.

  • Role: All-rounder
  • Team: Gulf Giants
  • Batting: Right-handed
  • Bowling: Right-arm medium-fast
  • Born: 18 May 1985

At 41, he is one of the oldest players in the league, and his signing is a bet on experience: the Giants have bought a player who has been in every situation T20 cricket can produce, and who knows how to win the close games that decide short tournaments.

The third all-rounder

Wiese's role is to be the Giants' third all-rounder behind Omarzai and Campher, and the player who gives Jos Buttler a death bowler and a finisher in one selection. He bats at seven or eight, bowls two or three overs at the death, and his slower balls and yorkers have won games in every league he has played in. His experience of bowling the last over — the hardest job in T20 cricket — is the reason the Giants paid $70,000 for him.

He is also a mentor in the dressing room: a player who has played World Cups for two countries and franchise cricket on four continents, and who can guide the younger players through a long month. In a squad full of stars, Wiese is the professional who makes the team work.

Experience in a young squad

Gulf Giants' squad has stars in their prime — Buttler, Klaasen, Miller, Livingstone — and Wiese is the elder statesman among them. His job is not to be the star but to be the player who does the unglamorous work: the death overs, the rebuilding innings, the catch in the deep. Those contributions do not make headlines, but they win tournaments.
