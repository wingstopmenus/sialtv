The Kuwait finisher returns

Tahir is a left-handed middle-order batter who bowls occasional leg-spin, and he is a vital part of Kuwait's top order. He made his T20I debut against Qatar in July 2019 and has since become one of the most aggressive finishers in Associate cricket.

His October 2026 was a breakthrough. In Kuwait's Sub-Regional Asia A Qualifier win for the 2028 T20 World Cup, he scored 16 off seven balls against Bahrain and 32 not out off 16 against Qatar — the kind of finishing that wins tournaments. He said afterwards that winning the title with the Vipers had shown him what a championship environment looks like, and that watching Sam Curran, Fakhar Zaman and Lockie Ferguson train every day had raised his own standards. The Vipers bought him back for $10,000, and he is one of six Kuwait players in the league this season.

Career so far

Tahir has played more than 20 T20Is for Kuwait, scoring 224 runs, and he has been a regular in the side's middle order since his debut in 2019. He plays domestic cricket in Kuwait for Tally Rangers and has been part of the national side's rise up the world rankings.

His ILT20 journey began in Season 4, when the Vipers bought him for $10,000 and he was part of the squad that won the club's first title. He did not play many games — the champions' batting was settled — but he learnt what a title-winning dressing room looks like, and he took that lesson back to Kuwait. His qualifier performances in October 2026, against Bahrain and Qatar, were the proof: 16 off seven and 32 not out off 16, both in winning causes.

The batting around him

Babar leads the batting, Fakhar and Hetmyer supply the power, Tector the class — and Tahir fits into that line-up as the finisher — the left-handed batter who can come in at six or seven and score quickly.

His path to the eleven is through his finishing: if the Vipers need 40 off the last three overs, Tahir is the man who has done it for Kuwait. He is unlikely to play every game — the champions' batting is settled — but T20 tournaments are long, and a finisher who has just won a qualifier for his country is never far from a game.
