The Afghan quick who took six against India

Safi is a right-arm fast bowler who bats right-handed, and his career changed in June 2026. In Afghanistan's one-off Test against India at Mullanpur, he took 6 for 140 — including the wickets of Yashasvi Jaiswal and Sai Sudharsan on the first day, and a maiden five-wicket haul that finished with Mohammed Siraj's wicket.

His story is remarkable. He quit cricket at 16 because of financial difficulties, returned to the game, and was spotted by Pakistan bowling coach Rana Naved-ul-Hasan at Afghanistan's high-performance centre, where he was clocked at 145kph. A groin injury in late 2025 ruled him out of the ODI series against Bangladesh, but he recovered to be named in Afghanistan's Test squad for the India game — and took six wickets. The Vipers bought him at the Season 5 auction, and he is one of three Afghans in the champions' squad.

Career so far

Safi made his first-class debut in February 2019 for Kunduz Province and his T20 debut in September 2020 for Boost Defenders. He has played domestic cricket for Amo Sharks, Band-e-Amir Dragons and Pamir Legends, and he has been part of Afghanistan's squads since 2021.

His international career has been stop-start — injuries and competition for places — but his Test performance against India in June 2026 was a breakthrough: 6 for 140 on a flat Mullanpur wicket, against one of the best batting line-ups in the world. He has also played ODI and T20I cricket for Afghanistan, and his pace — regularly above 140kph — makes him one of the quickest bowlers in the league. At 24, he is the future of Afghan fast bowling — and the Vipers have signed him for the title defence.

The pace attack around him

Safi joins the strongest pace attack in the league: Naseem's speed, Payne's left-arm swing, Tanveer's 17 title-season wickets and Ahmadzai's rise — and Safi's role is to be the Afghan quick who bowls fast and hits the deck hard — the bowler who can take wickets in the middle overs and give the captain a different option. Six of the Vipers' ten league games are in Dubai, where pace has often decided matches, and a 24-year-old who has just taken six wickets against India is not a squad player. He is a match-winner.
