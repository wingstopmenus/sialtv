Kuwait's ILT20 all-rounder

Meet Bhavsar plays for Kuwait, and his ILT20 career is part of the league's role as a finishing school for Associate cricket. Kuwait's players topped their sub-regional qualifier for the 2028 T20 World Cup in Malaysia, and they have credited their time in the ILT20 with sharpening their preparation and tactical awareness — the experience of training and playing alongside world-class cricketers, and of performing in front of crowds, that Associate players rarely get elsewhere. Bhavsar is one of the players who has benefited most.

The right-to-match call

The right-to-match card lets a franchise match the highest bid for a player from its previous squad and keep him. The Giants used one of theirs on Bhavsar at $10,000, a modest fee that reflects his role — squad depth and Associate cover — rather than a starting place. But short tournaments are won by squads: across ten league matches in 24 days, injuries and dips in form are certain, and the players who step in decide whether a side stays in the top four.

His most likely path into the eleven is as a replacement all-rounder if one of the overseas stars is unavailable, or as a tactical pick on a pitch where an extra all-round option is worth more than a specialist. Either way, he trains with the squad from day one, and the Giants' history suggests players who are ready get their chance.

The Associate pathway

The ILT20's Associate player slots are one of the league's most important features: they give cricketers from Kuwait, the UAE and other Associate nations the chance to play alongside the world's best, and the standard of Associate cricket rises every year because of it. Bhavsar is one of six Kuwait players in Season 5, and his continued presence in the Giants squad is evidence that the pathway works.
