The UAE opener the Knight Riders paid for

Sharafu is a right-handed top-order batter and one of the UAE's most-capped young internationals, and the Knight Riders made him one of their headline auction buys at $110,000. That price says plenty about how the league values him: he is a UAE player, which every playing eleven must include, and he is a genuine opener rather than a squad filler.

His job in this side is to give Finn Allen and Colin Munro a platform — or to take the new ball himself if the overseas openers are rested. Sharafu has played ILT20 cricket before, so the step up is not new to him; what is new is the price tag and the expectation that comes with it. The Knight Riders open against Dubai Capitals on 22 November, and a strong start from their UAE opener would settle the whole top order.

Career so far

Sharafu made his UAE debut as a teenager and has been a regular in the national side's white-ball plans since, playing in the Asia Cup and in T20 World Cup qualifiers.

He first came to wider attention in ILT20 colours with Sharjah Warriorz, and his value has risen steadily since: he was a $110,000 buy at the Season 5 auction, and he also featured for Arabian Aces in the Abu Dhabi T10 in November 2026 alongside Moeen Ali and Andre Russell. His game is built on timing and placement rather than brute power, which suits the Abu Dhabi surfaces where the Knight Riders play six of their ten league games. For a UAE batter, the ILT20 is the shop window — and Sharafu has already used it to become one of the most expensive local players in the league.

The batting around him

The Knight Riders' batting is top-heavy with overseas power. Finn Allen and Colin Munro hit the ball as hard as anyone in the league, Kane Williamson holds an innings together, and Andre Fletcher and Michael Pepper add wicketkeeping depth.

Sharafu's place in that line-up is the local anchor: the batter who knows the UAE grounds, who can bat through the powerplay while Allen attacks, and who keeps the innings moving when the big names fall. With Moeen Ali and Sunil Narine to follow, the Knight Riders bat deep — but they need their UAE opener to fire if the top order is to work as planned.
