A keeper who can bat anywhere

Tom Moores has been Nottinghamshire's first-choice wicketkeeper in white-ball cricket for years, and his game is built on versatility: he can open, bat in the middle order, and keep wicket to pace and spin alike. He has played in the Hundred for Trent Rockets, where his keeping to international bowlers and his late hitting down the order have been his trademarks. He is the son of former England coach Peter Moores, and he grew up around the professional game.

  • Role: Wicketkeeper
  • Team: Dubai Capitals
  • Batting: Left-handed
  • Born: 4 March 1996

His signing gives the Capitals two international wicketkeepers in the squad, which matters in a ten-match season: Salt is the first choice, but a keeper's workload in Dubai's heat, across 24 days, is heavy, and Moores is a genuine alternative rather than a passenger. He is also a left-hander, which gives du Plessis another option for the top order.

The second keeper's job

Moores's most likely role is as Salt's deputy, ready to step in if the first-choice keeper needs a rest or picks up an injury, and as a batting option in his own right. His left-handed batting is useful in a mostly right-handed top order, and his experience of keeping to wrist-spin — he has kept to the best in English cricket — means he can take the gloves to Noor Ahmad without a drop in standards.

At $40,000 he is a mid-priced signing for a specialist role, which reflects how the Capitals value depth: a side that loses its keeper mid-tournament and has no replacement loses its shape. Moores makes sure that does not happen.

Two keepers, one plan

The Capitals' keeping group is Salt and Moores, with Masood ul Rahman Gurbaz learning behind them. That is a strong group for a short tournament: two keepers who can both bat in the top six, and a youngster who gets to learn from both. The plan is for Salt to play every game, but the champions have been here before — keepers get injured, form dips, and the side that has a second keeper wins the games the other side loses.
