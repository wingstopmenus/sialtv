The anchor in a power-hitting side

Williamson is a right-handed batter who bowls right-arm off-break, and he is the anchor the Knight Riders' explosive top order needs. Finn Allen and Colin Munro hit the ball as hard as anyone in the league; Williamson is the man who makes sure a fast start does not become a collapse.

His record needs little introduction: more than 100 Tests for New Zealand, captain of the side that won the inaugural World Test Championship in 2021, and the batter who carried New Zealand to the 2019 World Cup final, where he was named player of the tournament. In T20 cricket he is a different player from the power-hitters around him — he scores through timing, placement and reading the game — and that is exactly why the Knight Riders signed him. When the powerplay ends and the spinners come on, Williamson is the man you want at the crease.

Career so far

Williamson made his New Zealand debut in 2010 and has since scored more than 9,000 Test runs, including 33 hundreds, and captained his country in all three formats. He led New Zealand to the World Test Championship title in 2021, beating India in the final at Southampton.

His franchise career has taken him to Sunrisers Hyderabad in the IPL — where he captained the side to the 2018 final and won the Orange Cap — and to the Caribbean Premier League and the ILT20. He has also played in the Big Bash and The Hundred. His T20 game is built on class rather than power: he is one of the few batters in the world who can score at a strike rate above 130 without ever looking like he is trying to hit the ball hard.

The batting around him

The Knight Riders' batting is a study in contrasts. Allen and Munro are powerplay hitters, Williamson is the anchor, Andre Fletcher and Michael Pepper add wicketkeeping depth, and Moeen Ali, Gulbadin Naib and Sunil Narine give the lower order its power.

Williamson's role is to bat through the middle overs, rotate the strike against spin, and be there at the end. In a league where most sides collapse when their openers fall, a number three of Williamson's quality is a luxury — and it is the signing that could turn the Knight Riders from contenders into champions.
