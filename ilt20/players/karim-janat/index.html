Afghanistan's seam-bowling all-rounder

Karim Janat has played for Afghanistan in all three formats, and his best cricket has come in the shortest one, where his hitting down the order and his medium-fast bowling make him a natural T20 player. He has taken five-wicket hauls in T20 internationals and scored fifties from number seven, and his record shows a player who does his best work when the game is in the balance. He has also played in the Bangladesh Premier League and other franchise competitions.

  • Role: All-rounder
  • Team: Dubai Capitals
  • Batting: Right-handed
  • Bowling: Right-arm medium-fast
  • Born: 11 August 1998

His family connection is part of the story — his elder brother Asghar Afghan captained Afghanistan for years — but Janat's career stands on its own. He is one of a generation of Afghan all-rounders who learned the game in the country's domestic system and then took it around the world's T20 leagues, and the Capitals are the latest stop.

The fourth all-rounder

Dubai Capitals already have Stoinis, Shanaka and Hassan Khan as all-rounders, so Janat's job is to be the one who makes the balance work. If the pitch in Dubai offers seam movement, du Plessis can play Janat as a third seamer who bats at seven; if it turns, he can leave him out and still have three all-rounders. That flexibility is the point of the signing: $40,000 for a player who covers two disciplines and lets the captain pick his attack to the conditions.

With the bat, his role is the late surge — the last five overs, when the Capitals need 50 runs and the field is back. He has done that job for Afghanistan, and in this squad he is the insurance behind Powell and Shanaka rather than the first name on the list.

An attack with options in every direction

The Capitals' bowling has pace, spin and now seam all-rounders in the same squad: Mustafizur and Wood with the new ball, Noor and Aayan through the middle, and Janat, Stoinis and Shanaka as the medium-pace cover. That is a lot of options for a captain who likes to match bowlers to batters, and it means the champions rarely have to bowl a bowler they do not trust against a particular batter.
