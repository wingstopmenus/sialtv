Sharjah Warriorz

Raw pace, and plenty of it

Milne's gift is speed. He regularly bowls above 145 km/h, and his bouncer is a weapon that changes how batters play him — they cannot simply stand still and wait, because the short ball is always coming. He takes the new ball for the Warriorz and his job is the first two overs: hit the pitch hard, make the openers hurry, and take a wicket while the ball is still hard.

  • Role: Bowler
  • Team: Sharjah Warriorz
  • Batting: Right-handed
  • Bowling: Right-arm fast
  • Born: 13 April 1992, Palmerston North, New Zealand

He is also a bowler for the big occasion. His best spells for New Zealand have come in World Cups and in series deciders, and the Warriorz need that nerve: their first two games are at home against Gulf Giants and the defending champions, and a fast bowler who starts well sets the tone for the whole season.

A decade with New Zealand

Milne made his New Zealand debut in 2010 as a teenager and has played in the 2015 and 2019 ODI World Cups and in T20 World Cups. Injuries have interrupted him — fast bowlers who bowl at his pace pay a price — but he keeps coming back, and his record in franchise cricket, with Mumbai Indians in the IPL and in the BBL and The Hundred, is the record of a bowler teams trust in big games.

He is also a useful lower-order hitter who has won games with the bat for New Zealand. For the Warriorz, though, the value is the ball: pace is the one thing you cannot coach, and Milne has more of it than almost anyone in the league.

The Sharjah Warriorz around him

Milne is the pace in a spin-built squad: one of the fastest bowlers in world cricket, and the man who makes batters hurry.
