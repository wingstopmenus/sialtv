The 18-year-old left-armer

Pollard is a left-arm fast-medium bowler who bats left-handed, and he was one of only five West Indians picked at the ILT20 Season 5 auction. He made his CPL debut for Barbados Royals in 2026 and impressed enough that the Knight Riders moved for him at the auction — for just $10,000, the lowest price in the squad.

Left-arm pace is gold in T20 cricket: it creates a different angle, it troubles right-handed openers, and it is rare enough that every side wants one. Pollard is 18, raw, and learning — but he has the two things you cannot teach, height and pace. With Dwayne Bravo as the Knight Riders' head coach and Jason Holder as captain, he has two of the best fast-bowling mentors in the Caribbean game in his own dressing room.

Career so far

Pollard came through West Indies Under-19 cricket and made his CPL debut for Barbados Royals in 2026, where his pace and bounce caught the eye. He has also played for West Indies A.

His career is only just beginning — the ILT20 will be his first franchise tournament outside the Caribbean — but the signs are strong. The Knight Riders' $10,000 bid was a bet on potential rather than record, and it is the kind of bet this franchise has made before: young West Indian quicks have a habit of repaying faith quickly. If Pollard gets a game in Abu Dhabi, where the Knight Riders play six of their ten league matches, it will be a debut worth watching.

The pace attack around him

The Knight Riders' pace attack is led by Jason Holder and Gulbadin Naib, with Amshi de Silva and Abdullah Tarakhail as the other young quicks. Pollard is the youngest of the group and the only left-armer among the seamers.

That left-arm angle is his way in. If the Knight Riders want variety against a right-hand-heavy top order, Pollard is the option. He is unlikely to play every game — the side has too much experience ahead of him — but T20 tournaments are long, injuries happen, and an 18-year-old who bowls 140kph from the left is never far from a debut.
