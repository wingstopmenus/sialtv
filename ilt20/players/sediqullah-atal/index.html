Afghanistan's next opener

Sediqullah Atal's breakthrough came in the ACC Emerging Teams Asia Cup in Oman in late 2024, where he was the tournament's standout batter, scoring heavily at the top of the order and hitting the ball cleanly against attacks from across Asia. That tournament turned him from a domestic prospect into an Afghanistan international, and he has since played T20 internationals for his country, opening the batting and showing the same fearless approach that marked his emergence.

His game is built on timing and a high backlift: he hits through the line, favours the leg side, and scores quickly in the powerplay. Left-handed openers who attack are prized in T20 cricket because they change the angles for every bowler, and Atal is Afghanistan's version. The Capitals paid $70,000 for a player they expect to grow into a regular international — and to score runs for them while he does.

Cover, competition, and a left-handed option

Atal's role in the Capitals squad is as the reserve opener and the left-handed option. The starting top order is du Plessis, Salt and Holden, but a ten-match season in 24 days means the openers will need rest or cover, and Atal is the player who steps in without changing the side's approach: he attacks in the powerplay, just like the men ahead of him. His left-handedness is also a tactical tool — du Plessis can use him to break up a right-handed top order and force the opposition to change their bowling plans.

At 24, he is one of the youngest overseas batters in the squad, and his Season 5 is about showing he belongs at this level. A fifty in his first game would make him hard to leave out; a quiet start would leave him waiting for the next chance.

The left-handers' club

The Capitals' batting is mostly right-handed, which makes their left-handers valuable: Atal, Holden and Luke Wood are the only ones in the squad, and two of them bat in the top order. A left-hander at the crease makes the bowlers change their lines and the captain move his fielders, and across a short tournament those small disruptions add up. Atal's job is to be the left-hander who makes the opposition think.
