Zimbabwe's pace leader

Blessing Muzarabani stands close to two metres tall, and his bowling uses every centimetre of it: steep bounce from a good length, seam movement both ways, and a bouncer that hurries even the best batters. He has led Zimbabwe's attack in all three formats, taken five-wicket hauls in Test cricket, and played county cricket in England for Northamptonshire, where his bounce on English pitches made him a regular wicket-taker. He has also played in the IPL and in franchise leagues around the world.

  • Role: Bowler
  • Team: Gulf Giants
  • Batting: Right-handed
  • Bowling: Right-arm fast-medium
  • Born: 2 October 1996

His retention is a statement about what the Giants value: experience, height, and a bowler who has done the job before. In a pace attack rebuilt around the Joseph brothers, Muzarabani is the senior bowler who knows the league, knows the grounds, and knows how to bowl in Dubai.

The bounce bowler

Muzarabani's job is to bowl the overs that nobody else in the attack can: the hard length, the steep bounce, the bouncer that pushes batters back. On Dubai's true pitches, bounce is a weapon — it makes the batters play at balls they would rather leave, and it creates wickets from defensive shots. He will share the new ball with the Joseph brothers and bowl in the middle overs, where his height makes him hard to score against.

He is also a useful lower-order batter, which matters in a side that bats deep. But his primary job is wickets, and his record says he takes them: in every format, in every country, his bounce has been the difference.

Pace, height, and experience

Gulf Giants' pace attack has a clear shape now: the Joseph brothers bring speed, Muzarabani brings bounce, and Tabraiz Shamsi brings spin through the middle. That is a balanced attack on paper, and Muzarabani is the bowler who links the new ball to the middle overs — the one whose spells decide whether the spinners bowl with runs to play with.
