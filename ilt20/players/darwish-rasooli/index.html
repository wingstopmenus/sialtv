Afghanistan's power hitter in reserve

Darwish Rasooli has played for Afghanistan in T20 internationals as a middle-order batter, and his game is built on power rather than patience: he looks to hit the ball hard and straight, and his best innings come when the field is up and the bowlers are under pressure. He has been around the Afghanistan set-up for several seasons, moving between the national side and the country's domestic competitions, and he has played franchise cricket in the region before.

  • Role: Batter
  • Team: Dubai Capitals
  • Batting: Right-handed
  • Bowling: Right-arm off-break

His signing fits the Capitals' pattern for the lower end of their squad: proven international hitters bought cheaply, so that the eleven never runs out of batting. Rasooli is not bought to open or to anchor — he is bought to be the batter who walks in with eight overs left and changes the arithmetic of a chase.

Where he fits in a stacked batting order

The Capitals' top order is full: Faf du Plessis, Phil Salt and Marcus Stoinis take the first three places in most plans, with Dasun Shanaka and Rovman Powell behind them. Rasooli's path into the eleven is as cover for one of those five, or as a tactical pick on a pitch where an extra power hitter is worth more than a sixth bowler. His part-time off-breaks help there — a captain can use him as a sixth bowling option and keep the batting deep.

At $10,000 he is the lowest-priced overseas batter in the Capitals squad, so picking him costs the side almost nothing when it needs a hitter. The Capitals' title defence will not be built on him, but title defences are often saved by the player nobody planned around.

A batting order with no soft middle

What makes the Capitals dangerous is that their batting does not stop at the top three. Shanaka and Powell are finishers in their own right, and Rasooli extends that threat one place further down: even at seven or eight, the side has a batter who has hit international bowlers out of the ground. Opposition captains cannot plan a quiet over against the lower middle order and expect to get away with it.
