Season 5

Ireland's new-ball bowler

Mark Adair has led Ireland's pace attack in all three formats, and his T20 record is that of a bowler who takes wickets rather than containing runs. He has taken five-wicket hauls in T20 internationals and has been Ireland's go-to bowler in World Cups, where his ability to bowl yorkers at the death has kept his side in games against stronger teams. He plays his domestic cricket in England and Ireland, and he has franchise experience in the Caribbean and in England's domestic T20 competition.

  • Role: Bowler
  • Team: Dubai Capitals
  • Batting: Right-handed
  • Bowling: Right-arm fast-medium
  • Born: 27 March 1996

His batting is a real part of the package: Adair has scored first-class hundreds and hits the ball cleanly enough to be used as a lower-order hitter. For a T20 side, a number eight or nine who can score at two runs a ball is a luxury, and the Capitals have two or three of them.

Season 5

The third seamer with a point to prove

Adair's job in the Capitals attack is to be the third seamer behind Mustafizur Rahman and Luke Wood, and the one who bowls the hard overs — the death, when the batters are set and the boundaries are short. His yorker and his slower ball are his weapons, and his experience of bowling at the death for Ireland, often defending small totals, is exactly what the Capitals need: their title last season was won with the bat, and this season the bowlers have to win games on their own.

At $55,000 he is the most expensive of the Capitals' pace signings after the pre-signed Mustafizur, which shows where the champions think their weakness is. If Adair holds his nerve at the death in Dubai, the Capitals defend totals they used to lose.

Season 5

A pace attack with three different jobs

The Capitals' pace group has a clear shape: Wood swings the new ball, Adair bowls the hard middle and death overs, and Mustafizur closes the game. Matthew Potts adds a fourth option, a hit-the-deck bowler who can bowl in any phase. That is a balanced attack on paper, and Adair is the bowler who links the new ball to the death — the one whose overs decide whether the spinners bowl with runs to play with.
