Sharjah Warriorz vs MI Emirates, Friday, 11 December 2026 — Evening session in Sharjah

Sharjah Warriorz host MI Emirates at Sharjah Cricket Stadium on Friday 11 December 2026, in the evening session, in the 26th match of ILT20 Season 5. It is the Warriorz’s last league match of the season — and for MI Emirates, the penultimate one before a final-day trip to Abu Dhabi.

The timing gives this match its edge. Sharjah Warriorz will have played nine league matches before this one, more than any other side at this stage, which means this is their final chance to add two points before the league stage closes. MI Emirates will have played eight, with one more to come — against Dubai Capitals on Sunday afternoon. A win here is worth two points to either side, and for the Warriorz there is no later game to make up for a defeat.

James Vince captains the Warriorz at their home ground in Sharjah, where the side is based. Nicholas Pooran captains MI Emirates, the 2024 champions. Once the match is played, the scorecard will appear on this page.

How both sides arrive in Sharjah

No Season 5 results exist yet, so this section cannot rank either side on form — the season opens on 21 November. Instead, here is what is already known about both teams: their captains, their coaches, and their record in the tournament so far.

Vince keeps the captaincy for Sharjah Warriorz, with Adrian Birrell as coach, and the attack is the side’s calling card: Adil Rashid leads the spin, and Waqar Salamkheil — last season’s leading bowler award winner — is back in the squad. The Warriorz have no ILT20 title yet after four seasons, and this is their last league match in which to earn the points that would keep a first title in reach.

MI Emirates, coached by Mark Boucher, were runners-up in January, when Desert Vipers beat them in the 2025-26 final. Pooran captains a squad built around Jonny Bairstow at the top, Rashid Khan with the ball, and Romario Shepherd and Sherfane Rutherford in the middle order. The 2024 champions have the batting to chase anything Sharjah sets, and the bowling to defend a total of their own.

The run-in and the race for the top four

Four teams qualify for the playoffs, and the seeds are settled by the league table after 30 matches. The top two meet in Qualifier 1 on 16 December, with the winner going straight to the final; the third and fourth seeds meet in the Eliminator on 17 December, where the loser’s season ends. Every win is worth two points, and this is the only match of the final weekend that one side cannot afford to lose twice over — because for the Warriorz, there is no next match.

For Sharjah Warriorz, the maths is blunt. This is their tenth and last league match: win, and they finish on whatever their nine earlier results gave them plus two more points; lose, and they finish on their earlier points alone and must hope the teams around them slip. For MI Emirates, a win here would let them go into Sunday’s match against Dubai Capitals knowing that a second win would complete a perfect final week.

After this match, four league matches remain — Desert Vipers against Abu Dhabi Knight Riders on Saturday, two matches on Sunday, and Abu Dhabi Knight Riders against Dubai Capitals on Monday 14 December. Tuesday 15 December is a rest day, and the playoffs begin on Wednesday 16 December.

The players who can decide it

Each name below links to that player’s profile. The season starts on 21 November, so there are no Season 5 figures to quote — these are the players to watch for the roles they fill and the records they carry into the tournament.

James Vince captains Sharjah Warriorz and bats at the top, so the home side’s innings will follow his start. Beside him, Tom Banton is the batter who can score quickly from the first over, while Sikandar Raza gives the side a middle-order anchor who also bowls. With the ball, Adil Rashid is the spinner opposition teams plan around, and Waqar Salamkheil arrives as last season’s leading bowler award winner.

For MI Emirates, Nicholas Pooran captains and keeps wicket, and his middle-order hitting is the side’s sharpest weapon. Jonny Bairstow opens the batting, Rashid Khan leads the bowling attack, and Fazalhaq Farooqi takes the new ball. Kusal Perera adds experience to the top order.

The match may turn on spin. Rashid and Salamkheil against Pooran and Bairstow is the contest Sharjah’s home crowd will watch most closely — and the one MI Emirates will have planned for longest.
