Sydney Sixers in BBL|16

The Sixers finished second in the league stage last season with 13 points, six wins from ten, and then ran into Perth twice: a 48-run defeat in the Qualifier, and a six-wicket defeat in the final at Optus Stadium, where they were bowled out for 132. Steve Smith top-scored with 24 in the final — a measure of how completely Perth's attack shut them down.

The response is evolution, not overhaul. Steve Smith stays, Sean Abbott and Ben Dwarshuis lead the attack, Josh Philippe keeps wicket, and the new names are New Zealand keeper-batter Tim Seifert, Ollie Davies and young quick Fergus O'Neill. Matthew Mott, the former England white-ball coach, takes over as head coach.

The Sixers play four home matches at the Sydney Cricket Ground and one at Manuka Oval in Canberra, against Adelaide Strikers on 3 January. They finish the league stage away to Hobart Hurricanes on Sunday 17 January — the earliest start of the season, 8:10 AM IST.

What qualification needs

Eight teams play ten league matches each. A win is worth 2 points and a no result is worth 1 point. The top four qualify for the finals, and net run rate decides places when teams are level on points.

Last season is the guide to the cut. Perth Scorchers topped the table with 14 points from seven wins, Sydney Sixers and Hobart Hurricanes took the next two places with 13, and Melbourne Stars took the last finals place with 12 points from six wins. Six wins, in other words, is the realistic minimum — and seven wins all but guarantees a top-two place and a Qualifier, with its second chance at the final.

The finals are: the Qualifier on Tuesday 19 January (1st vs 2nd, winner straight to the final), the Knockout on Wednesday 20 January (3rd vs 4th), the Challenger on Saturday 23 January (Qualifier loser vs Knockout winner) and the final on Tuesday 26 January 2027, Australia Day, with a reserve day available. Finals venues and start times are TBC.

What to watch

Three things decide whether the Sixers go one better.

The Perth problem. The Sixers lost to Perth three times last season, including the two matches that mattered most. Their 10 January home match against the champions is the fixture that will show whether the gap has closed.

Smith's role. Steve Smith is still the best batter in the squad. The Sixers need him to make the big scores the final showed they lacked — 132 all out wins nothing in January.

The Sydney derby. The Sixers meet the Thunder twice, on 19 December and 15 January, both times with a finals place likely on the line. Derby wins are worth the same two points, but they are worth more than that in a tight table.
