Sydney Thunder in BBL|16

The Thunder missed the finals last season, a year after reaching the BBL|14 final — where they lost to Hobart Hurricanes. The new season is a fresh start under a new head coach, former England captain Andrew Flintoff, and the squad has been rebuilt around pace: Trent Boult and Lockie Ferguson, two of the most experienced fast bowlers in world cricket, join Daniel Sams and Tanveer Sangha.

The batting starts with David Warner, who returns after captaining the side last season, and Sam Konstas, the young opener whose rise has been one of Australian cricket's stories. Cameron Bancroft and Nic Maddinson add experience, and England keeper-batter Sam Billings is the other overseas signing.

The Thunder play three home matches at Sydney Showground Stadium and two at Manuka Oval in Canberra, and finish the league stage away to Brisbane Heat at The Gabba on Sunday 17 January, in the last league match of the season.

What qualification needs

Eight teams play ten league matches each. A win is worth 2 points and a no result is worth 1 point. The top four qualify for the finals, and net run rate decides places when teams are level on points.

Last season is the guide to the cut. Perth Scorchers topped the table with 14 points from seven wins, Sydney Sixers and Hobart Hurricanes took the next two places with 13, and Melbourne Stars took the last finals place with 12 points from six wins. Six wins, in other words, is the realistic minimum — and seven wins all but guarantees a top-two place and a Qualifier, with its second chance at the final.

The finals are: the Qualifier on Tuesday 19 January (1st vs 2nd, winner straight to the final), the Knockout on Wednesday 20 January (3rd vs 4th), the Challenger on Saturday 23 January (Qualifier loser vs Knockout winner) and the final on Tuesday 26 January 2027, Australia Day, with a reserve day available. Finals venues and start times are TBC.

What to watch

Three things decide whether the Thunder return to the finals.

Boult and Ferguson. Two world-class fast bowlers in the same attack is a luxury no other BBL side has. If both stay fit, the Thunder can defend anything — and take early wickets against any top order.

Warner and Konstas. The oldest and youngest openers in the league, in the same team. Warner's experience and Konstas's fearlessness could be the most entertaining opening pair of the season — or the most fragile.

Flintoff's first season. Andrew Flintoff has never coached in the BBL. A new coach, a rebuilt attack and a new-look squad make the Thunder the hardest team in the league to predict.
