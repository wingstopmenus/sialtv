Hobart Hurricanes in BBL|16

Hobart's first title came in BBL|14, built on Mitch Owen's 108 in the final, and last season showed it was no one-off: the Hurricanes finished third with 13 points and six wins, and only lost to Sydney Sixers in the Challenger. The core is intact — Tim David, Ben McDermott, Beau Webster and Owen with the bat, Ellis and Riley Meredith with the ball — and the signing of Adam Zampa from Melbourne Renegades is the clearest statement of the window.

Zampa changes the shape of the attack. Hobart already had pace; now they have the best leg-spinner in the country for the middle overs, alongside Bangladesh leg-spinner Rishad Hossain and West Indies keeper-batter Shai Hope. It is a squad built to defend totals at Bellerive Oval, where the Hurricanes are always hard to beat.

Jeff Vaughan coaches the side. Hobart play four home matches at Bellerive Oval and one at Aurora Stadium in Launceston, against Adelaide Strikers on 9 January, and finish the league stage at home against Sydney Sixers on Sunday 17 January.

What qualification needs

Eight teams play ten league matches each. A win is worth 2 points and a no result is worth 1 point. The top four qualify for the finals, and net run rate decides places when teams are level on points.

Last season is the guide to the cut. Perth Scorchers topped the table with 14 points from seven wins, Sydney Sixers and Hobart Hurricanes took the next two places with 13, and Melbourne Stars took the last finals place with 12 points from six wins. Six wins, in other words, is the realistic minimum — and seven wins all but guarantees a top-two place and a Qualifier, with its second chance at the final.

The finals are: the Qualifier on Tuesday 19 January (1st vs 2nd, winner straight to the final), the Knockout on Wednesday 20 January (3rd vs 4th), the Challenger on Saturday 23 January (Qualifier loser vs Knockout winner) and the final on Tuesday 26 January 2027, Australia Day, with a reserve day available. Finals venues and start times are TBC.

What to watch

Three things decide whether Hobart go one better than last season.

Zampa in the middle overs. Hobart's pace attack was already strong. Zampa gives Ellis a wicket-taker for overs 7 to 15, and on a big Bellerive outfield that control is what turns good totals into wins.

Owen's encore. Mitch Owen announced himself with a century in the BBL|14 final. A second season at the top of the order, with bowlers now planning for him, is the real test of a breakout.

The Perth test, early. Hobart's second match is away to the champions at Perth Stadium on 20 December. Win that, and the Hurricanes will believe the title can come back to Hobart.
