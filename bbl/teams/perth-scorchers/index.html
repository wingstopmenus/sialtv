Perth Scorchers in BBL|16

No team in BBL history has defended a title as often as Perth have won one — and BBL|15 was the most complete of their six. The Scorchers topped the league stage with 14 points, seven wins from ten, then beat Sydney Sixers twice in a week: by 48 runs in the Qualifier, and by six wickets in the final at Optus Stadium, in front of 55,018 people. Mitch Marsh made 44, Finn Allen 36, and Josh Inglis hit the winning six; David Payne took 3 for 18 and was player of the final.

The title defence starts in unfamiliar territory: the season opener in Chennai, against Melbourne Renegades, is the first BBL match ever played outside Australia. Two of the final's heroes have moved on — Allen is now with Melbourne Stars — and Inglis had not re-signed when the squad lists were last updated. But the core is intact: Marsh, Cooper Connolly, Aaron Hardie and Ashton Agar with the bat, and Jhye Richardson, Lance Morris and Joel Paris with the ball.

Adam Voges coaches the side, and Ashton Turner captained the BBL|15 title win. The new overseas names are England batter Zak Crawley, wicketkeeper-batter Aneurin Donald and leg-spinner Calvin Harrison. Perth play five home matches at Perth Stadium, and finish the league stage away to Adelaide Strikers on Saturday 16 January.

What qualification needs

Eight teams play ten league matches each. A win is worth 2 points and a no result is worth 1 point. The top four qualify for the finals, and net run rate decides places when teams are level on points.

Last season is the guide to the cut. Perth Scorchers topped the table with 14 points from seven wins, Sydney Sixers and Hobart Hurricanes took the next two places with 13, and Melbourne Stars took the last finals place with 12 points from six wins. Six wins, in other words, is the realistic minimum — and seven wins all but guarantees a top-two place and a Qualifier, with its second chance at the final.

The finals are: the Qualifier on Tuesday 19 January (1st vs 2nd, winner straight to the final), the Knockout on Wednesday 20 January (3rd vs 4th), the Challenger on Saturday 23 January (Qualifier loser vs Knockout winner) and the final on Tuesday 26 January 2027, Australia Day, with a reserve day available. Finals venues and start times are TBC.

What to watch

Three things decide whether Perth win a seventh title.

The Chennai opener. The champions open the season away from home, in a country where the BBL has never played. A win in Chennai would be a statement; the Renegades, who count it as a home game, will see it the same way.

Life after Allen and Inglis. Finn Allen opened in the final and is gone; Josh Inglis hit the winning six and had not re-signed at the last update. Marsh and Connolly must carry the top order until the batting settles.

The Perth Stadium run. Five home matches, and the Scorchers barely lose at home. The fixtures give them three home games in the first five — if they bank those, the top two is theirs to lose.
