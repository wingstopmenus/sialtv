Brisbane Heat in BBL|16

The Heat missed the finals last season, two years after lifting the trophy in 2023-24, and the rebuild is built on bowling. Xavier Bartlett, Spencer Johnson and Michael Neser form one of the sharpest pace attacks in the competition, with Matt Kuhnemann's left-arm spin holding the middle overs. Tom Curran, signed from England, and New Zealand batter Tim Robinson are the new overseas names.

The batting leans on experience. Usman Khawaja, Matthew Renshaw and Nathan McSweeney are all proven at the top of the order, and Max Bryant's power is the counterweight when the innings needs pace. The question is the same one that followed Brisbane last season: whether the batting can give the bowlers enough runs when the Gabba pitch does not help them.

James Hopes coaches the side. The Heat play five home matches at The Gabba, and they finish the league stage there too — against Sydney Thunder on Sunday 17 January, in the last league match of the whole season.

What qualification needs

Eight teams play ten league matches each. A win is worth 2 points and a no result is worth 1 point. The top four qualify for the finals, and net run rate decides places when teams are level on points.

Last season is the guide to the cut. Perth Scorchers topped the table with 14 points from seven wins, Sydney Sixers and Hobart Hurricanes took the next two places with 13, and Melbourne Stars took the last finals place with 12 points from six wins. Six wins, in other words, is the realistic minimum — and seven wins all but guarantees a top-two place and a Qualifier, with its second chance at the final.

The finals are: the Qualifier on Tuesday 19 January (1st vs 2nd, winner straight to the final), the Knockout on Wednesday 20 January (3rd vs 4th), the Challenger on Saturday 23 January (Qualifier loser vs Knockout winner) and the final on Tuesday 26 January 2027, Australia Day, with a reserve day available. Finals venues and start times are TBC.

What to watch

Three things decide whether the Heat get back to the finals.

The pace trio. Bartlett, Johnson and Neser can take wickets in any conditions. If all three stay fit, Brisbane can defend modest totals — and modest totals are the Heat's recurring problem.

Khawaja's form. The batting still runs through Usman Khawaja. When he makes runs at the top, Renshaw and McSweeney can play around him; when he does not, the middle order is exposed early.

The Gabba finish. Brisbane play three of their last five at home, and the season ends at The Gabba against the Thunder. A side chasing a finals place could hardly ask for a kinder run-in.
