BBL Challenger 2026-27, Saturday, 23 January 2027 — Loser of Qualifier vs Winner of Knockout, venue and time TBC

The Challenger of Big Bash League 2026-27 is played on Saturday, 23 January 2027, between the loser of the Qualifier and the winner of the Knockout. The league has yet to confirm the venue and the start time. This is the last match before the final, and the last chance to reach it.

Two teams arrive at the Challenger by opposite roads. One of them finished in the top two, lost the Qualifier on Tuesday, and has had four days to live with it. The other finished third or fourth, won the Knockout on Wednesday, and arrives with the momentum of a sudden-death win. The prize is the same for both: a place in the final on Australia Day, against the winner of the Qualifier, who has been waiting since Tuesday.

How the BBL finals work

Four teams make the finals, and four matches decide who lifts the trophy.

The Challenger is the third of them: the loser of the Qualifier against the winner of the Knockout, and the winner goes to the final on Australia Day, Tuesday 26 January. The loser of the Challenger is out — there is no second chance at this stage of the tournament.

The road to the Challenger runs through the first two finals. The Qualifier, on Tuesday 19 January, is 1st against 2nd, and its winner goes straight to the final. The Knockout, on Wednesday 20 January, is 3rd against 4th, and its loser is out. The Challenger is where those two roads meet: a top-two team fighting to get back to the final, against a team that has already survived sudden death once.

Two points for a league win, one for a no-result, with net run rate breaking ties on points. The top four qualify, and finishing position is everything: first and second earn the Qualifier and a second chance, third and fourth face the Knockout and sudden death. The Challenger is the second chance made real — the reason finishing in the top two matters.

What the winner takes from this match

The stakes of the Challenger are the final itself. Win, and you play for the title on Tuesday, against a team that has had a week to prepare. Lose, and your season ends one match short of the final — the hardest place to finish in any tournament. For the Qualifier loser, the Challenger is the second chance they earned by finishing in the top two. For the Knockout winner, it is the third match in a run that started with sudden death.

For the Qualifier loser, the danger is the four-day wait. A team that loses a Qualifier can carry the defeat into the Challenger, and the Knockout winner will arrive believing they can beat anyone. For the Knockout winner, the danger is the opposite: a team that finished above them in the league, with a week’s rest behind them, waiting at the end of a long road. The winner of the Challenger will have earned their place in the final the hard way — or the hardest way.

Frequently asked questions

When is the BBL Challenger played?

The Challenger is played on Saturday, 23 January 2027, four days after the Qualifier and three days after the Knockout. The venue and start time are still to be confirmed, and this page will be updated when the league confirms them. It is the third of four finals matches, and the winner goes to the final on 26 January.

Who plays in the Challenger?

The loser of the Qualifier and the winner of the Knockout. The Qualifier, on Tuesday 19 January, is 1st against 2nd, and its loser drops into the Challenger. The Knockout, on Wednesday 20 January, is 3rd against 4th, and its winner goes to the Challenger. The Challenger winner meets the Qualifier winner in the final.

What happens to the Challenger loser?

They are out of the tournament, one match short of the final. There is no second chance in the Challenger. The winner goes to the final on Tuesday 26 January, Australia Day, against the winner of the Qualifier, who has been waiting since the Qualifier on 19 January.

Why does the Qualifier loser get a second chance?

Because finishing first or second across ten league matches earns it. A win in the league stage is worth two points and a no-result is worth one, and net run rate decides ties. The top two get the Qualifier and its safety net; third and fourth get the Knockout, where a loss ends the season.
