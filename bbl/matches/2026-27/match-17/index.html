Perth Scorchers vs Sydney Thunder, Wednesday, 30 December 2026 — 1:45 PM IST, Perth Stadium

The 17th match of the BBL is Perth Scorchers against Sydney Thunder on Wednesday, 30 December 2026, at Perth Stadium, starting at 1:45 PM IST. Perth Stadium hosts five matches this season, and this is the Scorchers' second home match of BBL|16.

The first ball goes down at 4:15 PM local time in Perth, an afternoon start under the western sun. Thunder travel across the country for this one, and the Scorchers will be glad to be home: Perth Stadium has been the hardest ground in the league to win at for years.

How both sides arrive in BBL|16

The season opens on Saturday 12 December 2026, so neither side has BBL|16 form yet. Here is where the Scorchers and the Thunder stand before a ball is bowled.

Perth are the defending champions with six titles, from 2013-14, 2014-15, 2016-17, 2021-22, 2022-23 and 2025-26. Ashton Turner captained the BBL|15 title win, and the captaincy for BBL|16 is still to be confirmed. Adam Voges coaches the side, and Zak Crawley is a new overseas name in the squad.

Thunder start again under new head coach Andrew Flintoff, rebuilt around pace with Trent Boult leading the attack. David Warner returns after captaining the side last season, and the captaincy for BBL|16 is still to be confirmed. The club's only title came in 2015-16, and Flintoff has been brought in to build a side that can win another.

Six titles against a fresh start

Two points for a win, one each for a no-result. Every side plays ten league matches, and only the top four progress.

For Perth, a second home win would put the champions in exactly the position they like: top of the pile before the new year, with the rest of the league chasing them. The Scorchers have made a habit of winning at Perth Stadium, and two home wins from two would set the tone for their title defence.

For Thunder, this is a free hit with real upside. Nobody expects a rebuilt side to win in Perth, which is exactly why a win here would change how the league sees Flintoff's team. The finals begin with the Qualifier on 19 January and end with the final on Australia Day, 26 January, and net run rate decides tied places.

The players who can decide it

The four names that matter most, each with a profile to open.

Mitch Marsh is the Scorchers all-rounder to watch at Perth Stadium. He can take a game away in a handful of overs with the bat and offers overs with the ball, and on his home ground he is the player the Thunder attack will plan around.

Cooper Connolly is the Scorchers all-rounder who has become central to Perth's plans. He offers runs and wickets in the same match, and against a Thunder side still finding its shape, his versatility could tilt the game Perth's way.

Trent Boult is the Thunder bowler around whom the new pace attack is built. His swing with the new ball can take early wickets against any top order, and Perth's openers will have to survive his opening spell to build a total.

Sam Konstas is the Thunder batter to watch at the top of the order. He is young, fearless and quick to attack, and a fast start from him would take the pressure off the rest of the Thunder batting in Perth.
