Hobart Hurricanes vs Brisbane Heat, Thursday, 17 December 2026 — Hobart

The 4th match of the Big Bash League 2026-27 season is Hobart Hurricanes against Brisbane Heat on Thursday, 17 December 2026, at Bellerive Oval in Hobart. The first ball is at 1:45 PM IST (1:15 PM PKT, 08:15 UTC; 7:15 PM local time), and Bellerive Oval hosts four matches this season.

Bellerive Oval has been a hard place to visit in recent seasons, and the Hurricanes will want to make it one again. They won the title in 2024-25, and their squad now includes Adam Zampa, the biggest new signing of their off-season. Brisbane arrive two days after their own opener at the Gabba, so this is a quick turnaround — and a first away test for a Heat side whose captain is still to be confirmed.

Champions at home, travellers on the road

Pre-season — the season starts on Saturday 12 December 2026, so there is no BBL|16 form yet. This is what the Hurricanes and the Heat carry into the season.

Hobart are the 2024-25 champions, and they defend that title with Nathan Ellis as captain and Jeff Vaughan as coach. Adam Zampa is the big new signing, joining a squad that already includes Tim David and Mitch Owen. The Hurricanes’ one title is recent, and their challenge now is to show it was the start of a run, not a single season.

Brisbane’s captain for BBL|16 is still to be confirmed, and James Hopes coaches the side. The Heat have two titles, from 2012-13 and 2023-24, and their squad for this season has been announced so far and is still filling. Their second match in three days comes away from home, which is a sharp early test of their depth.

Home pride against an away test

A win is worth two points, and a no-result is worth one. Ten league games each stand between the sides and the finals.

Hobart’s home opener is about defending more than points — champions who lose their first home match spend the next week answering questions, while a win reminds the rest of the league what Bellerive Oval is like. For Brisbane, an away win this early would be a statement of its own, proving a still-filling squad can win on the road. Both sides will know the table is blank; neither wants to be the first to fall behind.

Four players to watch in Hobart

These are the four names that can decide it — each links through to a profile.

Nathan Ellis captains the Hurricanes and leads their attack, and his death bowling is among the best in the competition. At Bellerive Oval he knows the conditions better than anyone, and his yorkers at the end of an innings are the spell Brisbane must survive. As captain, his field settings and bowling changes will shape the match.

Tim David is the Hurricanes batter who can take a match away in a handful of overs. His power hitting at the death turns good totals into winning ones, and on a small ground like Bellerive Oval his range is a serious threat. Brisbane’s bowlers will have a plan for him — the question is whether it holds.

Xavier Bartlett s new-ball spell is where Brisbane’s defence of any total begins. At Bellerive Oval, where the ball can move early, his ability to swing it both ways makes him a threat to Hobart’s top order. If he strikes in the powerplay, the Heat can squeeze a home side that likes to score freely.

Spencer Johnson gives the Heat genuine pace, and that pace is a weapon on any surface. His short balls and yorkers test batters who like to stand still and hit through the line, and against a Hurricanes batting line-up built on power, his overs in the middle of the innings could be the ones that decide it.
