Sydney Thunder vs Sydney Sixers, Saturday, 19 December 2026 — Sydney

The 6th match of the BBL 2026-27 season is Sydney Thunder against Sydney Sixers on Saturday, 19 December 2026, at Sydney Showground Stadium. The first ball is at 1:45 PM IST (1:15 PM PKT, 08:15 UTC; 7:15 PM local time), and Sydney Showground hosts three matches this season.

Derby matches have their own logic. Form matters less, the crowd is louder, and both sides know each other’s players better than any other opponent. The Thunder are three days into life under Andrew Flintoff, while the Sixers arrive four days after their opener at the Gabba as last season’s runners-up. Sydney Showground is the Thunder’s home, and they will want their own crowd to see them beat their city rivals first.

How both Sydney sides arrive in BBL|16

Pre-season — the season starts on Saturday 12 December 2026, so there is no BBL|16 form yet. Here is the Thunder and the Sixers as announced so far.

The Thunder begin again under new head coach Andrew Flintoff, rebuilt around pace with Trent Boult leading the attack. David Warner returns after captaining the side last season, though the BBL|16 captaincy is still to be confirmed. Their one title came in 2015-16, and a derby win at home would be the perfect way to launch the rebuild.

The Sixers were runners-up in BBL|15, beaten by six wickets in the final by Perth, and they arrive with a point to prove. Their captain for BBL|16 is still to be confirmed, and Matthew Mott coaches. With three titles behind them, the Sixers are one of the competition’s most consistent sides, and they rarely lose derby matches quietly.

Two points, and the derby edge

A win banks two points; a no-result banks one. Each side has ten league matches to earn a place in the top four.

Two points are two points, but a derby win is worth more than the table shows. For the Thunder, beating the Sixers at home would tell their supporters the Flintoff era is real, not just a new name on the coaching board. For the Sixers, winning away in a derby would keep their season on track and remind the Thunder who has been the stronger Sydney side in recent seasons.

Four players to watch in the derby

The names below each link to that player’s SialTV profile.

Trent Boult is the centrepiece of the Thunder’s rebuild, the pace spearhead Andrew Flintoff has built the attack around. His swing with the new ball is among the best in world cricket, and against a Sixers top order that includes Steve Smith, his opening spell could decide the match before it settles. In a derby, an early wicket is worth double.

Daniel Sams is the Thunder all-rounder who does the jobs nobody else wants — bowling the hard overs, hitting late runs, and fielding everywhere. His left-arm pace and late-order hitting give the Thunder balance, and in a derby his energy in the field can lift the whole side. He is the kind of player derbies are won by.

Sean Abbott is the Sixers all-rounder who has won them more matches than most supporters can count. His death bowling is calm under pressure, his lower-order hitting has rescued innings before, and his experience of Sydney derbies shows in the moments that matter. The Thunder will need to keep him quiet with bat and ball.

Ben Dwarshuis leads the Sixers’ pace attack with the new ball, and his left-arm angle is a problem for right-handed batters at the top of the order. At Sydney Showground, where the ball can swing early, his opening spell against David Warner is the first contest of the match — and possibly the one that decides it.
