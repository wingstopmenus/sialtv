Perth Scorchers vs Brisbane Heat, Monday, 4 January 2027 — 3:45 PM IST, Perth Stadium

Match 24 of the Big Bash League 2026-27 season is Perth Scorchers against Brisbane Heat on Monday, 4 January 2027, at Perth Stadium. The first ball is at 3:45 PM IST, which is 3:15 PM PKT and 10:15 UTC, and 6:15 PM local time in Perth. It is the fifth BBL|16 match at this ground.

Perth Stadium has been the Scorchers' fortress through their six-title run, and the Heat know the scale of the task. Brisbane's two titles, from 2012-13 and 2023-24, prove they can win the league, but winning in Perth demands something extra: the Scorchers' record at home is the benchmark every visiting side is measured against. An evening start under lights only adds to the atmosphere.

How both sides arrive in BBL|16

The season starts on Saturday 12 December 2026, so there is no BBL|16 form to read yet. That is what the Scorchers and the Heat bring into BBL|16.

The Scorchers are the defending champions with six titles, and Ashton Turner captained the BBL|15 title win, though the BBL|16 captaincy is still to be confirmed. Adam Voges coaches, and Zak Crawley is a new overseas name in the squad. Six titles is a record no other club can match, and the Scorchers defend it at home first. Perth Stadium, under lights, is where the champions' defence should start.

Brisbane Heat go into BBL|16 with their captaincy still to be confirmed, and James Hopes coaching. The squad announced so far is still filling, so the Heat's shape may keep changing before the season starts. Their two titles show the club can build a winning side; the question is whether this group is ready on 4 January. The Heat's first test is the hardest one in the league: the champions, in Perth.

Two points, and a place in the finals race

A win is worth two points, and a no-result is worth one. Ten matches each, four finals places — that is the shape of the race.

For the Scorchers, this is the defence starting at home. Champions who win their home games rarely miss the finals, and a win over the Heat would put the first marker on the board in front of their own crowd. For the Heat, there is no better way to announce a season than beating the champions in Perth — it would change how every other side views them.

The finals are never far away in a ten-match league stage. The Qualifier is on 19 January, the Knockout on 20 January, the Challenger on 23 January and the final on 26 January. Sides that win their home games and steal one or two away from home are the ones that reach those dates, and this match is a home game the Scorchers will expect to win.

The players who can decide it

Each of the four below has a SialTV profile — the names link through.

Mitch Marsh is the first Scorchers name to watch, and at Perth Stadium he bats on a ground he knows better than any visitor. A strong start from Marsh sets the tone for the champions' innings and forces the Heat to chase the game from the outset.

Aaron Hardie is the second Scorchers name to watch, and his all-round contribution is the kind of thing that wins tight games in Perth. If he delivers with bat and ball, the Scorchers' balance looks a level above most sides in the league.

Usman Khawaja is the first Heat name to watch, and the visitors need him to hold the innings together against the champions' attack. If Khawaja bats deep into the innings, the Heat have a total to defend and a real chance of an upset.

Xavier Bartlett is the second Heat name to watch, and his bowling will be tested against a Scorchers batting line-up that knows Perth Stadium well. Early wickets from Bartlett are the Heat's clearest route to quietening the home crowd.
