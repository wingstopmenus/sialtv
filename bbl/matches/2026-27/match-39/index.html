01 — The fixture

Hobart Hurricanes vs Sydney Sixers, Sunday, 17 January 2027 — 8:10 AM IST (7:40 AM PKT, 02:40 UTC, 1:40 PM AEDT local), Bellerive Oval

Match 39 of the Big Bash League season is Hobart Hurricanes against Sydney Sixers on Sunday, 17 January 2027, at Bellerive Oval, Hobart. The first ball is at 8:10 AM IST (7:40 AM PKT, 02:40 UTC, 1:40 PM AEDT local time) — the earliest start of the season for viewers in India and Pakistan — and it is the fourth match of the season at Bellerive Oval.

The penultimate league match, and a meeting of the two most recent champions before Perth’s BBL|15 win: Hobart, champions in 2024-25, against the Sixers, three-time winners and runners-up last season. Bellerive Oval in the afternoon is a ground the Hurricanes know better than anyone, and the Sixers will have to win away from home, in the last weekend of the league stage, if they want to carry momentum into the finals.

02 — Form

How both sides arrive in BBL|16

Pre-season — the season starts on Saturday 12 December 2026, so there is no BBL|16 form yet. What the Hurricanes and the Sixers have to work with is set out below.

Hobart come into this match as the champions of 2024-25, and they have strengthened since: Nathan Ellis captains the side, Adam Zampa is the big new signing, and Jeff Vaughan coaches. The Hurricanes’ home record at Bellerive Oval is one of the best in the league, and a Sunday afternoon there, with the finals a week away, is the kind of fixture champions use to send a message.

The Sixers were runners-up in BBL|15, beaten by six wickets in the final by Perth, and they start this season with that defeat still fresh. Their captain for BBL|16 is still to be confirmed, and Matthew Mott is their coach. Three titles — 2011-12, 2019-20 and 2020-21 — make them one of the league’s great clubs, and the aim is to go one better than last season.

03 — What’s at stake

Two points, and the finals race

Two points go to the winner, one each if the match is a no-result. The top four after ten league matches each go through to the finals.

By Sunday 17 January only one league match will remain after this one, so the table will be nearly final: a Hurricanes win at home could seal a top-two finish, while a Sixers win away would keep their Qualifier hopes alive into the last day.

For Hobart, the stakes are about home advantage in the finals. Win at Bellerive and the Qualifier — and the direct route to the final — stays in their hands. For the Sixers, the stakes are about proving they can win the games that matter away from the SCG. Runners-up last season, they know that finals are won on the road as well as at home.

04 — Players to watch

The players who can decide it

Four to watch: every name links through to the player’s profile.

Mitch Owen is the Hurricanes all-rounder whose rise was one of the stories of their title season. At Bellerive Oval, where he knows every boundary, his hitting at the top of the order can take a match away from the Sixers before their bowlers have found their lengths.

Adam Zampa is the big new signing for Hobart, and as a bowler his leg-spin is the weapon the Hurricanes will use through the middle overs. Against a Sixers batting line-up built on experience, Zampa’s four overs could be the spell that decides the match.

Sean Abbott is the Sixers all-rounder who does the work that rarely makes headlines: wickets at the death, runs down the order, and catches that change innings. In a tight game at Bellerive, Abbott’s all-round contribution is the kind of performance that wins matches without dominating them.

Ben Dwarshuis leads the Sixers’ left-arm pace as a bowler, and Bellerive Oval rewards bowlers who swing the new ball. His opening spell against the Hurricanes’ top order will set the tone: early wickets, and the Sixers control the game; none, and Hobart’s batters take it away.
