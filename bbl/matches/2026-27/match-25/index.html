Hobart Hurricanes vs Sydney Thunder, Tuesday, 5 January 2027 — 1:45 PM IST, Bellerive Oval, Hobart

Match 25 of the BBL 2026-27 season is Hobart Hurricanes against Sydney Thunder on Tuesday, 5 January 2027, at Bellerive Oval in Hobart. The first ball is at 1:45 PM IST, which is 1:15 PM PKT and 08:15 UTC, and 7:15 PM local time in Hobart. It is the fourth BBL|16 match at this ground.

Bellerive Oval has become a genuine home advantage for the Hurricanes, and it showed in their 2024-25 title run. The Thunder, meanwhile, come to Hobart with a new coach and a rebuilt attack, and a ground like this is a fair test of whether the rebuild holds up. The Hurricanes will want a home win; the Thunder will be happy to spoil it.

How both sides arrive in BBL|16

There is no BBL|16 form yet — the season opens on Saturday 12 December 2026. Here is where the Hurricanes and the Thunder stand before a ball is bowled.

Nathan Ellis captains the Hurricanes, with Jeff Vaughan coaching, and Adam Zampa is the big new signing. The Hurricanes are the champions from 2024-25, and that title changed the club: they no longer arrive as outsiders hoping for a good season, but as the side every opponent measures itself against. Bellerive Oval is where the champions' defence should feel most at home.

Sydney Thunder start again under new head coach Andrew Flintoff, rebuilt around pace with Trent Boult, and David Warner returns after captaining the side last season. A fresh start is a chance and a risk at once: the Thunder have a new voice in charge and a clear plan, but plans need results before anyone trusts them. Hobart is a fair first test of whether the Flintoff rebuild holds up on the road.

Two points, and a place in the finals race

Two points for a win, one each for a no-result. Every side plays ten league matches, and only the top four progress.

For the Hurricanes, defending a title means defending home games first. Bellerive Oval should be a place visiting sides fear, and a win here keeps the champions on course for the top four. For the Thunder, a win in Hobart would be the first real evidence that the Flintoff rebuild works — and evidence matters more than promises in January.

January is when the league stage tightens. The Qualifier is on 19 January, and only four sides reach the finals, so dropped home games are expensive. The Hurricanes know this from their title season; the Thunder are learning it under a new coach. Whoever wins this one takes two points and a little belief with them.

The players who can decide it

The four names that matter most, each with a profile to open.

Nathan Ellis captains the Hurricanes, and his leadership is as important as his bowling in a title defence. A captain who delivers with the ball sets the standard for the whole side, and at Bellerive Oval Ellis has the home crowd behind him.

Tim David is the Hurricanes batter to watch, and his hitting can change a game in a handful of overs. If David gets going at Bellerive Oval, the Thunder's rebuilt attack faces exactly the kind of test Flintoff signed up to solve.

David Warner returns to the Thunder after captaining the side last season, and his experience is the counterweight to a new coach and a new plan. A strong innings from Warner would steady the Thunder and give the rebuild its first real result.

Sam Konstas is the second Thunder name to watch, and a big night for him in Hobart would show the Thunder's future is arriving on schedule. Young batters are judged on nights like this, against the champions, away from home.
