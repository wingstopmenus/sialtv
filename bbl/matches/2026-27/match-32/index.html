Sydney Thunder vs Melbourne Stars, Monday, 11 January 2027 — 1:45 PM IST, Manuka Oval, Canberra

Match 32 of the Big Bash League 2026-27 season is Sydney Thunder against Melbourne Stars on Monday, 11 January 2027, at Manuka Oval in Canberra. The first ball is at 1:45 PM IST, which is 1:15 PM PKT and 08:15 UTC, and 7:15 PM local time in Canberra. It is the third BBL|16 match at this ground.

Manuka Oval is a neutral ground in the sense that neither side calls it home, and that makes this a fair test of both. The Thunder's rebuild under Flintoff is built around pace, with Trent Boult leading the attack, and the Stars' hunt for a first title has been the league's longest-running story. A win in Canberra would mean something different to each side, but it would mean plenty to both.

Sydney Thunder start again under new head coach Andrew Flintoff, rebuilt around pace with Trent Boult, and David Warner returns after captaining the side last season. A new coach, a new plan and a new pace attack is a bold way to start a season, and the Thunder will be judged on whether the boldness turns into wins. Canberra is neutral ground, and a win there would be clean evidence the rebuild works.

Marcus Stoinis captains the Stars, with Cameron White coaching. Finn Allen joins Glenn Maxwell and Stoinis, and the side is still chasing a first title after four final defeats. The Stars have the names and the talent; what they need is the season where it all comes together at last. A win on the road in Canberra would show the Stars' title hunt is serious, not just hopeful.

Two points, and a place in the finals race

A win is worth two points, and a no-result is worth one. Ten matches each, four finals places — that is the shape of the race.

For the Thunder, a win in Canberra would be the first real evidence that the Flintoff rebuild works away from their Sydney home. For the Stars, a win on the road would show their title hunt is serious, not just hopeful — the Stars have lost four finals, and seasons are won by sides that win games like this one, in unfamiliar grounds, in January.

The finals begin on 19 January, and the league stage ends on 17 January, so 11 January is late enough that every point counts double. A loss here leaves a side needing favours from other results; a win keeps its fate in its own hands. Net run rate decides tied places, so a big win would be worth more than a narrow one.
