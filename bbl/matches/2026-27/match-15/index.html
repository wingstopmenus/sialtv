Brisbane Heat vs Adelaide Strikers, Monday, 28 December 2026 — 1:45 PM IST, The Gabba, Brisbane

The 15th match of the Big Bash League is Brisbane Heat against Adelaide Strikers on Monday, 28 December 2026, at The Gabba, Brisbane, starting at 1:45 PM IST. The Gabba hosts five matches this season, and this is the Heat's first home match of BBL|16.

The first ball goes down at 6:15 PM local time in Brisbane. The Gabba has been the Heat's fortress since the league began, and a home crowd on a Monday evening will expect the two-time champions to open their account in front of their own fans.

How both sides arrive in BBL|16

There is no BBL|16 form yet — the season opens on Saturday 12 December 2026. What the Heat and the Strikers have to work with is set out below.

The Heat hold two titles, from 2012-13 and 2023-24, and they start at home with James Hopes coaching. The captain for BBL|16 is still to be confirmed, and the squad announced so far is still being filled. Winning the first home game matters for a side that leans on the Gabba crowd as much as any team leans on its ground.

Adelaide's one title came in 2017-18, and this season they have signed Ben Stokes after the BBL scrapped its overseas draft for an open market. Matt Short captains the side and Tim Paine coaches. Stokes is the headline, but Short's leadership and the Strikers' Adelaide Oval form will decide how far they go.

Home comfort against a new overseas star

Two points go to the winner, one each if the match is a no-result. The top four after ten league matches each go through to the finals.

For the Heat, this is about defending home turf. The Gabba hosts five matches this season, and a side that wins its home games rarely misses the finals. Two points here would also put the Heat ahead of a direct rival for a top-four place before the league stage has properly settled.

For the Strikers, a win in Brisbane would be a statement. Beating the 2023-24 champions away from home, with Stokes in the side, would tell the rest of the league that Adelaide are not here to make up the numbers. The Qualifier is on 19 January and the final on Australia Day, 26 January, with net run rate deciding tied places.

The players who can decide it

Four to watch: every name links through to the player’s profile.

Usman Khawaja is the Heat batter to watch at the Gabba. He sets the tone at the top of the order, and on his home ground he knows the pace and bounce better than any visiting bowler.

Spencer Johnson is the Heat bowler who brings raw pace to the Gabba. Against a Strikers top order led by Matt Short, his spell with the new ball could decide whether Adelaide settle or spend the night rebuilding.

Ben Stokes is the Strikers all-rounder and the biggest signing of the BBL|16 open market. He signed after the league scrapped its overseas draft, and his first match in Adelaide colours will be watched across the competition.

Matt Short is the Strikers all-rounder who captains the side and opens the batting. He holds the team together at the top of the order, and a fast start from him would take the pressure off Stokes and the middle order.
