BBL|16 · Melbourne Stars

Tom Rogers — Stars batter, BBL 2026-27

This is the Melbourne Stars' Tom Rogers — Thomas Fraser Rogers, a left-handed top-order batter born in Greensborough, Victoria, in 1999, who has played for the Stars since the 2021-22 season. He is not the Melbourne Renegades' Tom Rogers: that is a different player, a fast bowler born in 1994. This Rogers bats at the top of the order — and the Stars' squad table lists him as a bowler, but his record is that of a batter.

Role: Bowler · Batting: Left-hand bat · Bowling: Right-arm medium · Born: 7 February 1999, Greensborough, Victoria · Team: Melbourne Stars

The opener who came in from the cold

Rogers' story is one of the strangest in the BBL. He made his debut for the Stars on 2 January 2022 as one of six replacement players called in when ten members of the original squad were unavailable because of COVID-19 — and he was ruled out of the next game, against the Renegades, a day later after testing positive himself.

He stayed. He became a regular for the Stars in BBL|12, earned a full contract, and has been with the club ever since. He is a left-handed top-order batter who bowls right-arm medium pace — the opposite of the Renegades' Tom Rogers, who is a right-arm fast bowler. His best List A score is 196, and he has played for Victoria since 2022-23 and for Galle Gallants in 2026. The Stars list him as a bowler in their announced squad; his batting is what keeps him in the side.

Squad note: the Stars have announced 14 players so far, as of 31 October 2026. The full squad has not been announced — clubs can carry up to 18 players and are still adding names — so this page will be updated as the club confirms more signings.

A COVID debut, then a regular place

Rogers' debut was a product of chaos: ten Stars players unavailable, six replacements called in, and a 22-year-old Victorian opening the batting in a BBL game he had no reason to expect to play. He made the most of it, and the club signed him long-term after his breakout season in BBL|12.

His BBL career has been a slow burn rather than a explosion. He has played 39 innings for the Stars, with two fifties — the second of them a joint-second-fastest half-century in Stars history, made against the Renegades in BBL|15 alongside Sam Harper, when the pair put on a partnership at 11.2 runs per over and Rogers hit a 99-metre six. It was the innings that showed what he can do when he gets going, and the Stars hope it is the start of a late-season resurgence rather than a one-off.

Where he fits in the Stars

The Stars' top order in BBL|16 is built around Finn Allen, and Rogers is the left-handed batter who can open with him or bat at three. His left-handedness matters in a mostly right-handed top order, and his medium pace gives the captain a part-time bowling option if a change is needed.

He is also the player the other Tom Rogers will be measured against, and the two will meet in the Melbourne derby on Saturday 16 January at the MCG — the Stars' last league game, away to the Renegades. One is a left-handed batter born in 1999; the other is a fast bowler born in 1994. They share a name, a city and a league, and nothing else.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Stars finished fourth last season, their best return in years, and the table is where their title chase will be measured.
