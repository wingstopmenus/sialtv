Ashton Agar — Scorchers all-rounder, BBL 2026-27

Ashton Agar is an Australian left-arm spinner who made one of the most famous Test debuts in history — 98 runs as a No. 11 on debut against England at Trent Bridge in the 2013 Ashes — and he has been a Perth Scorchers and Australia white-ball regular ever since. He is contracted through BBL|17, and he is one of the senior players the title defence relies on.

Role: All-rounder · Batting: Left-hand bat · Bowling: Slow left-arm orthodox · Team: Perth Scorchers

The spinner who ties the middle overs down

Agar's job is to bowl four overs of left-arm orthodox spin in the middle of the innings and keep the scoring rate down. He is a left-handed batter who can also hit the ball a long way down the order, and his all-round game gives the Scorchers a player who can do two jobs in one spot.

He has been a Scorchers player for his whole BBL career, and he has been part of the club's most successful period — six titles, more than any other team. His left-arm spin is the perfect complement to the Scorchers' pace attack: Richardson, Morris and Paris bowl fast, and Agar bowls slow, which makes it hard for batters to settle. He is also one of the best fielders in the side, and his experience of playing in big finals — including the BBL|15 title win — is part of what the club relies on.

Ninety-eight on Test debut

Agar made his Test debut for Australia against England at Trent Bridge in the 2013 Ashes, batting at No. 11, and scored 98 — the highest score by a No. 11 on Test debut, and a world record for that position at the time. He put on 163 with Phillip Hughes for the last wicket, and the innings made him famous overnight.

He has played white-ball cricket for Australia for more than a decade since, and he has been a regular in the T20 and one-day sides. His BBL career has been with the Scorchers throughout, and he has taken wickets in every season he has played. He is a Victorian by birth who moved to Western Australia as a youngster, and he has become one of the faces of the Scorchers' success.

Where he fits in the Scorchers

The Scorchers' spin group in BBL|16 is built around Agar and Cooper Connolly's left-arm orthodox, with Calvin Harrison's leg-spin and Will Malajczuk's off-spin as the alternatives. Agar is the senior spinner — the one the captain turns to when a wicket is needed, and the one who bowls the hard overs when the batters are set.

His batting matters too. The Scorchers' lower order is strong — Hardie, Turner, Agar — and his left-handed hitting down the order gives the side a finisher who can clear the rope. The title defence starts in Chennai, on a surface where spin has historically done well, and Agar's four overs could be the difference between winning and losing the opener.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Scorchers topped the table last season with 14 points, and the defence of that position starts in Chennai.
