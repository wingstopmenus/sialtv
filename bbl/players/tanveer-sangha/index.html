Tanveer Sangha — Thunder bowler, BBL|16

Tanveer Sangha is the leg-spinner who gives the Sydney Thunder attack its mystery — a right-arm leg-break bowler with a sharp googly, a New South Wales player with Australian T20 caps, and a bowler contracted until BBL|17. He is back for BBL|16 in an attack rebuilt around pace.

The leg-spinner in a pace attack

Sangha's job is the one that makes the Thunder's pace work. When Boult, Ferguson and Sams have done their damage with the new ball, Sangha comes on through the middle overs — leg-spin, googly, and a quicker ball — and takes the wickets that turn a good start into a win.

He is a right-arm leg-break bowler who turns it both ways, and his googly is his main weapon: batters read it late, play for the leg-break, and find the ball going the other way. In a format where wrist-spin is the most reliable way to take middle-overs wickets, Sangha is the Thunder's version.

He is also a capable lower-order batter, right-handed, who has added useful runs for the Thunder. But the Thunder did not contract him until BBL|17 for his batting; they did it for his four overs.

New South Wales, Australia, and the Thunder

Sangha plays domestic cricket for New South Wales and has represented Australia in T20 internationals. His rise was fast: a young leg-spinner who caught the eye in the Big Bash, earned state selection, and then national selection — all on the back of a googly that batters could not pick.

His Thunder career has been defined by consistency. He has been the side's front-line spinner for years, and he was part of the team that reached the BBL|14 final — losing to Hobart Hurricanes — and then missed the finals last season. The new season, under Andrew Flintoff, is a fresh start, and Sangha is one of the players the new coach will lean on.

For BBL|16, he shares the spin duties with Chris Green's off-spin, giving the Thunder two spinners turning it two different ways — a combination that can bowl to any batter, on any ground.

Pace around him, spin from him

The Thunder's rebuild has been about pace — Boult and Ferguson arriving, Sams already there. But pace alone does not win T20 games; it needs spin to control the middle overs, and Sangha is the spinner who does that job alongside Green.

The two spinners complement each other: Sangha turns it away from right-handers with his leg-break, Green turns it in with his off-break. Between them they give the Thunder a middle-overs attack that can attack any batting line-up.

The Thunder missed the finals last season. Sangha's job is to help make sure that does not happen again — not with a spectacular season, but with the same steady wicket-taking he has provided for years.
