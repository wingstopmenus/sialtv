Calvin Harrison — Scorchers bowler, BBL 2026-27

Calvin Harrison is a South African-born English leg-spinner who arrives at the Perth Scorchers after the best season of his career — 22 wickets at 15.27 in Northamptonshire's title-winning T20 Blast campaign, including a career-best 5 for 9 against Essex, and 10 wickets at 19.70 in the Hundred for Trent Rockets. He is 28, and BBL|16 is his first overseas franchise gig.

Role: Bowler · Batting: Right-hand bat · Bowling: Right-arm leg-break · Team: Perth Scorchers

The leg-spinner who won the Blast

Harrison's job is to give the Scorchers a leg-spin option. The club's spin group is built around the left-arm orthodox of Ashton Agar and Cooper Connolly, and Harrison's right-arm leg-break is the different angle — the ball that turns the other way, and the bowler who can take wickets when batters try to hit him out of the attack.

He was the Scorchers' first signing after the contracting embargo lifted, and the club's head coach Adam Voges knows him well: Voges was an assistant coach at Trent Rockets, where Harrison took 10 wickets at 19.70 and 2 for 30 in the final against Manchester Super Giants. His Blast figures — 22 wickets at 15.27, with a career-best 5 for 9 against Essex — made him one of the best spinners in English domestic cricket in 2026, and he was selected for England Lions in limited-overs matches against Pakistan Shaheens in the UAE.

From South Africa to Northamptonshire

Harrison was born in South Africa and moved to England as a youngster, coming through the county system as a tall leg-spinner who could also bat — he has made three first-class centuries from 41 matches. He became a key figure for Northamptonshire across formats, and his 2026 season was the breakthrough.

He is no stranger to Australian conditions: he spent four months in Australia during the 2024-25 season, playing for Camberwell Magpies in the Victorian Premier Cricket competition, which gave him experience of Australian pitches before this opportunity came. His form for Northamptonshire and Trent Rockets continued his strong run in 2026, and he was in the England Lions side that sealed a T20 clean sweep over West Indies A in November 2026, taking 2 for 16 and 2 for 27 in the first two matches.

Where he fits in the Scorchers

The Scorchers' spin group in BBL|16 is the most varied in the league: Agar and Connolly's left-arm orthodox, Harrison's leg-spin, and Will Malajczuk's off-spin. Harrison is the wrist-spinner — the one who can take wickets in the middle overs and change a game in four balls.

His batting matters too. Three first-class centuries say he is not a bowler who bats a bit; he is a genuine lower-order batter who can score runs when the side needs them. At Perth Stadium, where the Scorchers play five home games, his leg-spin will be hard to play on the bouncy pitch, and in Chennai, where the season starts, his variations could be the difference. The Scorchers have signed him for BBL|16, and his first overseas franchise season is a chance to show he belongs at this level.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Scorchers topped the table last season with 14 points, and the defence of that position starts in Chennai.
