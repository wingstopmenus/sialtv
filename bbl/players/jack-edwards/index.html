Jack Edwards — Sixers all-rounder, BBL|16

Jack Edwards is the New South Wales all-rounder who does a bit of everything for the Sydney Sixers — right-arm medium-fast with the ball, right-handed batting in the middle order, and athletic fielding in between. He is contracted for BBL|16 in a side that keeps betting on home-grown talent.

The all-rounder who fills the gaps

Every T20 side needs a player who can do the job nobody else wants, and Edwards is the Sixers' version. He can bowl in the powerplay or the middle, bat anywhere from five to eight, and field where the ball goes. That flexibility is what keeps him in the eleven when specialists rotate.

His bowling is honest right-arm medium-fast: good length, good shape, and a bouncer he is not afraid to use. He will not bowl the fastest ball of the night, but he will bowl the over the captain needs — the one that goes for six runs when the game is tight.

With the bat he is a clean striker who can rebuild an innings or finish one. The Sixers' batting is top-heavy with Smith, Philippe and Silk; Edwards is the insurance below them, the player who turns a collapse into a defendable total.

A New South Wales cricketer through and through

Edwards came through the New South Wales system and has been a regular in their white-ball sides. The Sixers have built their squad around that pipeline — Edwards, Joel Davies, Hayden Kerr, Lachy Shaw and Sean Abbott are all Blues — and the understanding shows in the field, where the side moves like a team that has played together for years.

He was part of the Sixers side that reached last season's final, and he knows what the dressing room is chasing: the club's fourth BBL title, after 2011-12, 2019-20 and 2020-21. The last two titles were won with a core of New South Wales players; the current side is built the same way.

For BBL|16, Edwards is one of the players the Sixers need to step up. The stars will do their part; the title will be decided by whether the all-rounders around them win their match-ups.

Where he fits in the Sixers' balance

The Sixers' XI has a clear shape: Smith and Philippe at the top, Silk and the Davies brothers in the middle, Abbott and Dwarshuis with the new ball, and a spin trio behind them. Edwards is the seam-bowling all-rounder who ties it together — the sixth bowling option and the seventh batting option in one player.

That matters because BBL squads are only 18 deep and injuries happen. A player who can cover two roles is worth more than a player who can cover one, and Edwards covers three if you count the fielding. When the Sixers name their XI for the Gabba opener, his name is one of the safer ones on the sheet.

The Sydney Smash on 19 December is a good early stage for him: a derby, under lights, against a Thunder side stacked with fast bowlers.
