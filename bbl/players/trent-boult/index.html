Trent Boult — Thunder bowler, BBL|16

Trent Boult is the headline signing of the Sydney Thunder's off-season — a New Zealand left-arm fast bowler, one of the finest white-ball bowlers of his generation, signed for BBL|16 as the Thunder rebuild their attack around pace under new head coach Andrew Flintoff.

The signing that changed the Thunder

The Thunder's off-season had one clear theme: pace. They signed Boult from New Zealand and Lockie Ferguson alongside him, giving themselves two of the most experienced fast bowlers in world cricket in the same attack. Boult is the bigger name of the two — a bowler who has swung the new ball in World Cup finals and taken wickets in every T20 league that matters.

He is a left-arm fast bowler, and left-armers who swing the new ball are the most valuable commodity in T20 cricket. Bowling from over the wicket to right-handers, Boult takes the ball away from them and into the edge; bowling to left-handers, he brings it back in. Batters have spent a decade trying to solve him, and most of them have failed.

For the Thunder, who missed the finals last season, Boult is not a luxury — he is the statement that the club intends to be back in the finals. You do not sign Trent Boult to finish fifth.

The new ball, and what comes after

Boult's reputation was built on the new ball, but his T20 value is broader than that. He bowls at the death too, where his yorker and his slower ball have closed out games for New Zealand and in franchise leagues around the world. He is a complete white-ball bowler, not a one-phase specialist.

He will share the new ball with Ferguson, the other New Zealand quick, and with Daniel Sams, the Thunder's left-arm all-rounder. Behind them, Tanveer Sangha's leg-spin and Chris Green's off-spin give the attack its middle-overs shape. On paper it is one of the strongest attacks in BBL|16 — and Boult is the reason why.

He is also a useful lower-order batter and a sharp fielder, but the Thunder did not sign him for either. They signed him for four overs a night, and for the wickets those overs bring.

A New Zealand great, still at it

Boult's career needs little introduction. A mainstay of New Zealand's attack across formats for more than a decade, a World Cup finalist, and a bowler whose left-arm swing has troubled the best batters in the world, he has played franchise cricket in the IPL, the Hundred and leagues around the world.

His move to the Thunder is part of a late-career chapter built around franchise cricket, and the Big Bash is the one major T20 league he had not conquered. The Thunder are betting that a bowler of his class does not need a settling-in period — that he arrives, takes the new ball, and takes wickets from the first game.

His first game in Thunder colours comes against Adelaide Strikers at Manuka Oval on 16 December, in Canberra.
