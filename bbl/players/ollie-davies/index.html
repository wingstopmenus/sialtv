BBL|16 · Sydney Sixers

Ollie Davies — Sixers batter, BBL|16

Ollie Davies is the Sixers' most pointed signing of the off-season: a hard-hitting New South Wales batter who has crossed Sydney, leaving the Thunder to join the Sydney Sixers for BBL|16. He arrives contracted until BBL|18, and he joins his younger brother Joel in the same squad for the first time.

The cross-town move

Davies was one of only two free agents snapped up by the Sixers in the post-season movement window — the other was Fergus O'Neill — and the move says as much about the Sixers as it does about him. Last season's finalists lost the decider because their batting was shut down for 132; they have answered by adding a batter who hits the ball as hard as anyone in New South Wales cricket.

He is a right-handed top-order batter who scores in boundaries. That is the simple version, and it is the version the Sixers bought: a player who can take a powerplay apart and turn a middling total into a match-winning one. His Thunder years showed the ability; the Sixers are betting the change of colours brings the consistency.

The family angle is the bonus. Younger brother Joel bowls left-arm spin for the Sixers, so the Davies brothers — one batting, one bowling — are teammates in the Big Bash for the first time.

What he brings to the top order

The Sixers' top order already has Steve Smith, Josh Philippe and Jordan Silk. Davies does not replace any of them; he gives the side something it lacked in the final — a batter who can dominate from ball one and keep the scoreboard moving when the big names fall early.

His game is built on power through the off side and a willingness to take the aerial route. On the SCG's short boundaries that is a dangerous combination, and five of the Sixers' ten league games are at home. If he finds form early, the Sixers' batting looks deeper than it did in January, when Perth's attack bowled them out cheaply in the final.

The question is the same one that follows every big hitter: can he do it often enough? The Sixers have given him a contract until BBL|18, which is their answer — they are buying the next three years, not just the next ten games.

The first Sydney Smash, against his old club

Davies does not have to wait long for the awkward reunion. The Sixers' second game of the season is the Sydney Smash against the Thunder on 19 December at the Showground — his old home ground, against his old teammates, in his new colours.

It is the kind of fixture that writes its own story. The Thunder rebuilt their attack around pace this winter, with Trent Boult and Lockie Ferguson joining, so Davies will face two of the world's best fast bowlers in his second game as a Sixer. A score there would say plenty about the move.

Before that comes the opener at The Gabba against Brisbane Heat on 15 December — a first chance to show the Sixers what their new batter can do.
