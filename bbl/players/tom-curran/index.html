Tom Curran — Brisbane Heat all-rounder, BBL 2026-27

Tom Curran is the Brisbane Heat’s new overseas all-rounder: an England international and 2022 T20 World Cup winner who has moved north after two seasons with the Melbourne Stars. He will miss the first three matches of the season due to family commitments, with New Zealand’s Bevon Jacobs covering as the international replacement player.

The World Cup winner who moved north

Curran is a right-handed batter and right-arm fast-medium bowler, and his game is built on doing the hard jobs well. He bowls at the death, bats in the lower middle order, and fields with the energy of a player who has spent a decade in franchise cricket around the world.

His move from the Stars to the Heat is one of the off-season’s bigger switches. Curran took 16 wickets in ten games for the Stars in BBL|15, and Brisbane have signed him to strengthen a pace attack that already includes Xavier Bartlett, Spencer Johnson and Michael Neser. On paper, it is one of the sharpest attacks in the competition.

A T20 World Cup winner with miles on the clock

Curran has played white-ball cricket for England for years, winning the T20 World Cup in Australia in 2022, and he has played franchise T20 in England, Australia, India and Pakistan. He has also played Test cricket for England, and his career has been built on versatility — new ball, death overs, lower-order runs.

He knows the BBL well. Two seasons with the Stars gave him a full education in Australian conditions, and he has played against most of the batters he will now bowl to. That experience is what Brisbane are buying: a player who does not need time to settle.

What Curran brings to BBL|16

Curran is one of the announced contracted players for BBL|16 — the full squad has not been announced yet — and his role, once he arrives, is the death bowler and lower-order finisher. The first three matches will be covered by Bevon Jacobs, so Curran’s season starts when he lands — and the Heat will want him straight into the side.

Watch his death bowling when he arrives. Curran’s slower balls and yorkers at the end of the innings are his calling card, and Brisbane’s finals hopes may depend on him holding his nerve there. There are no BBL|16 numbers for Curran yet; the season starts on 12 December, and his first game for the Heat comes after the opening three matches.

What’s next

BBL|16 runs from 12 December 2026 to 26 January 2027. The opener is Match 1, Melbourne Renegades vs Perth Scorchers, on Saturday 12 December at MA Chidambaram Stadium in Chennai — the first BBL match played outside Australia — starting at 2:40 PM IST (2:10 PM PKT). Tom’s team, the Brisbane Heat, open their campaign in Match 2. The full list of every BBL fixture is on the BBL Schedule.

  • Match 2 — Brisbane Heat vs Sydney Sixers: Tuesday 15 December 2026, 1:45 PM IST (1:15 PM PKT), The Gabba, Brisbane (Home).
  • Finals: The finals are the Qualifier on 19 January, the Knockout on 20 January, the Challenger on 23 January and the final on 26 January, Australia Day. Finals venues and times are still to be confirmed.
  • Points: Eight teams play ten league matches each. A win is worth 2 points, a no result is worth 1, and the top four qualify for the finals, with net run rate deciding places when teams are level on points.
