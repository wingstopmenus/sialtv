Shai Hope — Hobart Hurricanes wicketkeeper, BBL 2026-27

Shai Hope is the West Indies wicketkeeper-batter the Hobart Hurricanes have brought back for BBL|16: the white-ball skipper of the West Indies, who played five games for the club during their BBL|14 championship campaign and now returns to fill the top-order and wicketkeeping void left by Matthew Wade’s retirement.

The West Indies skipper returns

Hope is a right-handed batter who keeps wicket, and his game is built on class. He plays the ball late, finds gaps, and scores hundreds in one-day cricket for fun — he has more ODI centuries than almost any current West Indies player. In T20 cricket, that class translates into calm at the top of the order.

His return is the Hurricanes’ answer to Wade’s retirement. Wade was the club’s keeper, opener and heartbeat for years; Hope cannot replace all of that, but he can keep wicket, bat at the top, and bring the authority of a man who captains his country in white-ball cricket.

A West Indies great in the making

Hope has been one of West Indies’ best batters for years, scoring centuries in Test and one-day cricket and captaining the white-ball sides. He has played franchise cricket around the world, and his BBL|14 stint with Hobart — five games in a title-winning campaign — showed he could handle Australian conditions.

The Hurricanes secured his return as part of their title defence, and he arrives with a clear role: bat at the top, keep wicket, and lead by example. It is a big job, but Hope has done big jobs before.

What Hope brings to BBL|16

Hope is one of the announced contracted players for BBL|16 — the full squad has not been announced yet — and his role is the wicketkeeper-batter at the top of the order. With Tim David, Ben McDermott and Mitch Owen behind him, Hope is the player who gives the innings its shape.

Watch his batting in the powerplay. If Hope gets through the first six overs, the Hurricanes’ power hitters arrive with a platform; if he falls early, the middle order has to rebuild. There are no BBL|16 numbers for Hope yet; the season starts on 12 December, and his first innings of the campaign comes against Brisbane Heat on 17 December.

What’s next

BBL|16 runs from 12 December 2026 to 26 January 2027. The opener is Match 1, Melbourne Renegades vs Perth Scorchers, on Saturday 12 December at MA Chidambaram Stadium in Chennai — the first BBL match played outside Australia — starting at 2:40 PM IST (2:10 PM PKT). Shai’s team, the Hobart Hurricanes, open their campaign in Match 4. The full list of every BBL fixture is on the BBL Schedule.

  • Match 4 — Hobart Hurricanes vs Brisbane Heat: Thursday 17 December 2026, 1:45 PM IST (1:15 PM PKT), Bellerive Oval, Hobart (Home).
  • Finals: The finals are the Qualifier on 19 January, the Knockout on 20 January, the Challenger on 23 January and the final on 26 January, Australia Day. Finals venues and times are still to be confirmed.
  • Points: Eight teams play ten league matches each. A win is worth 2 points, a no result is worth 1, and the top four qualify for the finals, with net run rate deciding places when teams are level on points.
