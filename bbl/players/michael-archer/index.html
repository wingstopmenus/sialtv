BBL|16 · Melbourne Renegades

Michael Archer — Renegades leg-spinner, BBL 2026-27

Michael Archer is a 29-year-old leg-spinner from Tatura in regional Victoria who has taken the long road to professional cricket — through Carlton Cricket Club, the Renegades Academy, and a debut as a replacement player in BBL|15. In August 2026 he earned his first full BBL contract, and BBL|16 is his first full season as a Renegade.

Role: To be confirmed · Batting: Right-hand bat · Bowling: Right-arm leg-spin · Born: 28 February 1997, Tatura, Victoria · Team: Melbourne Renegades

The leg-spinner who earned his contract

The Renegades' squad table lists Archer without a formal role — the club has not yet put a label on him. His cricket says it plainly enough: he is a right-arm leg-spin bowler, and he is the wrist-spin option in a spin group that also includes Callum Stow's left-arm wrist-spin and the off-spin of Ben Manenti and Michael Bracewell.

His chance comes because the Renegades' spin places are open. Adam Zampa left for Hobart in the off-season, and the club's spin group is younger than it has been in years. Archer's leg-spin gives Luke Ronchi a bowler who can take wickets in the middle overs — the hardest thing to find in T20 cricket. He made his BBL debut in the club's final match of BBL|15 against Adelaide Strikers, bowling two overs for 15 runs after being called in as a replacement for Zampa, and he did enough in the Academy programme to earn a full deal.

Squad note: the Renegades have announced 15 players so far, as of 31 October 2026. The full squad has not been announced — clubs can carry up to 18 players and are still adding names — so this page will be updated as the club confirms more signings.

From Tatura to a first full contract

Archer grew up in Tatura in Victoria's north, played senior cricket for the town in the Cricket Shepparton competition — where he only turned to spin as a senior — and then worked his way through Victorian Premier Cricket, starting in third grade for Greenvale before moving to Carlton for the 2023-24 season and becoming part of their first-grade premiership-winning team. He also played club cricket in England for Farnsfield in 2024.

The breakthrough came in the Renegades' own system. He represented the Renegades Academy at the Melbourne T10 Invitational in 2025 and finished as the competition's leading wicket-taker, including best figures of 5 for 11, then played for the Renegades in the Top End T20 in Darwin, where he took a hat-trick and 4 for 13 against Northern Territory Strike. A teacher by trade, he was at his school in Thornbury when he was told he had earned his first professional contract — the first BBL player signed after the contracting embargo lifted in August 2026.

Where he fits in the Renegades

The Renegades' spin group is the most interesting part of their squad, and Archer is the leg-spin half of it. His job is the middle overs: bowl four overs, take wickets, and keep the scoring rate down while the fast bowlers rest. On the Chennai surface in the season opener, where spin has historically done well, he could be in the side from game one.

He is also a reminder of what the Renegades' Academy is for. The club signed him from its own programme, not from another state's system, and his story — regional Victoria, premier cricket, Academy, replacement player, full contract — is the one the club will point to when it talks about its pathway. If he takes wickets in BBL|16, he becomes a fixture; if he does not, the spin places are contested enough that he will have to fight for them.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Renegades missed the finals last season, and the climb back starts in Chennai.
