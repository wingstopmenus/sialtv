BBL|16 · Sydney Thunder

Ryan Hadley — Thunder bowler, BBL|16

Ryan Hadley is the young fast bowler the Sydney Thunder are backing for the long term — a New South Wales quick who made his Big Bash debut in BBL|15 and was re-signed in January 2026 for two more seasons, until the end of BBL|17.

A debut, then a deal

Hadley's story so far is short and encouraging. He made his Big Bash debut for the Thunder, and the club liked what it saw enough to re-sign him in January 2026 for a further two seasons — a deal that runs until the end of BBL|17. Young fast bowlers do not get two-year deals by accident.

He is a right-arm fast-medium bowler from New South Wales, and his game is built on the basics done well: good length, good shape, and the ability to bowl in the powerplay without going for too many. In a Thunder attack now stacked with international pace — Boult, Ferguson, Sams — Hadley is the young quick learning from the best in the business.

That is a valuable place to be. A young bowler who trains with Trent Boult and Lockie Ferguson every day, and plays alongside them on match days, learns more in one season than most learn in three.

Learning from the best

The Thunder's pace rebuild has changed Hadley's world. Last season he was a young quick in a developing attack; this season he is a young quick in an attack led by two New Zealand greats. His job has not changed — bowl well when the captain throws him the ball — but the standard around him has gone up.

He will compete for a place with the senior quicks and with Will Salzmann, the all-rounder who returned to the Thunder this season. The competition for spots is fierce, and that is the point: a side that wants to play finals needs depth, and Hadley is part of that depth.

His New South Wales career runs alongside it. The Blues are the pipeline the Thunder build from, and Hadley is one of the players who proves the model works.

What BBL|16 means for him

For a young fast bowler, a season like this one is about opportunity. The Thunder play ten league games between 16 December and 17 January, and fast bowlers get injured, get rested, and lose form. When the chance comes — and it will come — Hadley needs to be ready.

He has already shown he can handle the step up: a debut, then a two-year deal, then a place in a squad that expects to play finals. BBL|16 is the season where he turns promise into a regular place, or at least into the kind of performances that make the next contract a formality.

The season opens against Adelaide Strikers at Manuka Oval on 16 December.
