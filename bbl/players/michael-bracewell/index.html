Michael Bracewell — Renegades all-rounder, BBL 2026-27

Michael Bracewell is a New Zealand all-rounder who has captained his country in both T20 and one-day international cricket — and he has given up a central contract with New Zealand Cricket to play a full BBL season with the Melbourne Renegades. The 35-year-old signed a casual playing contract with the Black Caps so he could commit to BBL|16.

Role: All-rounder · Batting: Right-hand bat · Bowling: Right-arm off-break · Team: Melbourne Renegades

The all-rounder who chose the BBL

Bracewell's signing is unusual: international players rarely walk away from a central contract to play a full domestic T20 season. He did it because he wanted a maiden Big Bash campaign, and the Renegades wanted a player who can bat in the middle order, bowl four overs of off-spin, and lead a dressing room.

That versatility is his value. He is a right-handed batter who can rebuild an innings or finish one, and a right-arm off-break bowler who gives Luke Ronchi a spin option that also bats — the same role Ben Manenti plays, but with international captaincy experience behind it. New Zealand Cricket confirmed the move in October 2026, shifting him from a central contract to a casual one. The Renegades get him for the whole season, and that availability is part of the deal: no international call-ups pulling him away mid-tournament.

Squad note: the Renegades have announced 15 players so far, as of 31 October 2026. The full squad has not been announced — clubs can carry up to 18 players and are still adding names — so this page will be updated as the club confirms more signings.

A late bloomer who captained his country

Bracewell made his international debut for New Zealand in 2022, at 31 — a late start by modern standards — and went on to represent his country across all three formats. He has captained New Zealand at both T20I and ODI level, a sign of how quickly he earned the trust of the side.

His domestic career was built with Wellington, where his off-spin and middle-order batting made him one of New Zealand's most reliable domestic all-rounders for years before the international call finally came. He is also part of a famous cricket family: his uncle is former New Zealand captain John Bracewell, and his cousin Doug Bracewell also played for New Zealand. The Renegades are his first BBL club, and at 35 he is the most experienced overseas signing in the squad.

Where he fits in the Renegades

The Renegades' middle order is where games are won and lost in T20 cricket, and Bracewell is the player built for it. Fraser-McGurk, Brown and Bairstow do the damage at the top; Bracewell and Manenti are the ones who make sure a good start becomes a good total, and a bad start does not become a collapse.

His off-spin also balances the attack. With Archer's leg-spin and Stow's left-arm wrist-spin, the Renegades have three different spin types, and Bracewell's is the most economical — the one you bowl when you need four quiet overs, not necessarily four wickets. His captaincy experience helps too: the Renegades' BBL|16 captain is still to be confirmed, and a dressing room with a man who has captained his country is a calmer place.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Renegades missed the finals last season, and the climb back starts in Chennai.
