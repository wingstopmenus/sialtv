BBL|16 · Sydney Thunder

Sam Billings — Thunder wicketkeeper, BBL|16

Sam Billings is the England wicketkeeper-batter the Sydney Thunder have kept for BBL|16 — the only overseas player still contracted when the off-season began, and a player who has captained England in one-day cricket. He is back as the Thunder's overseas anchor under new head coach Andrew Flintoff.

The overseas player the Thunder kept

When the contracting embargo lifted in August, every club had to decide which overseas players to keep. The Thunder kept one: Sam Billings. That tells you what the club thinks of him — not just a wicketkeeper-batter, but a leader, a finisher, and a player who understands the Big Bash.

Billings is a right-handed batter who keeps wicket, and he does both jobs to a high standard. With the bat he is at his best in the middle order, where he can rebuild an innings or finish one — the kind of player who turns 140 into 170. Behind the stumps he is sharp against spin, which matters in a Thunder attack that leans on Tanveer Sangha's leg-spin and Chris Green's off-spin.

He has also captained Kent and stood in as England's one-day captain, and that experience shows in the way he manages a game from behind the stumps.

England, Kent, and the world

Billings has played white-ball cricket for England across formats, and he has been a franchise cricketer around the world — the IPL, the Hundred, the Big Bash. His domestic base is Kent, where he has captained the side and been one of their most reliable white-ball players for years.

His Big Bash career has been with the Thunder, where he has become a fixture rather than a flying visit. Overseas players who return season after season are rare in the competition; Billings is one of them, and the Thunder's decision to keep him when every other overseas contract lapsed says plenty about the relationship.

For BBL|16, he is the overseas player the new signings are built around. Trent Boult and Lockie Ferguson are the headline arrivals, but Billings is the one who knows the club, the ground, and the competition.

A new Thunder, under Flintoff

The Thunder missed the finals last season, a year after reaching the BBL|14 final — where they lost to Hobart Hurricanes. The new season is a fresh start under Flintoff, the former England captain who has signed a two-year deal, and the squad has been rebuilt around pace: Boult and Ferguson join Daniel Sams and Tanveer Sangha.

Billings's role in that rebuild is to give the middle order its spine. The Thunder's batting has Warner and Konstas at the top and power through the middle; Billings is the player who makes sure the innings does not fall apart between them. A wicketkeeper who bats in the middle order is also a second captain on the field — and with a new head coach, that matters.

The season opens against Adelaide Strikers at Manuka Oval on 16 December.
