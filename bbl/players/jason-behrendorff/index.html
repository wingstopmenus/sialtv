BBL|16 · Melbourne Renegades

Jason Behrendorff — Renegades bowler, BBL 2026-27

Jason Behrendorff is one of the most respected fast bowlers in the BBL — a left-arm quick who won titles with Perth Scorchers and played for Australia at the 2019 World Cup. He joined the Renegades as a headline signing, but he will not bowl in BBL|16: he was ruled out for the season with a shoulder injury, and the club has signed Mustafizur Rahman as cover.

Role: Bowler · Batting: Left-hand bat · Bowling: Left-arm fast-medium · Team: Melbourne Renegades

The leader the attack has lost

Behrendorff was signed to be the Renegades' new-ball leader — the experienced left-armer who swings the ball early, bowls in the powerplay, and teaches a young pace group how to plan an innings. His season-ending shoulder injury, confirmed by the club in November 2026, takes that leadership out of the side before a ball is bowled.

It is a cruel blow for a club that had already lost plenty of key bowlers this off-season. Behrendorff's value was never just his wickets: left-arm pace at the top of the order gives a captain a different angle, and his experience of winning BBL titles with Perth is exactly what a Renegades side that missed the finals last season was buying. The club's response — signing Bangladesh left-armer Mustafizur Rahman, one of the world's most prolific T20 wicket-takers — shows how big the hole is. Behrendorff is contracted through BBL|17, so the plan is for him to return next season.

Squad note: the Renegades have announced 15 players so far, as of 31 October 2026. The full squad has not been announced — clubs can carry up to 18 players and are still adding names — so this page will be updated as the club confirms more signings.

A Scorchers champion, then a Renegade

Behrendorff spent most of his BBL career with the Perth Scorchers, where he was part of title-winning sides and one of the league's most reliable new-ball bowlers. His left-arm swing and accuracy earned him a place in Australia's white-ball sides, including the 2019 World Cup squad.

The Renegades signed him during the BBL's first Player Movement Window as one of three headline arrivals, alongside Brendan Doggett and Caleb Jewell. It was a statement signing: a proven winner joining a club trying to become one again. The shoulder injury means the statement will have to wait a year — but his contract runs to BBL|17, and the club has made it clear he remains part of its plans.

Where he fits in the Renegades

On paper, Behrendorff was the senior quick in an attack that pairs him with Doggett and Tom Rogers. Without him, the new-ball job falls to Doggett and Rogers, with Sam Elliott in his first full season and Michael Archer's leg-spin providing the variation. The Renegades' batting is strong enough to win games, but the bowling now has to prove it can defend totals without its leader.

His absence also changes the Chennai opener. The first BBL match ever played outside Australia will be played on a slower surface, where Behrendorff's swing and cutters would have been valuable. Instead, the Renegades will lean on their spinners — Archer, Callum Stow, Ben Manenti and Michael Bracewell — and hope the quicks find a way through. Behrendorff's role this season is off the field: mentoring the young quicks and getting his shoulder right for BBL|17.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Renegades missed the finals last season, and the climb back starts in Chennai.
