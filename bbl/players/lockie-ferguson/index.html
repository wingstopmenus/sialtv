Lockie Ferguson — Thunder bowler, BBL|16

Lockie Ferguson is the second half of the Sydney Thunder's New Zealand pace double — a right-arm fast bowler signed for BBL|16 alongside Trent Boult, giving the Thunder two of the quickest and most experienced fast bowlers in world cricket in the same attack.

Pace, pure and simple

Ferguson's game is built on one thing: speed. He is a right-arm fast bowler who regularly bowls at the sharp end of the speed gun, and he uses that pace to hurry batters — short balls that climb, yorkers that arrive before the bat is down, and a bouncer that has ended many a T20 innings early.

He has been a New Zealand white-ball regular for years, and he has played franchise cricket around the world, including the IPL, where his pace has made him one of the most sought-after overseas quicks. T20 batters do not like facing genuine pace, and Ferguson bowls genuine pace.

For the Thunder, he is the complement to Boult. Boult swings the new ball; Ferguson hits the pitch hard. One asks questions with movement, the other with speed — and a batter who has just faced one has to reset completely for the other.

The Boult-Ferguson pair

The Thunder's decision to sign two New Zealand quicks together is deliberate. Boult and Ferguson have bowled together for New Zealand for years; they know each other's rhythms, they know when to attack and when to hold, and they know how to hunt in pairs. A new-ball partnership that already exists is worth more than two bowlers learning each other from scratch.

Behind them, Daniel Sams offers left-arm variety and Tanveer Sangha offers leg-spin, so the Thunder attack has a shape: pace at both ends, spin in the middle, and a death-bowling group that can close games out. On paper it is one of the strongest attacks in BBL|16.

The question is whether the batting can match it. The Thunder missed the finals last season; the bowling rebuild is the club's answer, and Ferguson is half of it.

New Zealand, the IPL, and the world

Ferguson has played white-ball cricket for New Zealand across formats, and he has been a fixture in franchise leagues — the IPL above all, where his pace has earned him contracts with several sides. He is a bowler built for the biggest stages, and the Big Bash is the next one.

His T20 record is that of a wicket-taker rather than a container: he bowls to take wickets, and he accepts that the occasional over will go for runs. In a Thunder side that wants to return to the finals, that aggression is the point — wickets win T20 games more reliably than dot balls do.

His first game in Thunder colours comes against Adelaide Strikers at Manuka Oval on 16 December, in Canberra.
