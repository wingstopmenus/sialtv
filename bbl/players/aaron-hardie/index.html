Aaron Hardie — Scorchers all-rounder, BBL 2026-27

Aaron Hardie is a Western Australia all-rounder who has played white-ball cricket for Australia — and he was the man who finished the BBL|15 final for the Perth Scorchers, hitting the winning runs as the champions beat Sydney Sixers. He is contracted through BBL|17, and he is one of the core players the title defence is built around.

Role: All-rounder · Batting: Right-hand bat · Bowling: Right-arm medium-fast · Team: Perth Scorchers

The all-rounder who does the finishing

Hardie's job is to be the player who is there at the end. He bats in the middle order, bowls right-arm medium-fast, and fields brilliantly — the kind of all-rounder who does not always make headlines but regularly makes the difference in close games.

His most famous moment came in the BBL|15 final at Optus Stadium, in front of 55,018 people. The Scorchers had bowled the Sixers out for 132, and Hardie was at the crease when the winning runs were hit — the image of a title won. He has played T20 and one-day cricket for Australia, and his domestic record for Western Australia is strong in both formats. He is a right-handed batter who can rebuild an innings or finish one, and a bowler who gives the captain four reliable overs.

Squad note: the Scorchers have announced 15 players so far, as of 31 October 2026. The full squad has not been announced — clubs can carry up to 18 players and are still adding names — so this page will be updated as the club confirms more signings.

From Western Australia to the national side

Hardie came through Western Australia's system as a batting all-rounder, and his domestic form earned him a place in the Scorchers' side and then in Australia's white-ball squads. He made his T20 international debut for Australia in 2023 and has played one-day cricket for the country since.

His BBL career has been with the Scorchers throughout, and he has been part of the side's most successful period — the club has won six titles, more than any other team in the league. He is a player who has grown up in a winning dressing room, and that shows in the way he plays: calm under pressure, clear about his role, and willing to do the unglamorous work. The Scorchers' coach Adam Voges trusts him in every situation, and that trust is earned.

Where he fits in the Scorchers

The Scorchers' side in BBL|16 is built around its core — Marsh, Connolly, Hardie and Agar with the bat, and Richardson, Morris and Paris with the ball — and Hardie is the all-rounder who links the two halves. He bats in the middle order behind Marsh and Turner, and bowls the overs that let the frontline quicks rest.

His value is his versatility. If the top order fires, he is the finisher; if it fails, he is the rebuild; if a bowler is injured, he is the cover. The Scorchers have lost two of the final's heroes — Finn Allen is now with the Stars, and Josh Inglis had not re-signed when the squad lists were last updated — so the players who remain, Hardie among them, carry more of the load. The title defence starts in Chennai, and Hardie is one of the players it depends on.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Scorchers topped the table last season with 14 points, and the defence of that position starts in Chennai.
