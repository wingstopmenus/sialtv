BBL|16 · Sydney Thunder

David Warner — Thunder batter, BBL|16

David Warner is the biggest name in the Sydney Thunder squad — a left-handed opener, one of the greatest white-ball batters Australia has produced, and the captain who led the Thunder to the BBL|14 final. He is back for BBL|16, the season after the Thunder missed the finals.

The captain who took them to the final

Warner's Thunder story is a late-career second act. After retiring from international cricket in 2024, he returned to the Big Bash as the Thunder's captain — and in BBL|14 he led them to the final, where they lost to Hobart Hurricanes. It was a reminder that a great player does not stop being great just because he stops playing for his country.

He is a left-handed opener who scores quickly from ball one, and his game has always been built on aggression: he takes the bowlers on, he scores in front of square, and he puts pressure on the fielding side before they have settled. In the Big Bash, where the powerplay decides so many games, that is the most valuable skill there is.

As captain he is demanding, direct, and tactically sharp — a leader who sets fields aggressively and backs his bowlers to take wickets. Under new head coach Andrew Flintoff, Warner's experience is the bridge between the old Thunder and the new one.

An Australian great

Warner's international career needs little introduction. A triple-format player for Australia for more than a decade, a World Cup winner, and one of the most destructive openers the game has seen, he retired from international cricket in 2024 as one of the finest batters of his era.

His Big Bash career has been with the Thunder, where he has become the club's biggest draw — the player fans come to see, and the player opposition bowlers plan for. His domestic base has always been New South Wales, and the Thunder are his Big Bash home.

For BBL|16, he is contracted again, and the squad around him has been transformed: Trent Boult and Lockie Ferguson with the ball, Sam Konstas at the top of the order with him, and a new head coach in Flintoff. The message is clear: the Thunder are not rebuilding, they are reloading.

One more run at the title

The Thunder missed the finals last season, a year after reaching the BBL|14 final. The new season is a fresh start under Flintoff, and Warner's job is the same as ever — score runs at the top, lead the side, and show the young players what it takes to win.

His opening partnership with Sam Konstas is one of the most intriguing in the competition: a veteran great and a 21-year-old sensation, batting together at the top of the order. If it works, the Thunder's batting looks formidable; if it does not, the middle order — Bancroft, Maddinson, Billings — has to carry the load.

The season opens against Adelaide Strikers at Manuka Oval on 16 December, and the Sydney Smash follows three days later — Warner against his old rivals, the Sixers, under lights.
