BBL|16 · Melbourne Renegades

Jake Fraser-McGurk — Renegades batter, BBL 2026-27

Jake Fraser-McGurk is one of the most feared young hitters in world cricket — an Australia white-ball opener who announced himself with a 29-ball ODI fifty against West Indies in February 2024, then took his power-hitting to the Indian Premier League with Delhi Capitals. For the Renegades, he is the batter the whole innings is built around.

Role: Batter · Batting: Right-hand bat · Team: Melbourne Renegades

The opener the innings is built around

Fraser-McGurk's job is the simplest and the hardest in the side: score fast, score big, and set the tone in the first six overs. When he gets going, the Renegades can post totals that no bowling attack wants to chase; when he falls early, the middle order has to rebuild.

His value is not just the runs but the pressure he puts on bowlers from ball one. Opening bowlers cannot settle into a length against him, captains burn their best bowlers early, and the field spreads — which lets the batter at the other end, often Caleb Jewell, play himself in. He is contracted through BBL|17, the longest deal of any Renegades batter alongside Josh Brown, because the club knows a player like him is the hardest thing in T20 cricket to replace.

Squad note: the Renegades have announced 15 players so far, as of 31 October 2026. The full squad has not been announced — clubs can carry up to 18 players and are still adding names — so this page will be updated as the club confirms more signings.

From a 29-ball fifty to the IPL

Fraser-McGurk's breakthrough came in February 2024, when he made his ODI debut for Australia and reached fifty from 29 balls against West Indies — one of the fastest ODI fifties by an Australian. It was the innings that put his name on the map around the world.

The Indian Premier League came calling soon after, with Delhi Capitals signing him for a season in which his strike rate at the top of the order made him one of the tournament's talking points. He has been a Renegades player since BBL|13, and his BBL reputation is built the same way: long levers, a high backlift, and a willingness to hit the first ball of the innings over the rope. The challenge now is consistency — turning the spectacular starts into the big hundreds that win tournaments.

Where he fits in the Renegades

The Renegades' batting plan starts with Fraser-McGurk and Josh Brown taking the powerplay apart. Brown's 140 off 57 balls against Adelaide Strikers in the BBL|13 Challenger showed what this top order can do on its day, and Bairstow's arrival adds a third destructive opener to the mix.

Luke Ronchi's problem is a good one: three openers for two spots, with Jewell and Harry Dixon also in the top-order mix. Fraser-McGurk's place is not in doubt — he is the first name on the team sheet. The question is who bats with him, and how the Renegades use Bairstow's keeping and experience around him. If the top order fires, the team page says it plainly: this is a team that can beat anyone.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Renegades missed the finals last season, and the climb back starts in Chennai.
