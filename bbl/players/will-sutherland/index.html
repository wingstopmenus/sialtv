Will Sutherland — Renegades all-rounder, BBL 2026-27

Will Sutherland is a Victorian all-rounder who has captained both his state and the Renegades — a right-arm fast-medium bowler and a powerful middle-order batter who made his ODI debut for Australia against West Indies in February 2024. He is contracted through BBL|17, and he is the player the Renegades' rebuild is built around.

The all-rounder at the heart of the side

Sutherland's job is to do everything. He bowls in the powerplay and at the death, bats in the middle order, and captains — he has led Victoria and has captained the Renegades before. The BBL|16 captaincy is still to be confirmed, but whoever wears the armband, Sutherland is the on-field leader.

His value is his completeness. There are better pure batters and better pure bowlers in the league, but few players who do both jobs as well as he does, and his presence lets the Renegades balance their side: an extra batter when he bowls, an extra bowler when he bats. He is a big man who hits the ball a long way and bowls in the mid-130s, and he has been the face of the Renegades' Victorian core for years. The club gave him a contract through BBL|17 because he is the player the next generation — Dixon, Peake, Stow, Archer — looks to.

Squad note: the Renegades have announced 15 players so far, as of 31 October 2026. The full squad has not been announced — clubs can carry up to 18 players and are still adding names — so this page will be updated as the club confirms more signings.

From Victoria's captain to Australia

Sutherland came through Victoria's system as a teenage all-rounder with a famous name — his father James was Cricket Australia's chief executive — and made his mark early, captaining Australia at the Under-19 World Cup before becoming Victoria's captain at a young age.

His international breakthrough came in February 2024, when he made his ODI debut for Australia against West Indies. It was the reward for years of consistent domestic cricket, where his Shield record as a bowling all-rounder and his white-ball hitting had made him one of the most respected players in the country. He has not yet become an Australia regular, but he is in the frame, and a strong BBL|16 as the Renegades' senior all-rounder keeps him there.

Where he fits in the Renegades

The Renegades' squad is built around its Victorian core, and Sutherland is the core's leader. He bats in the middle order behind Fraser-McGurk, Brown and Bairstow, and bowls the overs that matter — the powerplay when a wicket is needed, the death when the game is on the line.

His captaincy experience also matters because the BBL|16 captain is still to be confirmed. If he takes the armband, he leads a side with three international stars and a group of young Victorians; if he does not, he is still the senior player the captain turns to. Either way, the Renegades' season will be shaped by how much Sutherland does with bat and ball.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Renegades missed the finals last season, and the climb back starts in Chennai.
