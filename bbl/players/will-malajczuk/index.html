Will Malajczuk — Scorchers all-rounder, BBL 2026-27

Will Malajczuk is a 19-year-old all-rounder from Perth who scored the fastest century in Under-19 World Cup history — an unbeaten hundred from 51 balls against Japan in January 2026, with five sixes and 12 fours. The Perth Scorchers signed him on a two-year deal in September 2026, and BBL|16 is his first Big Bash season.

The teenager with a world record

The Scorchers' squad table lists Malajczuk without a formal role — the club has not yet put a label on him. His cricket says it plainly enough: he is a left-handed top-order batter who bowls right-arm off-spin, and he is one of the brightest young prospects in Australian cricket.

His record hundred came in the group stage of the 2026 Under-19 World Cup, when he blasted to an unbeaten century from 51 balls against Japan — the fastest century in the tournament's history. He had already captained Western Australia at the Under-19 National Championships, and he was named Australia's Under-19 captain for an ODI series against India in Queensland in September 2025. The Scorchers signed him on a two-year deal, and Jhye Richardson — who was with him at the Chennai launch — called him the player to watch out for, a powerful left-hander who also bowls off-spin.

Squad note: the Scorchers have announced 15 players so far, as of 31 October 2026. The full squad has not been announced — clubs can carry up to 18 players and are still adding names — so this page will be updated as the club confirms more signings.

From Wembley Downs to a state contract

Malajczuk is from Wembley Downs in Perth, plays club cricket for Subiaco-Floreat, and is an all-round sportsman who also played for Claremont Football Club in the WAFL colts — he had to choose between Australian rules football and cricket as his primary sport, and he chose cricket.

He made his List A debut for Western Australia against Tasmania on 10 February 2026, scoring a quickfire 23, and in his second match he took two wickets with his right-arm off-spin against Victoria. He was rewarded with his first state contract for the 2026-27 season, and he trained with the senior Australian team ahead of the Ashes Test in Perth. He models his batting on South African Quinton de Kock, and he is pronounced Mala-check.

Where he fits in the Scorchers

The Scorchers' spin group in BBL|16 is built around Agar and Connolly's left-arm orthodox, with Harrison's leg-spin and Malajczuk's off-spin as the alternatives — and Malajczuk is the youngest of them, and the one with the most room to grow. His left-handed batting gives the side a different option at the top of the order, and his off-spin gives the captain a bowler who can tie batters down.

His role this season is likely to be as a squad player who gets his chance when a senior player is rested or injured — and with 10 league games and a long season, chances come. He has already shown he can score runs at every level, and his 51-ball hundred says he can do it fast. If he gets a game in BBL|16, the Scorchers will know quickly whether he is a future star or a player who needs more time.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Scorchers topped the table last season with 14 points, and the defence of that position starts in Chennai.
