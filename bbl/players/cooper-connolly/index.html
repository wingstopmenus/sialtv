Cooper Connolly — Scorchers all-rounder, BBL 2026-27

Cooper Connolly is a Western Australia all-rounder who has already played Test and one-day cricket for Australia — and he was part of the Perth Scorchers' BBL|15 title-winning side. He is a left-handed batter and a left-arm orthodox spinner who captained Australia at the 2022 Under-19 World Cup, and he is one of the brightest young players in the country.

Role: All-rounder · Batting: Left-hand bat · Bowling: Slow left-arm orthodox · Team: Perth Scorchers

The young all-rounder at the heart of the champions

Connolly's job is to be the Scorchers' future, playing in the present. He bats left-handed in the middle order, bowls left-arm orthodox spin, and fields brilliantly — the kind of all-rounder who can win a game with bat or ball, and who has already done both at the highest level.

He captained Australia at the 2022 Under-19 World Cup, made his one-day international debut for Australia against England in September 2024, and made his Test debut in Sri Lanka in February 2025. That is a remarkable rise for a player who is still in his early twenties, and it says the national selectors see him as a long-term international. He is contracted through BBL|16, and the Scorchers see him as one of the core players the side is built around — alongside Marsh, Hardie and Agar with the bat.

An Under-19 captain, then a Test debutant

Connolly's rise began at the 2022 Under-19 World Cup, where he captained Australia and showed the leadership and the all-round game that would take him to the top. He made his mark in domestic cricket for Western Australia, where his left-handed batting and left-arm spin made him a regular in both formats.

His international breakthrough came quickly: an ODI debut against England in September 2024, then a Test debut in Sri Lanka in February 2025. He has played white-ball cricket for Australia since, and his BBL career has been with the Scorchers throughout — including the BBL|15 title win, where he was part of the side that beat Sydney Sixers twice in a week. He is a player who has grown up winning, and that shows in the way he plays.

Where he fits in the Scorchers

The Scorchers' side in BBL|16 is built around its core — Marsh, Connolly, Hardie and Agar with the bat — and Connolly is the youngest of them, and the one with the most room to grow. He bats in the middle order, bowls four overs of left-arm spin, and gives the captain a player who can do two jobs in one spot.

His left-arm spin is also part of the plan. The Scorchers' spin group is built around Agar and Connolly's left-arm orthodox, with Harrison's leg-spin and Malajczuk's off-spin as the alternatives — and Connolly's is the one that can tie batters down in the middle overs. At Perth Stadium, where the Scorchers play five home games, his batting against pace will be tested, and his bowling on the bouncy pitch will be hard to play. The title defence starts in Chennai, and Connolly is one of the players it depends on.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Scorchers topped the table last season with 14 points, and the defence of that position starts in Chennai.
