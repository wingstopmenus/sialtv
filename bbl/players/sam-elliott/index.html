Sam Elliott — Renegades all-rounder, BBL 2026-27

Sam Elliott is a right-arm fast-medium bowler from Victoria who earned his first full BBL contract for BBL|16 after two seasons as a local replacement player with the Renegades. He is the son of former Australia Test opener Matthew Elliott, and his breakthrough came in September 2024, when he took 7 for 12 against Tasmania — the best figures by a Victorian in the One-Day Cup.

Role: All-rounder · Batting: Right-hand bat · Bowling: Right-arm fast-medium · Born: 18 February 2000, Mitcham, Victoria · Team: Melbourne Renegades

The quick who earned his place

Elliott's story is one of patience. He was awarded a rookie contract with Victoria in May 2019, spent years on the edge of the side, and joined the Renegades as a replacement player — first for BBL|14, then for Will Salzmann, who was ruled out with a hamstring injury on the eve of BBL|15. This season, for the first time, he is a full contracted player.

His best BBL performance so far came against the Perth Scorchers, where he took 4 for 28 — one of the best individual bowling performances of that campaign — and he finished the season with five wickets in total. He is a new-ball and middle-overs bowler, consistent rather than express, and his value is reliability: he bowls good areas, takes wickets when batters try to hit him, and does not leak runs. With Jason Behrendorff ruled out for the season, that reliability is worth more than ever.

Seven for twelve, and a Shield final

Elliott's signature performance came on 23 September 2024, in the opening round of the One-Day Cup, when he took 7 for 12 against Tasmania at Junction Oval and bowled them out for 126. It was only his 11th game in the format, and his figures passed Jon Holland's 6 for 29 as the best by a Victorian in the competition — the second-best figures ever in the One-Day Cup, behind only Shaun Tait's 8 for 43.

He built on it. In the 2025-26 Sheffield Shield season he took 33 wickets at 17.18 and was preferred to Mitch Perry for the last pace spot in Victoria's Shield final side — a call the Victorian coach described as one of the hardest he had made. He has also played for Australia A, and his father Matthew was the leading run-scorer in the 1997 Ashes series. The family name carries weight; Sam is now making his own.

Where he fits in the Renegades

The Renegades' pace attack is younger than it looks, and Elliott is the bowler who holds it together. Brendan Doggett and Tom Rogers take the new ball, and Elliott is the first-change quick who bowls through the middle overs and keeps the pressure on while the spinners work from the other end.

His domestic form says he belongs. He took 14 wickets at 23.28 in the One-Day Cup season that earned him his Renegades chance, including that career-best 7 for 12, and he was Victoria's most reliable performer across both red-ball and white-ball cricket last season. If he takes his Shield form into BBL|16, he will not be a replacement player again — he will be a first-choice quick.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Renegades missed the finals last season, and the climb back starts in Chennai.
