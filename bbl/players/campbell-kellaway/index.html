BBL|16 · Melbourne Stars

Campbell Kellaway — Stars batter, BBL 2026-27

Campbell Kellaway is a left-handed opening batter from Victoria who has been with the Melbourne Stars since BBL|13 — and the club has kept him because he is a homegrown talent who was selected for Australia A after a strong finish to the domestic season. He has played 10 games for the Stars, and BBL|16 is the season he is expected to make the opening spot his own.

The homegrown opener waiting for his run

Kellaway's job is to give the Stars a left-handed option at the top of the order. The Stars' batting is built around right-handers — Finn Allen, Glenn Maxwell, Marcus Stoinis — and a left-handed opener changes the angles for the opposition's new-ball bowlers.

He has been a long-term prospect for the club, not a quick fix. The Stars signed him for BBL|13, kept him through two more seasons, and re-signed him through BBL|17 — a sign that they see him as part of the side's future, not just a squad filler. His selection for Australia A, after a strong finish to the domestic season last summer, was the clearest sign that the wider game has noticed him too. The challenge now is turning a place in the squad into a place in the eleven, and holding it.

From Victoria to Australia A

Kellaway came through Victoria's system as a left-handed top-order batter, and his domestic form earned him a place in the Stars' BBL|13 squad. He has played 10 games for the club since then, learning the league from the inside while building his state career.

His breakthrough at state level came late last summer, when a strong finish to the domestic season earned him selection for Australia A — the step below the national side, and the place where future internationals are tested. The Stars' high performance manager Clint McKay called him and Anlezark long-term prospects when they re-signed in August 2025, and said the club's programme is built to produce as many T20 cricketers as possible. Kellaway is the programme working.

Where he fits in the Stars

The Stars' top order has a new look in BBL|16: Finn Allen has arrived from Perth to open, and the middle order is built around Maxwell and Stoinis. Kellaway's role is to be the left-handed opener who can bat with Allen, or the reserve opener who steps in when the side needs a change.

His left-handedness matters against the league's new-ball attacks, most of which are built around right-arm quicks who like bowling to right-handers. At the MCG, where the Stars play four of their five home games, his ability to play straight and use the big outfield is a better fit than a hitter who only scores square of the wicket. If he gets a run of games, the Stars will know quickly whether he is a future opener or a squad player.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Stars finished fourth last season, their best return in years, and the table is where their title chase will be measured.
