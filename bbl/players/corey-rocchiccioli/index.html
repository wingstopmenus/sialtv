Corey Rocchiccioli — Sixers bowler, BBL|16

Corey Rocchiccioli is the Western Australia off-spinner the Sydney Sixers signed for BBL|16 as the replacement for Test-capped Todd Murphy. A right-arm off-break bowler who gives the ball flight and drift, he arrives on a one-year deal — and he is looking forward to reuniting with friends like Josh Philippe in the Sixers dressing room.

The spinner who replaces Murphy

The Sixers had a hole to fill. Todd Murphy, their Test-capped off-spinner, moved on — and the club's answer was Rocchiccioli, a Western Australia spinner who has built his reputation on control and on taking wickets when batters come after him.

He is a different kind of off-spinner from the containing type: he flights the ball, he drifts it, and he invites the big shot. That is a brave way to bowl in T20 cricket, and it is the way that takes wickets. The Sixers have signed him for exactly that — a spinner who attacks, not one who survives.

The announcement came in October 2026, and it took the Sixers to 16 announced players of a maximum 18. His own words on signing said it plainly: he was excited to play alongside Sean Abbott and Tim Seifert, and to reunite with Philippe and the young New South Wales players coming through.

Off-spin with a point of difference

Rocchiccioli's bowling is built on the classic off-spinner's tools — flight, drift, dip, and a quicker ball — but his value in the Big Bash is the match-up. Right-handed batters who want to hit through the line have to take him on, and left-handers have to deal with the ball turning away from them.

He will share the spin duties with Jager Chohan's leg-spin and Joel Davies' left-arm orthodox, giving the Sixers three spinners turning it three different ways. At the SCG, where the pitch has traditionally helped spin more than most Australian grounds, that trio could decide home games.

He is also a capable lower-order batter, right-handed, who can add runs at the end of an innings. The Sixers' batting failed in last season's final; every extra run from the tail helps.

From Western Australia to Sydney

Rocchiccioli plays his domestic cricket for Western Australia, where he has been part of one of the strongest state sides in the country. The move to the Sixers takes him from Perth's pace-friendly conditions to Sydney's spin-friendly ones — a change that should suit his bowling.

He knows the Sixers dressing room already. Josh Philippe is a Western Australia teammate, and the pair have played together for years. That familiarity matters in a T20 squad: a new signing who already trusts the keeper standing up to him, and the captain who throws him the ball.

For BBL|16, his job is clear — bowl four overs most nights, take wickets in the middle, and give the Sixers the spin option they lost when Murphy left.
