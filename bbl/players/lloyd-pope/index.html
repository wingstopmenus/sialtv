Lloyd Pope — Adelaide Strikers bowler, BBL 2026-27

Lloyd Pope is the leg-spinner the Adelaide Strikers build their middle overs around: a right-arm wrist-spinner who has been taking wickets since he was a teenager — most famously eight of them in a single Under-19 World Cup quarter-final against England in 2018.

The wrist-spinner who attacks

Pope is a right-arm leg-spin bowler who bats right-handed, and his job is to take wickets in the middle of the innings. Leg-spin is a risk and a reward in T20: the ball can be hit, but it can also turn past the bat, and Pope’s googly has troubled batters at every level he has played.

For Adelaide, he is the point of difference. The Strikers’ attack is built on pace — Henry Thornton, Ben Stokes, Tom Straker — and Pope is the bowler who changes the pace of the game entirely. When he is on, the middle overs belong to him; when he is off, the captain has to protect him. That is the life of a wrist-spinner.

Eight wickets and a reputation made young

Pope made his name at the 2018 Under-19 World Cup, taking 8 for 35 against England in the quarter-final — one of the great bowling performances in youth cricket, and still the spell people mention first when his name comes up. It put him on the map before he had played a full domestic season.

Since then he has played for South Australia and the Strikers, learning the harder part of a spinner’s career: bowling in T20 when batters are trying to hit every ball. His BBL career has been about that education, and BBL|16 is another chapter in it — with a stronger attack around him than he has had before.

What Pope brings to BBL|16

Pope is one of the announced contracted players for BBL|16 — the full squad has not been announced yet — and his role is the middle-overs wicket-taker. With Stokes and Thornton bowling pace at both ends of the innings, Pope’s four overs in the middle are where Adelaide will try to break partnerships.

Watch his match-up against left-handed batters. Leg-spin turning away from the left-hander is one of T20’s classic contests, and Pope’s googly is designed for exactly that moment. There are no BBL|16 numbers for Pope yet; the season starts on 12 December, and his first spell of the campaign comes against Sydney Thunder on 16 December.

What’s next

BBL|16 runs from 12 December 2026 to 26 January 2027. The opener is Match 1, Melbourne Renegades vs Perth Scorchers, on Saturday 12 December at MA Chidambaram Stadium in Chennai — the first BBL match played outside Australia — starting at 2:40 PM IST (2:10 PM PKT). Lloyd’s team, the Adelaide Strikers, open their campaign in Match 3. The full list of every BBL fixture is on the BBL Schedule.

  • Match 3 — Adelaide Strikers vs Sydney Thunder: Wednesday 16 December 2026, 1:45 PM IST (1:15 PM PKT), Manuka Oval, Canberra (Away).
  • Finals: The finals are the Qualifier on 19 January, the Knockout on 20 January, the Challenger on 23 January and the final on 26 January, Australia Day. Finals venues and times are still to be confirmed.
  • Points: Eight teams play ten league matches each. A win is worth 2 points, a no result is worth 1, and the top four qualify for the finals, with net run rate deciding places when teams are level on points.
