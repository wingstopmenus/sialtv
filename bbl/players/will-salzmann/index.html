BBL|16 · Sydney Thunder

Will Salzmann — Thunder bowler, BBL|16

Will Salzmann is the Western Sydney all-rounder who has come home — a 22-year-old from Camden who returns to the Sydney Thunder for BBL|16 on a one-year deal, after being listed with the club in BBL|13 and then developing with New South Wales. He is still waiting for his Big Bash debut.

Back where it started

Salzmann was born and raised in Camden, in Sydney's south-west, and he was first listed with the Thunder in BBL|13. He did not get a game then, and he went back to New South Wales to earn his way back — which is exactly what he did.

The performance that sealed his return came in New South Wales' One-Day Cup opener against Queensland in October 2026: a player-of-the-match 70 off 65 balls, helping the Blues chase down a target of 359 to win by four wickets. It was the kind of innings that makes selectors take notice — a young all-rounder winning a game his side should have lost.

The Thunder signed him on a one-year deal in October 2026, with general manager Trent Copeland saying the club had been keen to secure him and that he had never been better placed to take the next step. His junior club is the Tahmoor Turtles, and he marked the signing with a clinic for their young cricketers at Cricket Central.

An all-rounder in the Thunder's mould

Salzmann is a right-handed batter who bowls right-arm seam — a middle-order all-rounder in the Daniel Sams mould, and the Thunder have room for more than one of those. Squad coverage lists him among the side's three middle-order all-rounders, alongside Sams and Aidan O'Connor, moving around based on the situation.

His batting is his calling card: the 70 against Queensland was not a one-off, but the latest in a string of impressive performances for New South Wales. His bowling is the complement — seam-up overs that give the captain a sixth or seventh option, and a fielder who covers ground in the deep.

He is 22, and he has time. The Thunder have signed him for BBL|16, not for a decade — but the club's language suggests they see a player who can grow into a regular, not a one-season story.

Still waiting for the first game

Salzmann has never played a Big Bash game. He was listed in BBL|13 without playing, and he has spent the seasons since making himself impossible to ignore. BBL|16 is the season where that changes — or where the wait continues, if the competition for places is too fierce.

The competition is real. The Thunder's squad is stacked with all-rounders — Sams, Green, O'Connor — and with two New Zealand quicks in Boult and Ferguson. Salzmann's chance will come from form, from fitness, or from a tactical change. When it comes, he will be ready: he has been ready for three years.

His first chance to play comes against Adelaide Strikers at Manuka Oval on 16 December.
