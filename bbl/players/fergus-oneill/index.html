Fergus O'Neill — Sixers bowler, BBL|16

Fergus O'Neill is the Victorian fast bowler the Sydney Sixers signed from the Melbourne Renegades for BBL|16 — one of only two free agents the Sixers snapped up in the post-season window, the other being Ollie Davies. He arrives contracted until BBL|17, and he gives the finalists a new-ball option they did not have last season.

The Renegades quick who crossed over

O'Neill's move is the kind that tells you what a club thinks it is missing. The Sixers lost last season's final because their batting was shut down — but their answer in the off-season was not only batting. They added O'Neill to the attack as well, a right-arm fast-medium bowler with first-class experience for Victoria.

He is a bowler built for Australian conditions: tall, accurate, and able to move the ball off the seam. He bowls the hard lengths that make batters play, and he has the temperament for the new ball — the overs where a wicket changes the game and a loose ball disappears.

Contracted until BBL|17, he is not a one-season fix. The Sixers are buying the next two years of a young quick who has already shown he can handle first-class cricket and is now being asked to do the same in the Big Bash.

A new face in a settled attack

The Sixers' pace attack has a familiar core: Sean Abbott and Ben Dwarshuis share the new ball, and have done for years. O'Neill's arrival gives the captain a third option — a bowler who can take the new ball when one of the senior pair needs a rest, or bowl through the middle when the game needs a wicket.

That depth matters over a ten-game league season. Fast bowlers get injured, get rested, and lose form; a side with three genuine new-ball options is a side that can cope. O'Neill is also a different shape from Abbott and Dwarshuis — a Victorian seamer rather than a New South Wales one — and different angles make an attack harder to line up.

Behind the quicks, the Sixers have rebuilt their spin too: Corey Rocchiccioli's off-spin and Jager Chohan's leg-spin give the middle overs a new shape. O'Neill's job is to make sure the innings starts well enough for the spinners to attack.

What Victoria saw in him

O'Neill came through the Victorian system, where he earned first-class opportunities on the back of accuracy and bounce rather than raw pace. Victorian quicks have a long record of succeeding in the Big Bash — the state's pitches reward the same skills the Big Bash does — and the Sixers have backed that record before.

His Renegades spell gave him his first taste of Big Bash cricket. The move to the Sixers gives him a bigger stage: a side that expects to play finals, a home ground in the SCG that rewards clever seam bowling, and a dressing room full of internationals to learn from.

The first test comes at The Gabba, where Brisbane's batting is at its most aggressive. A new-ball bowler who can hold his nerve there will have earned his place quickly.
