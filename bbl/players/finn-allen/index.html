BBL|16 · Melbourne Stars

Finn Allen — Stars batter, BBL 2026-27

Finn Allen is the signing of the BBL|16 window — a New Zealand opener who was Perth Scorchers' leading run-scorer last season, then moved to the Melbourne Stars on a three-year deal. He opened the batting for Perth in last season's final and made 36; now he wears green, and the Stars' batting has a new spearhead.

Role: Batter · Batting: Right-hand bat · Team: Melbourne Stars

The opener the Stars built their summer around

Allen's job is to do for the Stars what he did for the Scorchers: score fast at the top and set the tone. He was last season's leading run-scorer for the champions, and the Stars signed him for three years — through BBL|18 — because a player like him does not come on the market often.

His value is his intent. Allen does not play himself in; he hits from the first over, and his strike rate forces the opposition to change plans before the powerplay is over. The Stars' coach Cameron White called him and Jamie Overton some of the most sought-after players in franchise cricket, and the club's batting — Maxwell, Stoinis, Cartwright, Harper — is built to score quickly around him. If Allen fires, the Stars' top order is the most dangerous in the league.

From Perth's leading scorer to a three-year deal

Allen made his name as a New Zealand white-ball opener, and his international highlight is a 137 off 62 balls against Pakistan in a T20 international in January 2024 — one of the best T20 innings by a New Zealander. He has also played in the Indian Premier League with Royal Challengers Bengaluru.

Last season he was Perth's leading run-scorer, and he opened the batting in the BBL|15 final against Sydney Sixers, making 36 as the Scorchers won their sixth title. Then he left. The Stars signed him on a three-year deal, the longest of any Stars player, and the move was the biggest of the off-season: the champions' best batter joining the side that has never won the title. He also plays in the ILT20 with Abu Dhabi Knight Riders, which runs to 20 December — so his BBL start may overlap with that commitment.

Where he fits in the Stars

The Stars finished fourth last season, their best return in years, and the club's response was to sign the best available opener. Allen joins Maxwell and captain Stoinis in a batting group that, on paper, can match anyone — and the team page says so plainly.

His left-field value is what he does to the opposition. Bowlers who have planned for Maxwell and Stoinis now have to plan for Allen first, and that changes the first six overs of every Stars innings. At the MCG, where the Stars play four home games, his hitting over the short square boundaries is a weapon. The question is not whether he scores runs, but whether the Stars can finally turn a great batting group into a first title.
