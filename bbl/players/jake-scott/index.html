Jake Scott — Sixers squad member, BBL|16

Jake Scott is the young all-rounder the Sydney Sixers signed on his first BBL contract for BBL|16 — described by the club as emerging talent with an eye to the future. His listed role on the announced squad is to be confirmed, and he is one of the youngest names in a side chasing its fourth title.

A first BBL contract

Scott's signing was announced in late September 2026, and the club's language was deliberate: an exciting young all-rounder, signed with an eye to the future. The Sixers were not buying a finished player; they were buying the next few years of one.

He is a New South Wales player — part of the same pipeline that has produced Jack Edwards, Joel Davies, Hayden Kerr and Lachy Shaw — and he was in the Blues' One-Day Cup squad in October 2026, named alongside fellow Sixers youngster Riley Kingsell. That is the clearest public marker of where he stands: inside the state's white-ball plans, and now inside the Sixers' too.

The Sixers' announced squad list leaves Scott's role as to be confirmed, and this page leaves it that way. Squad coverage describes him as an all-rounder, and the club's own announcement used the same word.

The Sixers' way with young players

The Sixers have done this before. Their title-winning sides were built on a core of New South Wales players who came through together, and the current squad is full of them. Scott is the next name in that line — signed young, developed inside the system, and given time.

Patience is the path here. A young player joining a champion side rarely walks straight into the eleven; he waits, he trains, and he takes the chance when it comes — often late in a season, when the games matter less than the future, or when a senior player is rested or injured.

For a young all-rounder, the season is about the dressing room as much as the field: training with Sean Abbott, learning from Steve Smith, and being ready when the captain needs an extra bowling option or a late-order hitter.

Where he sits in the squad

The Sixers have announced 16 of a maximum 18 players for BBL|16, so two spots remain to be filled. Scott is one of the 16, which means the club has already chosen him over other options — a meaningful vote of confidence for a player on his first contract.

Scott is part of a changing of the guard at the Sixers too. Abbott, Smith and Silk still lead the senior group, but the squad around them keeps getting younger — Scott, Riley Kingsell and Lachy Shaw are the next wave, and BBL|16 puts all three inside the tent for the first time.

A debut this season is not guaranteed; whether he plays a game is still to be confirmed. What is settled is his place in the squad, in the system, and in the club's plans.
