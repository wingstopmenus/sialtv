BBL|16 · Melbourne Renegades

Brendan Doggett — Renegades bowler, BBL 2026-27

Brendan Doggett is a right-arm fast-medium bowler who has spent time in Australia's Ashes squad — and the Renegades signed him to lead a new-ball attack that lost plenty of key bowlers this off-season. He came to Melbourne from the Adelaide Strikers, and with Jason Behrendorff ruled out for the season, his experience matters more than ever.

The new-ball bowler the attack leans on

Doggett's job in BBL|16 is straightforward: take the new ball, bowl hard lengths, and give the Renegades early wickets. He is one of the few quicks in the squad who has bowled at the top of a BBL innings for years, and his time around the Australian Test squad has sharpened him.

The context makes him central. Behrendorff, the club's most experienced fast bowler, is out for the season with a shoulder injury, and the Renegades' pace stocks are younger than they look on paper — Tom Rogers is the established name, Sam Elliott is in his first full BBL season, and Michael Archer has played one BBL game. Doggett's overs, especially in the powerplay, are the ones the captain will plan around. If he strikes early, the Renegades' spin group — Archer, Callum Stow, Ben Manenti and Michael Bracewell — has something to defend.

From the Strikers to the Ashes squad

Doggett built his reputation in Queensland and South Australian cricket before his BBL career took him to the Adelaide Strikers, where he bowled in the powerplay and at the death. The Renegades picked him up during the BBL's first Player Movement Window, one of three headline arrivals alongside Behrendorff and Caleb Jewell.

His selection in Australia's Ashes squad during 2025-26 was the clearest sign of how far his stock had risen — a domestic fast bowler trusted to be around a Test attack. He has not yet played Test cricket, but the call-up put him in the frame, and a strong BBL|16 with the new ball keeps him there. For a bowler in his late twenties, this season is the platform.

Where he fits in the Renegades

The Renegades' batting is the side's strength — Fraser-McGurk, Brown and Bairstow at the top — so the bowling has to be good enough to defend totals rather than win games on its own. Doggett's role is to make sure the opposition never gets a flying start.

He pairs naturally with Tom Rogers, another right-arm quick who likes the ball swinging early, and the two of them give Luke Ronchi four powerplay overs of pace before the spin takes over. At the MCG and Junction Oval, where the Renegades play their home games, his ability to hit the pitch hard and find bounce is a better fit than a swing-only bowler. The Chennai opener, on a slower surface, will test his variations early.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Renegades missed the finals last season, and the climb back starts in Chennai.
