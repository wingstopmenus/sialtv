Marcus Stoinis — Stars all-rounder, BBL 2026-27

Marcus Stoinis is the Melbourne Stars' captain and one of the most destructive batters in BBL history — the holder of the second-highest individual BBL score, 147 not out against Sydney Sixers in January 2020. He is an Australia white-ball regular and a 2021 T20 World Cup winner, and he leads a Stars side that has never won the title.

The captain carrying the title wait

Stoinis's job is to lead the Stars to their first BBL title — and to bat like the player who made 147 not out against the Sixers. He is the captain, the senior all-rounder, and the batter the side looks to when the game is on the line. He is contracted through BBL|16, and this season is his chance to finish the job.

His record says he can do it. His 147 not out against Sydney Sixers in January 2020 was the highest BBL score at the time, and it remains the second-highest ever, behind only Glenn Maxwell's 154 not out. He is a right-handed batter who hits the ball as cleanly as anyone in the league, and a right-arm medium-pace bowler who gives the captain — himself — a useful extra option. He has played white-ball cricket for Australia for a decade, won the 2021 T20 World Cup, and played in the IPL with Lucknow Super Giants.

A record innings, and a World Cup

Stoinis's BBL career has been with the Stars throughout, and his 147 not out in January 2020 is the innings that defines it: an unbeaten hundred that took the Stars to a huge total and showed what the side could do when its senior players fired. He has been the club's captain for several seasons, and his leadership has been tested by the side's repeated failures in finals.

Internationally, he has been an Australia white-ball regular since 2015, and he was part of the side that won the 2021 T20 World Cup in the UAE — Australia's first T20 world title. He has also been one of the most sought-after all-rounders in franchise cricket, playing in the IPL and in leagues around the world. At 37, he is the senior player in a Stars side that has added Finn Allen and the Overton twins around him.

Where he fits in the Stars

The Stars finished fourth last season, their best return in years, before losing the Knockout to Hobart Hurricanes — and the club's response was to sign Allen and the Overtons to support Stoinis and Maxwell. The captain now has a batting group that, on paper, can match anyone.

His role is to be the finisher and the leader. Allen opens, Maxwell bats in the middle, and Stoinis is the player who bats through the last five overs and wins the close games. His medium pace is also useful at the MCG, where change of pace matters more than raw speed. If the Stars are to end their 16-season wait for a first title, it will be because their captain had his best season.
