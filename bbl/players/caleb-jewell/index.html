Caleb Jewell — Renegades batter, BBL 2026-27

Caleb Jewell arrives at the Melbourne Renegades as a BBL champion. The left-handed opener spent seven seasons with the Hobart Hurricanes, opened the batting in their BBL|14 title win, and then asked for a new chapter — the Renegades traded for him in February 2025 on a two-year deal.

Role: Batter · Batting: Left-hand bat · Born: 21 April 1997, Hobart, Tasmania · Team: Melbourne Renegades

The experienced opener the top order needed

Jewell is the steady hand in a Renegades top order built for fireworks. Jake Fraser-McGurk and Josh Brown hit the ball as hard as anyone in the league; Jewell is the batter who makes sure the innings does not fall apart around them.

He has opened in the BBL for years, so he knows the first six overs better than most — which bowlers to target, when to take the Power Surge, and how to rebuild after an early wicket. His T20 best of 76 came against Brisbane Heat in January 2025, and across 58 Big Bash appearances for Hobart he scored 1,160 runs at a strike rate of 126.5. The Renegades did not sign him for one innings; they signed him for the reliability that lets the hitters play freely.

Seven years a Hurricane, then a trade

Jewell made his first-class debut for Tasmania on 15 March 2016 and his T20 debut for the Hurricanes in the Abu Dhabi T20 Trophy on 5 October 2018. He became a mainstay at the top of the order for Tasmania — the 2022-23 Ricky Ponting Medallist, with a career-best Shield score of 227 against Western Australia in 2024 — and played 58 BBL games for Hobart.

His last act as a Hurricane was the biggest one. In the BBL|14 final he opened alongside breakout star Mitchell Owen, hit 13 off 12 balls in a 109-run opening stand, and helped Owen stay on strike during his record-equalling 39-ball century as Hobart beat Sydney Thunder to win the title. A few weeks later he was traded to the Renegades, with Hobart happy to free salary-cap space and facilitate his search for a bigger opportunity. He also spent the 2025 English summer with Derbyshire.

Where he fits in the Renegades

The Renegades' top order has genuine power, but power without a platform wastes itself. Jewell gives Luke Ronchi a left-handed opener who has batted in BBL finals and knows what a title-winning dressing room feels like — something this squad, which missed the finals last season, does not have much of.

He is also a useful foil for Fraser-McGurk. When the young opener is flying, Jewell rotates the strike and lets him bat; when Fraser-McGurk falls early, Jewell is the one who steadies the innings and bats through the powerplay. At the MCG, where the Renegades finish the league stage in the Melbourne derby, his experience of big occasions will count for plenty.
