BBL|16 · Sydney Sixers

Tim Seifert — Sixers wicketkeeper, BBL|16

Tim Seifert is the New Zealand wicketkeeper-batter who has joined the Sydney Sixers for BBL|16, arriving from the Melbourne Renegades as an overseas signing. A right-handed batter who scores fast at the top of the order, he gives the finalists a second wicketkeeper and another powerplay weapon.

From the Renegades to the Sixers

Seifert's move is one of the off-season's quieter ones, but it could be one of the most useful. The Renegades let him go; the Sixers picked him up as an overseas player for BBL|16, adding a New Zealand international to a squad that already had Josh Philippe behind the stumps.

Two wicketkeepers in one squad is not a luxury in the Big Bash — it is flexibility. Seifert can open the batting, bat in the middle order, keep wicket if Philippe is injured or rested, or play purely as a batter. That range lets the Sixers shape their XI around the opposition and the conditions, rather than around one fixed combination.

He is also a proven Big Bash player. He knows the grounds, the bowlers and the pace of the competition, so there is no settling-in period. He arrives ready to play from the first game.

Powerplay hitting, New Zealand style

Seifert's game is built for the first six overs. He is a right-handed batter who takes the aerial route early, scores square of the wicket, and puts pressure on bowlers before they have settled. New Zealand have used him at the top of their T20 side for the same reason — he can win a powerplay on his own.

That is exactly what the Sixers lacked in last season's final, when they were bowled out for 132 and never got going. A second powerplay hitter, alongside Philippe, means the opposition cannot simply bowl at one danger man and contain the other end.

Behind the stumps he is sharp and athletic, with the quick hands a keeper needs against spin — useful in a Sixers attack that now has three spinners in Rocchiccioli, Chohan and Davies.

Where he fits in the Sixers' XI

The Sixers' batting has a clear shape: Smith anchors, Philippe and Seifert provide the pace at the top, Silk and the Davies brothers work the middle, and Abbott and Kerr finish. Seifert's arrival means the side can play two wicketkeeper-batters without weakening the bowling — a combination most Big Bash sides cannot match.

He is one of two overseas players in the announced squad, alongside England leg-spinner Jager Chohan. The Sixers have used their overseas spots carefully: a spinner who takes wickets, and a keeper-batter who scores quickly. Both address a specific need from last season's final.

His first game in magenta comes at The Gabba on 15 December, against a Brisbane attack that will test any opener.
