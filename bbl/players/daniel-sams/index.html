BBL|16 · Sydney Thunder

Daniel Sams — Thunder all-rounder, BBL|16

Daniel Sams is the left-arm all-rounder who has been at the heart of the Sydney Thunder for years — a bowler with one of the best slower balls in the Big Bash, a lower-order hitter who has won games from nowhere, and a player contracted until BBL|17. He is back for BBL|16 in an attack rebuilt around pace.

The slower ball that wins games

Sams's signature is the slower ball — bowled with the same action as his stock delivery, arriving late, and deceiving batters who have already committed to the big shot. It is one of the most effective deliveries in the Big Bash, and Sams has built a career on it.

He is a left-arm fast-medium bowler who can bowl in the powerplay, the middle and the death, and his variety makes him hard to line up. Batters who face him have to watch for the yorker, the bouncer, the slower ball and the cutter — four different deliveries from the same run-up, and no way to know which one is coming.

With the bat he is a hard-hitting lower-order player who has finished games for the Thunder more than once. In a side that has lost finals by small margins, a few late runs from number eight can be the difference — and Sams has provided them.

New South Wales, Australia, and the Thunder

Sams plays domestic cricket for New South Wales and has represented Australia in T20 internationals. His Thunder career has been defined by consistency: he is one of the first names on the team sheet, season after season, and he was part of the side that reached the BBL|14 final — losing to Hobart Hurricanes.

His international experience shows in his temperament. He has bowled in big games for Australia and in finals for the Thunder, and he does not go missing when the pressure comes. Last season the Thunder missed the finals; Sams was one of the players who kept the side competitive through a difficult year.

For BBL|16, he is part of a pace group that has been transformed around him: Trent Boult and Lockie Ferguson arrive from New Zealand, and Sams — the left-armer who was already there — gives the attack its variety.

Pace, pace, and more pace

The Thunder's rebuild has been about pace — Boult and Ferguson joining, Sams and Tanveer Sangha already there. But pace alone does not win T20 games; it needs a bowler who can change the tempo, and Sams is that bowler. His slower balls and cutters are the change of pace that makes the quicks' speed look even quicker.

He will share the new ball with Boult or Ferguson, bowl through the middle when the game needs a wicket, and close out games at the death. It is the full job, and he has done it for the Thunder for years.

The season opens against Adelaide Strikers at Manuka Oval on 16 December, and the Sydney Smash follows three days later — Sams's slower balls against the Sixers' powerful top order, under lights.
