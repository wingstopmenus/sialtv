Matthew Renshaw — Brisbane Heat batter, BBL 2026-27

Matthew Renshaw is the Brisbane Heat’s most reliable run-scorer of recent seasons: a left-handed batter and former Australia Test opener who was the club’s top run-scorer in BBL|14 with 280 runs, and who re-signed on a three-year deal as the Heat built their future around him.

The Heat’s most reliable batter

Renshaw is a left-handed batter who bowls occasional right-arm off-spin, and his game is built on balance. He can open the batting or bat in the middle order, he scores quickly enough for T20 and steadily enough to hold an innings together, and he is the batter the Heat turn to when the top order fails.

His three-year deal, signed before BBL|15, says how the club sees him. Renshaw is not a short-term signing; he is the senior batter the young core is built around, and his 280 runs in BBL|14 — the most for the club that season — are the reason why.

A Test opener who rebuilt in Brisbane

Renshaw made his Test debut for Australia in 2016, opening the batting as a 20-year-old, and he played in the Ashes and on tours of Asia. He lost his place, rebuilt his game with Queensland, and came back into the Australian side — the classic story of a talented batter who had to learn the hard way.

For the Heat, he is the link between the generations. He has played with the club’s champions and its rebuilders, and he is now the senior batter alongside Usman Khawaja in a top order that also includes the young Max Bryant, Tim Robinson and Lachlan Hearne.

What Renshaw brings to BBL|16

Renshaw is one of the announced contracted players for BBL|16 — the full squad has not been announced yet — and his role is the flexible senior batter. He can open if the side needs him to, or bat at three or four and hold the middle order together; his off-spin gives the captain an extra option on turning pitches.

Watch his batting in the middle overs. Renshaw’s ability to rotate the strike and pick off the loose ball is what lets the Heat’s power hitters play with freedom around him. There are no BBL|16 numbers for Renshaw yet; the season starts on 12 December, and his first innings of the campaign comes against Sydney Sixers at the Gabba on 15 December.

What’s next

BBL|16 runs from 12 December 2026 to 26 January 2027. The opener is Match 1, Melbourne Renegades vs Perth Scorchers, on Saturday 12 December at MA Chidambaram Stadium in Chennai — the first BBL match played outside Australia — starting at 2:40 PM IST (2:10 PM PKT). Matthew’s team, the Brisbane Heat, open their campaign in Match 2. The full list of every BBL fixture is on the BBL Schedule.

  • Match 2 — Brisbane Heat vs Sydney Sixers: Tuesday 15 December 2026, 1:45 PM IST (1:15 PM PKT), The Gabba, Brisbane (Home).
  • Finals: The finals are the Qualifier on 19 January, the Knockout on 20 January, the Challenger on 23 January and the final on 26 January, Australia Day. Finals venues and times are still to be confirmed.
  • Points: Eight teams play ten league matches each. A win is worth 2 points, a no result is worth 1, and the top four qualify for the finals, with net run rate deciding places when teams are level on points.
