Joel Davies — Sixers all-rounder, BBL|16

Joel Davies is the left-arm spin all-rounder the Sydney Sixers keep turning to when they need control through the middle overs. A New South Wales product and the younger brother of teammate Ollie Davies, he is contracted for BBL|16 in a side that lost last season's final and is determined to go one better.

The quiet all-rounder in the middle

Davies does the job that rarely makes headlines: left-arm orthodox spin, tight lines, and enough batting down the order to matter. In a Sixers attack built around pace — Abbott, Dwarshuis, O'Neill — he is the bowler who slows the game down and lets the quicks attack from the other end.

His value shows up in the match-ups. Against right-handers who want to hit through the line, his arm ball and his change of pace are hard to get away; against left-handers, he can turn it away and force the big shot to the long boundary. Captains love a spinner who goes at under seven an over in the middle of a T20 innings, and Davies has built his Sixers career on exactly that.

With the bat he is a capable lower-order hitter rather than a front-line batter — but in a side that has lost finals by small margins, a few late runs from number eight can be the difference.

The Davies brothers, together at last

Joel's older brother Ollie spent last season with the Sydney Thunder. For BBL|16, Ollie has joined the Sixers as a free agent — so the brothers are teammates for the first time in the Big Bash, in the same squad, in the same dressing room.

It is a small story with a real edge to it. The Davies brothers grew up playing together in New South Wales, and now one bowls left-arm spin and the other bats in the top order of the same side. The Sydney Smash on 19 December — Sixers against Thunder, Ollie's old club — is the first time the brothers line up together against the team Ollie left.

For Joel, the family reunion changes nothing about his job. He is still the spinner the captain calls on when the game needs slowing down.

Where he fits in the Sixers' attack

The Sixers' spin department has a new shape for BBL|16. Corey Rocchiccioli's off-spin arrived from Western Australia, Jager Chohan's leg-spin is back for a third summer, and Davies' left-arm orthodox completes the set — three spinners turning it three different ways.

That variety matters at the SCG, the Sixers' home, where the pitch has traditionally helped spin more than most Australian grounds. Five of the Sixers' ten league games are at home, and Davies should bowl in all of them.

He is also part of the side's long-term plan. Contracted for BBL|16 alongside a core of young New South Wales players — Jack Edwards, Hayden Kerr, Lachy Shaw — he represents the Sixers' bet on home-grown talent rather than short-term overseas fixes.
