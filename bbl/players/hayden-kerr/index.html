Hayden Kerr — Sixers all-rounder, BBL|16

Hayden Kerr is the left-arm seam all-rounder who gives the Sydney Sixers balance. A New South Wales player, he bowls left-arm medium, bats down the order, and has been a Sixers regular for several seasons. He is contracted for BBL|16 as the side tries to go one better than last season's final defeat.

The left arm in the middle overs

Kerr's job is the one that holds an innings together. He bowls left-arm medium through the middle overs, where his angle across right-handers and his change of pace make scoring hard, and he bats low enough to rescue an innings and high enough to be trusted with one.

Left-arm seamers who can bat are the currency of modern T20 cricket, and Kerr is the Sixers' version. He does not need to be the fastest bowler on the side — Abbott and Dwarshuis handle that — he needs to be the reliable one, the bowler the captain can give the ball to in any situation and get a competitive over.

With the bat he is a left-handed hitter who has played useful innings for the Sixers in tight chases. In a side that was bowled out for 132 in last season's final, a few extra runs from the lower order are not a luxury.

A Sixers regular, season after season

Kerr has been part of the Sixers' setup for years now, and that continuity is part of his value. He knows the SCG, he knows the club's systems, and he knows the bowlers he shares the attack with — Abbott, Dwarshuis, and now Fergus O'Neill, the Victorian quick who arrived from the Renegades.

His New South Wales career runs alongside it. The Blues are the pipeline the Sixers build from, and Kerr is one of the players who proves the model works: a state cricketer who becomes a franchise regular, not by one spectacular season but by being good enough, often enough, for long enough.

For BBL|16 he is contracted alongside a spin trio — Rocchiccioli, Chohan and Joel Davies — that gives the Sixers more middle-overs options than they had last season. Kerr's seam is the complement to that spin.

One better than last season

The Sixers finished second in the league stage last season, six wins from ten and 13 points, then lost to Perth in the Qualifier by 48 runs and in the final by six wickets. The batting failed in the decider; the bowling, Kerr included, had carried the side to that point.

Going one better is not about one player. It is about the whole squad being a little deeper, a little more flexible, and a little harder to shut down. Kerr is part of that depth: an all-rounder who can bat seven and bowl four overs is two players in one squad spot, and in a competition where squads are only 18 deep, that is gold.

The early fixtures will test him quickly — Brisbane at the Gabba, then the Sydney Smash against a Thunder side stacked with fast bowlers.
