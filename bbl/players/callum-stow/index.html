BBL|16 · Melbourne Renegades

Callum Stow — Renegades bowler, BBL 2026-27

Callum Stow is a 24-year-old left-arm wrist-spinner — a rare bowling type in Australian cricket — and the Renegades re-signed him for BBL|16 after his debut season. He made his BBL debut against Hobart Hurricanes on 14 January 2025, then spent the off-season playing for the San Francisco Unicorns in Major League Cricket.

Role: Bowler · Batting: Right-hand bat · Bowling: Slow left-arm wrist-spin · Born: 27 August 2002, Geelong, Victoria · Team: Melbourne Renegades

The wrist-spinner who gives the attack variety

Left-arm wrist-spin is the hardest type of bowling to face in T20 cricket, because batters see so little of it — and Stow is one of very few bowlers in Australia who bowls it. His job is the middle overs: slow the game down, take wickets when batters try to hit him out of the attack, and give the captain a different option from the off-spin of Ben Manenti and Michael Bracewell.

He showed he belongs in BBL|15, taking wickets against Brisbane Heat in his second game and holding his place in a side that had Adam Zampa ahead of him. With Zampa gone to Hobart, the spin places are open, and Stow's four overs are the ones that can change a game in the middle. His MLC stint in the United States, where he took 4 for 25 against Lahore Qalandars, gave him franchise experience against international batters — useful preparation for a season that starts against the champions in Chennai, where spin matters.

From Geelong to the Unicorns

Stow came through Geelong Cricket Club and Victoria's pathway system, playing for Victoria in domestic cricket before the Renegades signed him in December 2024. His BBL debut came on 14 January 2025 against Hobart Hurricanes, and he kept his place through the rest of the season.

The off-season took him further. He was picked up by the San Francisco Unicorns in Major League Cricket, where his left-arm wrist-spin stood out in a league full of right-arm options. Back home, he was delisted from Victoria's contract list for 2026-27 but asked to keep training with the squad in the hope of further opportunities — a reminder that his BBL place is the platform his whole career now rests on.

Where he fits in the Renegades

The Renegades' spin group is the most interesting part of their squad: Stow's left-arm wrist-spin, Michael Archer's leg-spin, and the off-spin of Manenti and Bracewell give Luke Ronchi four different spin options. Stow is the one who turns the ball away from right-handers and into left-handers — the mirror image of a leg-spinner.

That variety matters on the Renegades' home grounds. At the MCG the ball can grip for wrist-spin, and at Junction Oval the smaller boundaries punish predictable bowling. Stow's job is to be unpredictable. If he takes wickets in the middle overs, the Renegades' fast bowlers — Brendan Doggett, Tom Rogers and Sam Elliott — have totals to defend at the death.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Renegades missed the finals last season, and the climb back starts in Chennai.
