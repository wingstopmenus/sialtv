BBL|16 · Sydney Sixers

Sean Abbott — Sixers all-rounder, BBL|16

Sean Abbott is the bowler the Sydney Sixers turn to when a game is slipping away — a fast-medium seamer who bowls at the death, and a lower-order batter who has won games from nowhere. He is back for BBL|16 as one of the most prolific bowlers in the competition's history, in a side that lost last season's final and wants it back.

The Sixers' spearhead, again

Abbott bowls the hard overs for the Sixers — the powerplay, the middle, and the death — and he has done it for more seasons than any other bowler in the side. BBL|16 is another year of the same job, and the Sixers' hopes rest on it more than ever after last season's final.

His value is not one skill but three. With the new ball he swings it just enough to ask questions; through the middle he changes pace and length; at the death he owns the yorker and the slower ball. Then, when the Sixers bat, he walks in low down the order and hits the ball as cleanly as anyone in the side — a batter who has finished games with the bat as often as he has closed them with the ball.

That range is why the Sixers keep building their attack around him. Younger quicks come and go, overseas signings rotate, but Abbott is the constant: the bowler the captain throws the ball to when the plan has run out.

A career built on the Big Bash

Abbott has been a Sixers man for most of his career, and an Australian white-ball international alongside it. New South Wales is his state side, and he has played one-day and T20 internationals for Australia, usually as the bowler trusted to do the unglamorous overs.

His Big Bash record is the quiet argument for his place among the greats of the competition. Season after season he is among the wickets, and he has done it while bowling the toughest overs in the format — the ones where a bad ball goes for six and a good one is expected, not praised. Few bowlers in BBL history have carried that load for as long.

He is also one of the competition's best lower-order hitters. The Sixers have won games because Abbott walked in at number eight and cleared the ropes, and that threat changes how opponents bowl at the men in front of him.

What last season's final left behind

The Sixers finished second in the league stage last season with 13 points, six wins from ten — then ran into Perth twice. A 48-run defeat in the Qualifier was followed by a six-wicket defeat in the final at Optus Stadium, where the Sixers were bowled out for 132.

Abbott's job in BBL|16 is to make sure the attack does not let a final get away like that again. Perth's batting shut the Sixers down in the decider; the response has been to keep the core of the side together and add fresh pace around him, with Fergus O'Neill arriving from the Renegades and Ben Dwarshuis still there to share the new ball.

The Sixers' first test comes early. They open at The Gabba against Brisbane Heat on 15 December, then face the Thunder in the Sydney Smash on 19 December — two games that will show quickly whether last season's hurt has sharpened them.
