BBL|16 · Melbourne Stars

Liam Hatcher — Stars bowler, BBL 2026-27

Liam Hatcher is a right-arm medium-fast bowler from New South Wales who has just had the best season of his career — 44 Sheffield Shield wickets in 2025-26, more than any other bowler in the competition, at speeds topping 146 kilometres per hour. The Melbourne Stars signed him for BBL|16, and he arrives as a bowler whose decade of patience has finally paid off.

01 — The quick whose time has come

The quick whose time has come

Hatcher's job in BBL|16 is to bring his Shield form into the shortest format. He is a tall, strong fast bowler who averages in the mid-130s and topped out at 146kph last summer, and his 44 Shield wickets — including 26 wickets at 20.92 in five matches opening the bowling — made him the best bowler in Australia's first-class competition.

That is a remarkable return for a bowler whose career has been a story of waiting. He was roped in by Sydney Thunder as a development rookie for BBL|06, played for the Melbourne Stars in BBL|10, and spent years on the edge of the New South Wales side while faster bowlers were picked ahead of him. He has always wanted to stay in New South Wales — he calls himself fiercely Western Sydney, wears the NSW No. 7 shirt in tribute to Newcastle Knights legend Andrew Johns — and the Stars are the BBL side that lets him do that while playing in Melbourne.

02 — Ten years an overnight success

Ten years an overnight success

Hatcher's talent was obvious early. He represented Australia at the 2016 Under-19 World Cup, and in August 2015 his 6 for 24 for Australia Under-19s against England Under-19s prompted Andrew Flintoff to praise him. He made his List A debut for Cricket Australia XI on 5 October 2015 and his first-class debut later that month against the touring New Zealanders.

Then came the wait: nine and a half years of frustration, 19 first-class matches snuck in around the edges, while a queue of international quicks kept him out of the side. The breakthrough came in 2025-26, when he finally got a run of games and took 44 Shield wickets — no one took more. He made his BBL debut for the Stars on 12 December 2020, and he has played 25 BBL matches, taking 27 wickets. BBL|16 is his chance to show that the Shield form was not a one-off.

03 — Where he fits in the Stars

Where he fits in the Stars

The Stars' pace attack is built around experience — Peter Siddle has played more than 200 Test wickets' worth of international cricket — and Hatcher is the bowler who gives it a new-ball edge. His height and pace make him a handful at the MCG, where the bounce rewards tall quicks, and his Shield record says he can bowl long spells without losing his pace.

He is also a useful lower-order batter, and his competitiveness is famous: he is a self-described sports nut, a member at Twin Creeks Golf Club with a handicap of six, and a Newcastle Knights fan who celebrated their upset win the week before the Shield final. If he takes his Shield form into BBL|16, the Stars' pace attack looks very different from last season.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Stars finished fourth last season, their best return in years, and the table is where their title chase will be measured.
