BBL|16 · Sydney Thunder

Matthew Gilkes — Thunder wicketkeeper, BBL|16

Matthew Gilkes is the wicketkeeper-batter who gives the Sydney Thunder depth behind the stumps — a New South Wales player, contracted until BBL|17, who can keep wicket, bat in the middle order, and step in whenever the side needs him. He is back for BBL|16 in a squad chasing the finals.

Last updated: 12 November 2026

The second keeper who is ready

Gilkes's job is one of the hardest in a T20 squad: be ready to play every game, knowing you might play none of them. Sam Billings is the Thunder's first-choice wicketkeeper — an England international, retained as the club's overseas anchor — and Gilkes is the player who covers him.

That does not make him a passenger. He is a right-handed batter who can play in the middle order as a specialist, and he has kept wicket for New South Wales and for the Thunder when called on. A second keeper who can bat is worth his place in any squad, and in a ten-game season with injuries and rotation, his chance usually comes.

He is also a sharp fielder when he is not keeping, so he can contribute even when he is not behind the stumps. The Thunder value that flexibility — it lets them shape their XI around the opposition rather than around one fixed combination.

A New South Wales cricketer

Gilkes came through the New South Wales system, where he has been a white-ball regular. The Thunder's squad is built on that pipeline — Gilkes, Sam Konstas, Daniel Sams, Tanveer Sangha, Chris Green and Ryan Hadley are all Blues — and the understanding shows in the field, where the side moves like a team that has played together for years.

His contract runs until BBL|17, which says the Thunder see him as part of the future, not a one-season fill-in. The club has kept faith with its young New South Wales players through the rebuild under new head coach Andrew Flintoff, and Gilkes is one of them.

For BBL|16, his task is to be ready. The season is long, the games come thick and fast, and a wicketkeeper who can step in without the side losing anything is gold.

A new Thunder, under Flintoff

The Thunder missed the finals last season, a year after reaching the BBL|14 final — where they lost to Hobart Hurricanes. The new season is a fresh start under Flintoff, the former England captain who has signed a two-year deal, and the squad has been rebuilt around pace: Trent Boult and Lockie Ferguson join Daniel Sams and Tanveer Sangha.

Gilkes's role in that rebuild is depth — the kind of depth that wins titles. A side that plays finals needs players who can step in without weakening the team, and a second wicketkeeper who can bat is one of the most valuable of those players.

The season opens against Adelaide Strikers at Manuka Oval on 16 December.
