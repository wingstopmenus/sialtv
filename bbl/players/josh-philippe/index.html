Josh Philippe — Sixers wicketkeeper, BBL|16

Josh Philippe is the wicketkeeper-batter who sets the tone for the Sydney Sixers. A Western Australia player with Australian T20 caps, he opens the batting, keeps wicket, and scores at a pace that can decide a game inside the powerplay. He is contracted for BBL|16 in a side chasing its fourth title.

The powerplay sets the tone

Philippe's job is simple to describe and hard to do: score quickly from ball one. As an opener and wicketkeeper, he faces the new ball, the field restrictions, and the best bowlers — and the Sixers' innings usually goes the way his first ten balls go.

He is a right-handed batter who scores all around the wicket, strong through the off side and happy to take the aerial route when the field is up. When he fires, the Sixers post totals that their attack can defend; when he falls early, the middle order — Smith, Silk, the Davies brothers — has to rebuild. That is the weight of opening in T20 cricket, and Philippe has carried it for the Sixers for years.

Behind the stumps he is just as important. The Sixers' attack mixes pace and spin, and a keeper who stands up well to the spinners and takes the sharp chances off the quicks is worth runs on the field.

Western Australia, Australia, and the Sixers

Philippe plays domestic cricket for Western Australia and has represented Australia in T20 internationals. His domestic record is that of a player trusted at the top of the order in every format he plays — a wicketkeeper who bats, rather than a batter who keeps.

His Sixers career has been defined by consistency at the top. He has opened through title runs and through rebuilding years, and he was there last season when the side finished second in the league stage with six wins from ten — only to lose twice to Perth when it mattered most.

For BBL|16, the top order around him has changed: Ollie Davies has arrived from the Thunder to add power, Tim Seifert has come in from the Renegades as a second wicketkeeper-batter, and Steve Smith remains the anchor. Philippe's role does not change — he is still the man who starts the innings.

The final the Sixers want to forget

Last season ended badly. The Sixers lost the Qualifier to Perth by 48 runs, then lost the final by six wickets after being bowled out for 132 at Optus Stadium — Steve Smith top-scoring with 24. It was a batting failure, and it was the openers' failure first: the innings never got going.

Philippe's task in BBL|16 is to make sure it does not happen again. The Sixers' attack is strong enough to defend most totals; the question is whether the batting can post them. A fast start from Philippe changes the whole shape of an innings — it lets Smith play himself in, it lets Silk play his game, and it puts the opposition's bowlers under pressure from the first over.

The Gabba opener against Brisbane Heat on 15 December is the first chance to set the tone.
