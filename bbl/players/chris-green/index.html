BBL|16 · Sydney Thunder

Chris Green — Thunder all-rounder, BBL|16

Chris Green is the Thunder man through and through — an off-spin all-rounder who has been part of the Sydney Thunder for years, captained the side, and is back for BBL|16 as the club rebuilds under new head coach Andrew Flintoff. He bowls right-arm off-break, bats right-handed down the order, and does the quiet jobs that win T20 games.

The Thunder's own

Green is one of those players a club is built around without ever making a fuss about it. He has been a Thunder regular for years, through good seasons and bad ones, and he has captained the side — a mark of the trust the club places in him. When the Thunder reached the BBL|14 final, Green was part of the team that got them there.

His game is built on control. He bowls right-arm off-spin with a flat trajectory and a sharp arm ball, and he goes at the kind of economy rate that lets the fast bowlers attack from the other end. In a Thunder attack now stacked with pace — Boult, Ferguson, Sams — Green's spin is the change of pace that makes the whole thing work.

With the bat he is a lower-order hitter who has won games from nowhere, and in the field he is one of the side's most reliable catchers. He is, in short, the player every T20 side needs and few sides have.

A former captain, still leading

Green has captained the Thunder before, and that experience still shows — in the way he talks to the bowlers, in the fields he suggests, and in the calm he brings when a game turns. With a new head coach in Flintoff and two new overseas quicks in the side, the Thunder need players who know the club and can show the newcomers how it works.

He is also a New South Wales player, part of the same state system as Daniel Sams, Tanveer Sangha and Sam Konstas. The Thunder's squad is built on that pipeline, and Green is one of its senior figures.

His role in BBL|16 is the same as ever: bowl four overs, take wickets when batters come after him, add runs down the order, and be the player the captain turns to when the plan needs changing.

Pace around him, spin from him

The Thunder's rebuild has been about pace — Boult and Ferguson arriving, Sams and Sangha already there. But pace alone does not win T20 games; it needs spin to control the middle overs, and Green is the spinner who does that job alongside Sangha's leg-spin.

The two spinners complement each other: Sangha turns it away from right-handers with his leg-break, Green turns it in with his off-break. Between them they give the Thunder a middle-overs attack that can bowl to any batter, on any ground, in any situation.

The Thunder missed the finals last season. Green's job is to help make sure that does not happen again — not with a spectacular season, but with the same steady, reliable cricket he has played for years.
