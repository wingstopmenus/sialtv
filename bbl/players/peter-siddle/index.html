Peter Siddle — Stars bowler, BBL 2026-27

Peter Siddle is one of the most respected fast bowlers Australia has produced — a veteran of 67 Tests and 221 Test wickets who has reinvented himself as a BBL specialist in his late thirties. The Melbourne Stars signed him for BBL|16, and he brings something no other bowler in the squad has: more than a decade of international cricket.

The veteran quick still going strong

Siddle's job is to bowl the hard overs and teach the young quicks how to do it. He is a right-arm fast-medium bowler who has played Test cricket against every side in the world, and his experience of bowling in Australian conditions — the bounce, the heat, the long spells — is unmatched in the Stars' squad.

He has been a BBL player for years, most recently with the Adelaide Strikers, and he has taken wickets in every season he has played. His value is not pace — he bowls in the mid-130s now — but skill: he swings the new ball, seams it off the pitch, and bowls the yorkers and slower balls that win T20 games at the death. He is contracted for BBL|16, and at 41 he is the oldest player in the Stars' squad by some distance.

Two hundred and twenty-one Test wickets

Siddle made his Test debut for Australia in 2008 and went on to play 67 Tests, taking 221 wickets. He was part of the Australian attack for more than a decade, bowling in Ashes series home and away, and he took a hat-trick against England at the Gabba on his birthday in 2010 — one of the most famous moments of his career.

He has also played one-day and T20 cricket for Australia, and county cricket in England with Essex and Nottinghamshire. His BBL career has taken him to the Adelaide Strikers and now the Stars, and he has been a mentor to young fast bowlers wherever he has played. The Stars' young quicks — Austin Anlezark and Liam Hatcher — will learn more from a season alongside Siddle than from any coaching manual.

Where he fits in the Stars

The Stars' pace attack is a mix of experience and youth: Siddle and Hatcher are the senior quicks, Anlezark is the future, and Jamie Overton provides the pace. Siddle's role is the powerplay and the death — the two hardest phases to bowl in T20 cricket — and his ability to bowl both makes him the captain's most trusted bowler.

His presence also changes the dressing room. The Stars have never won the title, and they have a history of losing the big moments — last season they lost the Knockout to Hobart Hurricanes after finishing fourth. Siddle has won Ashes series and played in World Cups; he knows what winning looks like, and he knows how to handle the pressure of a final. If the Stars reach the finals in BBL|16, his experience will be worth more than his wickets.

The BBL Points Table will carry the standings once the season starts — the Stars finished fourth last season, their best return in years, and the table is where their title chase will be measured.
