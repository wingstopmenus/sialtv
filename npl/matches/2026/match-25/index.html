Janakpur Bolts vs Chitwan Rhinos, Friday, 13 November 2026 at 4:30 PM NST

The 25th match of the NPL season is the evening game of Friday 13 November 2026, starting at 4:30 PM NST — 10:45 AM UTC, 3:45 PM PKT — at the Tribhuvan University International Cricket Ground in Kirtipur. It follows the afternoon match between Kathmandu Gorkhas and Sudurpaschim Royals, and it is the last game of the day.

This is the first meeting of the two sides this season. Chitwan Rhinos sit fourth on 5 points from five matches; Janakpur Bolts sit fifth on 4 points from six matches, and this is their final league game. The winner takes a big step towards the playoffs; the loser is left needing help from the final weekend. The full scorecard will appear on this page once the match is played.

Janakpur have lost their last three matches, all by clear margins, while Chitwan have alternated wins and defeats all season. Something has to give on Friday evening, and for Janakpur there is no tomorrow if it does not.

Janakpur's season has slipped away in the last ten days. They beat Kathmandu Gorkhas by 8 wickets in Match 5 and Pokhara Avengers by 9 runs in Match 9, and then the defeats started: by 2 runs to Biratnagar Kings in Match 12, chasing 174/3; by 16 runs to Lumbini Lions in Match 14; and by 27 runs to Sudurpaschim Royals in Match 21, when they were bowled out for 93 chasing 120/9. Three defeats in a row, each one a game they could have won, and now the last game of their league season is a knockout in all but name.

Chitwan's season has been the opposite kind of uneven. They beat Biratnagar Kings by 8 runs in Match 2, had their game against Lumbini Lions abandoned in Match 7, lost to Sudurpaschim Royals by 5 wickets in Match 11, beat Pokhara Avengers by 30 runs in Match 17, and lost to Kathmandu Gorkhas by 10 runs in Match 20. They have never won two in a row, and they have never lost two in a row either. A win on Friday would be their third of the season and would keep fourth place in their own hands.

A Chitwan win takes them to 7 points with one match left, against Karnali Yaks on Sunday, and would end Janakpur's season on the spot — the Bolts would finish on 4 points with no games left, and no route to the top four. Chitwan would then need only a point from Sunday, or results elsewhere, to confirm a playoff place.

A Janakpur win takes them to 6 points and past Chitwan in the table, but it does not finish the job. With no games left, Janakpur would need Biratnagar Kings and Karnali Yaks to drop points on the final weekend, and their net run rate of −0.303 would count against them in any tie. It would keep them alive; it would not make them safe.

The top four qualify for the playoffs: Qualifier 1 on Tuesday 17 November, the Eliminator on Wednesday 18 November, Qualifier 2 on Thursday 19 November and the final on Saturday 21 November, all at 4:30 PM NST at Kirtipur. Kathmandu Gorkhas lead on 8 points, Sudurpaschim Royals are second on 8 and Lumbini Lions third on 7 — and fourth place, currently Chitwan's on 5 points, is the prize both sides are playing for.

Players to watch

Season 3 numbers only, from matches 1 to 21. Every name below links to that player's profile.

Harmeet Singh is the leading wicket-taker in the whole league, with 11 wickets in six matches at an economy of 5.83, and he is Janakpur's captain. His best spell, 4 for 17 against Sudurpaschim Royals in Match 21, came in a defeat — which tells you how much Janakpur have leaned on him. If he takes early wickets on Friday, Chitwan's batting has a problem.

With the bat, Janakpur look to Saif Hassan, who has 140 runs in four matches with a highest score of 71, and Trit Raj Das, who has 112 runs in six matches with a highest score of 61. Both have made fifties this season, and Janakpur need one of them to make another on Friday.

For Chitwan, Ravi Bopara has 171 runs in four matches with a highest score of 55, the second-most runs in the league, and Rijan Dhakal has 10 wickets in four matches, the second-most in the league. Chitwan's two best players are a batter and a bowler, and both are in the form that decides close games.

Head to head

Janakpur Bolts and Chitwan Rhinos have not met before in Season 3, so this is their first meeting of the season. It is a strange way for a shootout to arrive — neither side has a read on the other from this season, only the shared experience of losing to the same teams at the top of the table.

Both have lost to Sudurpaschim Royals, and both have beaten Pokhara Avengers. The difference is the margins: Chitwan beat Pokhara by 30 runs, Janakpur by 9; Chitwan lost to Kathmandu by 10 runs, while Janakpur beat Kathmandu by 8 wickets in Match 5. On those numbers the two sides are almost impossible to separate — which is why Friday evening should be close.
