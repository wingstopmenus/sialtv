Season 3 summary

Sahil Patel's season is a story about a position. He is a wicketkeeper — the specialist position — and his game is defined by the gloves first. A domestic keeper valued at NPR 5 lakh in consecutive auctions is being paid for reliability: clean takes standing up to the spinners, sharp work to the medium-pacers, and the concentration to keep for 20 overs on a turning Kirtipur surface where every ball can misbehave.

That reliability has won him the gloves ahead of KS Bharat, the India keeper with a Ranji triple hundred and more than 1,800 T20 runs. Bharat is one of Kathmandu's three overseas signings, and he has not batted in the season. Patel has kept in the last three matches — against Biratnagar, Lumbini and Chitwan — and batted in all three: 10, 38 and 8. The 38 against Lumbini on 7 November was his best innings of the season, and it came in a 36-run win.

His batting is the secondary currency, and it has held up. Modern T20 keepers must contribute in the lower middle order, rotating strike and finding boundaries against spin — and Patel's 56 runs in three innings, at a strike rate of 94.92, is a working return for a man whose first job is behind the stumps. He has opened the batting in all three matches he has played, which says the captain trusts him with the new ball as well as the gloves.

Season 3 in numbers

Batting: 56 runs in three innings, highest score 38, strike rate 94.92. He has not bowled in the season — keepers rarely do. The table below is current through Match 20.

The numbers are modest, but the context is the job. Patel bats at the top of the order — he has opened in all three of his matches — and his role is to give the innings a start, not to finish it. His 38 against Lumbini took 36 balls, with two fours and two sixes: a measured innings in a match Kathmandu won by 36 runs, built on David Warner's 66 and finished by Sahan Arachchige's unbeaten 34.

There is one wrinkle in the record worth noting honestly. Patel's squad status carries a note: the auction tracker lists a sale, but the auction-day timeline says unsold, and the two have not been reconciled. What is not in doubt is that he is in the side, keeping wicket, and batting — and that his place has survived the presence of an India Test keeper in the squad.

Match 15, 5 November, vs Biratnagar Kings — 10 off 11 balls. His first innings of the season, opening the batting in a chase of 84. Kathmandu needed only three batters — Warner finished it with 44 not out — and won by nine wickets in 9.4 overs. Patel's 10 was a start, not a statement. Match 18, 7 November, vs Lumbini Lions — 38 off 36 balls. His best innings. Opening again, he made 38 with two fours and two sixes, and held the innings together while Warner made 66 at the other end. Kathmandu reached 160 for 8, Lumbini were bowled out for 124, and the Gorkhas won by 36 runs. Match 20, 9 November, vs Chitwan Rhinos — 8 off 12 balls. A shorter stay in a higher-scoring game.
