Sonu Devkota is the only Kathmandu player in this group who has done it all before. He spent Season 2 with the Gorkhas, the club brought him back for Season 3 at the base price of NPR 2 lakh — and five matches in, he is still waiting for his first ball. His name is on the 16-man squad list. The stats file, through five matches, has nothing under it.

The club's decision is the evidence that matters. Kathmandu saw him for a full Season 2 and chose to bring him back — a buy-back, not a first punt. A domestic bowler re-signed at the base price is typically a role-player: a middle-overs operator who bowls to fields, or a new-ball bowler with one standout skill the coaches want to develop. The club's decision to re-sign him says they saw something worth a second season.

His genuine edge is environmental. A Season 2 inside the Gorkhas means he has bowled at Kirtipur in franchise conditions, knows how the surface behaves, and has faced the franchise's batters in the nets. That knowledge is worth more than a debutant's raw talent when the call comes late in a campaign — and in T20 cricket, the call often comes late, through an injury, a match-up, or a match that no longer matters for the table.
