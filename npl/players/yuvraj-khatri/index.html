No wickets in three matches, 43 runs in four overs — the leg-spinner’s numbers are quiet, and the sample is small: two overs against Biratnagar, one against Kathmandu, one against Pokhara. An economy rate of 10.75 is the number that needs watching, but leg-spin is the wicket-taking art in T20 and four overs across three games is not a verdict on anyone.

With the bat, the return is two runs in two balls, twice not out — 1 not out against Janakpur, 1 not out against Pokhara. He was used as an all-round option in his debut Season 2, capable of useful lower-order runs rather than just surviving, and the two not-outs say the innings have not asked him to do more than that yet.

Squad role

Khatri’s value starts with a single word: wrist-spin. His right-arm leg-break turns away from right-handers and into left-handers — the opposite direction to every other spinner in Karnali’s squad — which makes him a matchup weapon captains deploy deliberately, not just a stock bowler.

Leg-spin is the wicket-taking art in T20: it invites the big shot and punishes the mishit, and his teenage fearlessness suits that high-risk role. He is most dangerous through the middle overs, attacking when batters must score. In a spin group crowded with left-arm orthodox and off-break options, his leg-breaks turn the other way — a rarity the Yaks have locked down for a second season.

Retention history

Karnali spent NPR 10 lakh on Khatri at the 2025 auction when he was just 17, then retained him for Season 3, naming him seventh in the seven-player retention list announced on 14 June 2026. Born on 30 September 2007, he is the youngest player on the retained list.

The retention is the franchise betting on the ceiling: a wrist-spinner’s best years are ahead of him, and the Yaks have decided those years will be theirs. Two games are left in the season, and a leg-break bowler with nothing to lose is exactly the kind of player who takes the wickets that change a must-win game.

Remaining fixtures

Karnali sit seventh on four points from five matches, with a net run rate of minus 0.846 — the worst in the league. The full table is on the NPL Points Table, and the remaining fixtures are on the NPL Schedule. Two league games are left: Lumbini Lions on Thursday 12 November and Chitwan Rhinos on Sunday 15 November. Win both and the Yaks reach eight points — enough, probably, unless Biratnagar Kings, who can also reach eight, edge them on net run rate. For Khatri, the run-in is the matchup itself: leg-break against middle orders that must score, in two games Karnali must win.
