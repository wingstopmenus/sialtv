Season 3 summary

Shahab Alam's method is patience. He tosses the ball up, invites the drive, and beats batters in the air as much as off the pitch. On Kirtipur's turning tracks, that style is the first-choice weapon: the surface grips, the ball holds up, and a left-arm spinner who flights it is the bowler the captain turns to when the game needs slowing down.

The numbers are the proof. Three wickets in four matches, 15 overs bowled, 80 runs conceded — an economy rate of 5.33, the best among Kathmandu's regular bowlers, and an average of 26.67. He has taken a wicket in three of his four matches, and the one match in which he did not — the opener against Janakpur, 0 for 18 in three overs — was the match Kathmandu lost by eight wickets.

The template for his bowling is a performance from the Darwin tour: 2 for 17 against the Hobart Hurricanes academy — economical, wicket-taking, and achieved against batters raised on pace. It is the same shape as his Season 3 so far: not the bowler who takes three wickets in a match, but the bowler who never lets the other side get away.

Season 3 in numbers

Batting: 4 not out in one innings. Bowling: 3 wickets in four matches, 15 overs, economy 5.33. The tables below are current through Match 20.

The economy rate is the number that matters. At 5.33 runs an over, Alam concedes more than two runs an over fewer than the captain Karan KC (7.41) and more than one fewer than Santosh Yadav (5.83). In a format where the middle overs decide matches, a spinner who goes at 5.33 while taking a wicket every five overs is the bowler who lets the rest of the attack hunt.

The batting is a footnote: 4 not out off six balls in the opener against Janakpur, when Kathmandu were bowled out for 115 and Alam was unbeaten at the end. He is a right-hand batter, but the bat is not why he is in the side — and in four of the five matches, he has not needed it.

Match by match

Match 5, 29 October, vs Janakpur Bolts — 4 not out off 6 balls, 0 for 18 in 3 overs. The only defeat, and the only match in which Alam did not take a wicket. Kathmandu were bowled out for 115 — Alam unbeaten on 4 at the end — and Janakpur chased 116 with eight wickets to spare. Match 10, 2 November, vs Karnali Yaks — 1 for 31 in 4 overs. His most expensive spell, in the highest-scoring match of Kathmandu's season. Karnali made 180 for 6, and Alam's wicket was one of the six. Kathmandu chased it down to win by four wickets. Match 15, 5 November, vs Biratnagar Kings — 1 for 13 in 4 overs. His best figures, in the most one-sided match of the season. Biratnagar were bowled out for 83, and Alam's four overs cost 13 runs with a wicket. Kathmandu won by nine wickets in 9.4 overs. Match 18, 7 November, vs Lumbini Lions — 1 for 18 in 4 overs. The same shape again: four overs, one wicket, under five an over, in a 36-run win. Lumbini were bowled out for 124 chasing 161. Alam did not bowl in the Chitwan win on 9 November — the captain went with Karan KC, Santosh Yadav and Sahan Arachchige instead.
