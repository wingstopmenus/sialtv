Santosh Yadav — the 19-year-old spinner with six wickets

Santosh Yadav is a 19-year-old all-rounder — left-hand bat, left-arm orthodox spin, born 17 November 2006 — and he has taken 6 wickets in four matches for Kathmandu Gorkhas. His 3 for 11 against Biratnagar Kings earned him the player-of-the-match award.

Last updated: 10 November 2026

Teenage spinner, first-choice bowler

Santosh Yadav's Season 3 has been the breakout of Kathmandu's campaign. He is 19 years old — born 17 November 2006 — and he bowls left-arm orthodox spin, the two-skill package that makes him a genuine all-rounder rather than a bowler who bats. His left-arm spin is the primary weapon: on Kirtipur's turning tracks, a spinner who drifts the ball in and turns it away from right-handers is the middle-overs controller every T20 side needs.

The numbers back it up. Six wickets in four matches, at an average of 11.67 and an economy rate of 5.83 — the second-best average and the second-best economy among Kathmandu's bowlers, behind only Sahan Arachchige's small-sample figures. His best is 3 for 11 against Biratnagar Kings on 5 November, in a match Kathmandu won by nine wickets after bowling Biratnagar out for 83. He was named player of the match.

With the bat, he has been a finisher in miniature: 15 runs in three innings, with two not-outs, at a strike rate of 250.00. His 8 not out against Chitwan Rhinos on 9 November came off two balls — one of them a six — at the end of Kathmandu's 180 for 8. It is a small return, but it is the return of a man who bats at the end of the innings and is expected to score quickly or not at all.

The bowling average of 11.67 is the standout figure. Among Kathmandu's bowlers with more than a couple of matches, nobody else is below 19 — Karan KC's 19.57 is next, and Shahab Alam's 26.67 is after that. A wicket every 12 balls, at under six runs an over, is the profile of a bowler the captain can throw the ball to in any phase and expect control.

The batting average of 15.00 is a quirk of the small sample — two not-outs in three innings — and the strike rate of 250.00 is the product of six balls faced. Neither number means much yet. What means something is that the captain has bowled him in four of the five matches, for 12 overs, and that he has taken a wicket in every one of them.

The Biratnagar match — and the rest

Match 10, 2 November, vs Karnali Yaks — 5 not out off 3 balls, 1 for 8 in 2 overs. His first appearance, and a quiet one with the bat: 5 not out at the end of the chase, as Kathmandu won by four wickets chasing 181. With the ball, 1 for 8 in two overs — economical, and a wicket on debut for the season. Match 15, 5 November, vs Biratnagar Kings — 3 for 11 in 3 overs. The breakout. Biratnagar were bowled out for 83, and Yadav's three wickets for 11 runs were the best figures of the match. He was named player of the match, and Kathmandu won by nine wickets in 9.4 overs. He did not bat — only three batters were needed. Match 18, 7 November, vs Lumbini Lions — 2 off 1 ball, 1 for 32 in 4 overs. His most expensive spell of the season, and his quietest batting day. Lumbini were still bowled out for 124, and Kathmandu won by 36 runs — and even on his worst day with the ball, he took a wicket. Match 20, 9 November, vs Chitwan Rhinos — 8 not out off 2 balls, 1 for 19 in 3 overs. The finisher in miniature: 8 not out off two balls, one of them a six, at the end of Kathmandu's 180 for 8. With the ball, 1 for 19 in a 10-run win. Four matches, four wickets taken — the only Kathmandu bowler with a wicket in every match he has played.

The teenager in the table-toppers' attack

Kathmandu Gorkhas lead the NPL Season 3 points table with 8 points from five matches — four wins, one loss, net run rate +1.168 — and they are already certain of a playoff place. Karan KC captains the side, Monty Desai is head coach, and the spin attack that has carried them is built on two left-armers: Yadav, 19, and Shahab Alam, whose slow left-arm orthodox has taken 3 wickets in four matches at an economy rate of 5.33.

Yadav's action gives the captain a like-for-like alternative to Alam's — and a younger, sharper one, with a wicket in every match. With David Warner gone home and two league matches left — Sudurpaschim Royals on 13 November and Pokhara Avengers on 15 November — the bowling that wins the run-in will be the bowling that has won the season so far.
