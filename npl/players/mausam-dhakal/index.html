The figures are steady rather than spectacular, which is exactly the point of the signing. Dhakal is not the strike bowler — Rijan and Kamal own that job — he is the first local name in an attack built around the overseas trio, the bowler who holds an end while the wickets fall at the other.

Cross-franchise move

The cross-franchise move is the story: two full NPL seasons with Karnali meant Chitwan’s scouts had an extended, unfiltered look — these venues, these batters, the evening conditions that shape Nepali T20 — before spending. At NPR 3 lakh he was Chitwan’s priciest domestic bowling purchase: the franchise paying a premium for the known quantity over the unknown.

His franchise road began with the Karnali Yaks, who paid NPR 10 lakh for him in Category B at the inaugural 2024 auction. Two seasons in league conditions is the credential no auction price can fake — he has bowled in these venues, against these batters, in the conditions that shape Nepali T20, the kind of league-specific knowledge a first-season import spends half a campaign acquiring.

Squad role

At 3 lakh, the most Chitwan paid for any domestic bowler, the expectation is contribution, not development: overs that matter, in phases that matter. He has delivered exactly that so far — four games, four spells, three wickets, and an economy that keeps the captain coming back to him.

The role sits between the strike pair and the control bowlers: not the new-ball burst, not the death-overs squeeze, but the middle-overs holding job that lets the attack breathe. It is the least glamorous seat in the side — and the one the whole structure depends on.

Remaining fixtures

Chitwan are fourth after Match 21, with five points from five games and a net run rate of +0.009. The full table is on the NPL Points Table, and the remaining fixtures are on the NPL Schedule. Two league games are left: Janakpur Bolts on Friday 13 November and Karnali Yaks on Sunday 15 November. Win both and the Rhinos reach nine points — enough to guarantee a playoff place.
