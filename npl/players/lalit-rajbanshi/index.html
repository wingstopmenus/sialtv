Season 3 summary

Lalit Narayan Rajbanshi has taken 4 wickets in six matches in NPL Season 3 for Janakpur Bolts. The retained left-arm spinner, the side’s marquee player, has bowled in every match — but his best figures so far are 1 for 26, and in Match 21 against Sudurpaschim Royals he went wicketless for 24.

Bowler. Retained. Captain: Harmeet Singh. Head coach: Shivnarine Chanderpaul.

Lalit Narayan Rajbanshi is a retained Janakpur Bolts player, a Nepal-capped left-arm orthodox spinner the franchise has trusted in all three NPL seasons. His Season 2 record explains the decision: against Karnali Yaks he bowled three overs, conceded 16 runs and took two wickets — control at 5.33 an over with nine dot balls in eighteen deliveries. Season 2 scorecards also show unbeaten lower-order cameos, including 10 not out from 11 balls.

In Season 3 he is the lead domestic spinner. His banked middle-overs quota frees captain Harmeet Singh to use his own left-arm spin as a tactical weapon rather than a necessity. The squad list spells his name “Lalit Narayan Rajbanshi”; the season stats file lists him as “Lalit Rajbanshi”. The figures on this page are his either way.

The season has been steady without ever catching fire. He took 1 for 38 against Karnali Yaks in the opener, 1 for 30 against Kathmandu Gorkhas, 1 for 26 against Pokhara Avengers, 0 for 30 against Biratnagar Kings, 1 for 31 against Lumbini Lions, and 0 for 24 in three overs against Sudurpaschim Royals in Match 21. With the bat he has 8 runs in four innings, all three of his dismissals avoided — 1, 0 not out, 2 not out and 5 not out.

Season 3 analysis

Four wickets at an economy of 8.14 and an average of 44.75 is a long way from the Season 2 form that earned his retention, when he went at 5.33 an over with nine dots in eighteen balls. The economy is the problem: among Janakpur’s regular bowlers, only James Neesham (10.17) and Abhisekh Tiwari (8.63) have gone for more, and both of them bowl far fewer overs. A lead spinner who concedes more than eight an over is not holding an end — he is the end the opposition targets.

But the role still matters. Rajbanshi has bowled in all six matches, more than any other Janakpur bowler except Harmeet Singh, and his quota is what lets the captain use his own spin as a weapon. The wickets will come if the economy tightens: he took two wickets in three overs in Season 2, and the control that produced nine dot balls in eighteen deliveries is still in there. Friday against Chitwan Rhinos is the last league match, and the side’s marquee spinner has one game left to make Season 3 look like the seasons that earned him the tag.
