The marquee overseas — four wickets at 4.50

Shakib Al Hasan has taken four wickets in NPL Season 3 at an economy of 4.50 an over, the tightest among Pokhara’s frontline bowlers. He has also scored 26 runs in three matches.

All-rounder. Overseas signing. Captain: Kushal Bhurtel. Head coach: Ajantha Mendis.

The numbers behind him are among the finest in the game: 71 Tests, 247 ODIs and 129 T20Is across nearly two decades, with 317 ODI wickets and 7,570 ODI runs. His signature remains the 2019 World Cup, where 606 runs and 11 wickets made it the only 600-run, ten-wicket tournament ever. Pokhara signed him as the centre of their push for a first playoff appearance — four overs of left-arm spin, a flexible left-hand batting option, and a dressing-room standard the squad has lacked.

His season has been three matches, not five: he played the opener against Sudurpaschim Royals, the Janakpur Bolts game and the Lumbini Lions game, then sat out the last two. The bowling has been exactly what was promised — 1 for 24, 3 for 15 and 0 for 6 — while the batting has been quiet: 14, 9 and 3. The side has missed him in the two games he did not play, both of them defeats.

What the numbers mean

The bowling figures are the best in the side by economy: 4 wickets in 10 overs for 45 runs, at 4.50 an over and an average of 11.25. The 3 for 15 against Janakpur Bolts in Match 9 was Pokhara’s best bowling performance of the season to that point, and it came in a game they lost by nine runs — the closest of their four defeats. The 0 for 6 in two overs against Lumbini Lions was the most economical spell by any Pokhara bowler that day.

The batting is the disappointment: 26 runs in three innings at an average of 8.67 and a strike rate of 63.4. A batter of Shakib’s class is expected to do more than 14, 9 and 3 — but he has batted down the order in a side that keeps collapsing around him, and his job was always the ball first. Two must-win matches remain, and Pokhara need both disciplines from their marquee signing now.

Where Pokhara Avengers stand

Shakib’s four wickets at 4.50 an over are the steadiest bowling in the side — Pokhara need that control in two must-win games.

Two matches remain, and both are must-win. Biratnagar Kings on Thursday 12 November at 12:30 PM NST, then Kathmandu Gorkhas on Sunday 15 November at 4:30 PM NST, both at the TU International Cricket Ground in Kirtipur. Two wins take Pokhara to six points, but that is only the start of the maths: Chitwan Rhinos would have to lose both of their remaining matches, and net run rate — currently minus 0.707 — would have to fall Pokhara's way.
