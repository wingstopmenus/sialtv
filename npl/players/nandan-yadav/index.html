The wickets have come steadily: 2 for 13 against Janakpur in the opening win, 1 for 43 against Biratnagar, 1 for 28 against Kathmandu, 1 for 33 against the Royals, and 0 for 41 against Pokhara. Five wickets in five matches, one in every game bar the last. The best figures, 2 for 13, came in the game Karnali won — and the economy rate of 8.70 is the soft part of the record, with the Biratnagar and Pokhara outings both going for more than ten an over.

With the bat, the numbers are small but loud: 17 runs from three innings, with a highest score of 9, at a strike rate of 77.27. The bat is not his job — the new ball is. What the batting does show is the second string: a T20I strike rate of 225, the highest among Nepal’s lower order in his debut year, hints at a hitter waiting for the right evening.

From 11 lakh to a Nepal cap

Nandan Yadav’s rise has been a late-bloomer’s story. The Siraha-born right-arm medium seamer, born on 27 July 2000, was signed by Karnali for NPR 11 lakh in the inaugural 2024 auction on domestic promise — then forced his way into Nepal’s national side in 2025.

He debuted against Qatar in April 2025 and became Nepal’s 42nd ODI player that June, against the Netherlands in Dundee. Nineteen T20 internationals have brought 22 wickets at an average under 20, with best figures of 2 for 18 — new-ball returns that mark him as a genuine international-quality seamer. Retained for Season 3, he is now one of two Nepal internationals leading the Yaks’ pace attack.

Five wickets, and a 225 strike rate

Nandan is a classical right-arm medium seamer in an era of express pace — and that is precisely his strength. He swings the new ball, seams it off early moisture, and attacks the top of off stump with a repeatable action, trusting movement over speed. His 22 T20I wickets at under 20 apiece show the method translates: batters expecting pace-on find lateral movement instead.

The second string is the bat. A T20I strike rate of 225 is not a typo and not a small-sample joke — it is the highest among Nepal’s lower order in his debut year, and it means the Yaks have a number eight or nine who can change a total in an over. Five wickets is the Season 3 return so far; the strike rate is the reason the run-in might ask more of him.

The run-in, through the seamer

Karnali sit seventh on four points from five matches, with a net run rate of minus 0.846 — the worst in the league. The full table is on the NPL Points Table, and the remaining fixtures are on the NPL Schedule. For Nandan, the run-in is new-ball work against two sides that have to win as well — and a late-order hitting chance if the game goes deep.
