Season 3 in numbers

Five matches, 194 runs at an average of 38.80, and a strike rate of 153.97. Bam is the only batter in the league to have passed 190 runs, and his two fifties — 87 against Karnali Yaks and 50 against Janakpur Bolts — are the two biggest innings of Biratnagar’s season.

The 87 came in a losing cause. Chasing 188 against Karnali Yaks, Bam hit 87 from 52 balls and still watched his side lose by four wickets — the story of Biratnagar’s season in one innings. The 50 against Janakpur Bolts came in the two-run win, the closest finish of the season so far, and his 26 in the opener against Chitwan Rhinos was the second-highest score of that innings too.

Only the Kathmandu game went wrong: 6 from 10 balls on the night the Kings were bowled out for 83, their lowest total of the season. Behind the stumps he is the side’s senior gloveman, keeping to Sandeep Lamichhane’s leg-break for a third straight season — the keeping-batting double that lets the Kings field their deep all-rounder stocks.

Top run scorer

Bam’s 194 runs lead the whole league — 23 ahead of Chitwan Rhinos’ Ravi Bopara on 171, and 50 ahead of his own team-mate Andre Russell on 144. No other batter in NPL Season 3 has two fifties and a strike rate above 150.

The record did not come from nowhere. Bam was retained on 16 June 2026 after two seasons as the Kings’ first-choice keeper, and his best league campaign before this one was NPL 2025, when his high score was 72. Across 24 NPL matches he has 383 runs. The international stage came first: a Nepal international with 23 T20I caps, he made his debut against the UAE in February 2022 and announced himself at the 2026 T20 World Cup with an unbeaten 39 from 20 balls against England at Wankhede.

His T20I strike rate sits in the 150s, which marks him as a powerplay aggressor rather than an accumulator — and Season 3 has been the same player, just with more runs. The question for the last two games is whether the rest of the batting can give him the support his numbers deserve.

The pattern is stark: when Bam scores, Biratnagar compete; when he fails, they collapse. His 87 against Karnali was 47 percent of the side’s 186, his 50 against Janakpur came in a 174-run total, and his 25 in the five-over game against Sudurpaschim Royals was the second-highest score of a 105-run innings. The one innings that breaks the pattern is the Kathmandu game — 6 runs, and the Kings managed only 83.

His strike rate of 153.97 is built on the six-hitting: 16 sixes in five innings, four more than any other player in the league, and 12 fours besides. He opens the batting, so the powerplay is where the damage is done — and where Biratnagar’s season is usually decided inside the first six overs.
