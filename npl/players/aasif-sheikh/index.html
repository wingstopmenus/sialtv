Aasif Sheikh — Lumbini Lions wicketkeeper-opener, NPL 2026

The most expensive domestic signing of the July auction, and the new face at the top of the champions’ order. Aasif Sheikh has 61 runs in four innings in NPL Season 3, opening the batting and keeping wicket for Lumbini Lions. His 32 on the opening night set up the seven-wicket win over the Royals.

Last updated: 10 November 2026

The NPR 20 lakh buy

Lumbini spent more than any other team at the July auction — NPR 44 lakh in total — and the biggest single cheque went to Aasif Sheikh: NPR 20 lakh, the most the franchise spent on any player, to bring the wicketkeeper-batter across from the Janakpur Bolts.

It was a signing made for two jobs at once. Sheikh is the ICC Spirit of Cricket Award winner, a man with 69 T20 internationals and 63 one-day internationals behind him, and the owner of a piece of Nepal cricket history: his 58 off 97 balls against India at the 2023 Asia Cup was the first ODI half-century by a Nepali against India. He has 1,646 T20I runs. The Lions bought him to open the batting and keep wicket — to give a settled, title-winning side a specialist opener and the senior voice the top order had been missing.

Season 3 in numbers

Four innings, 61 runs, four fours and four sixes. Sheikh is the third-highest run-scorer for Lumbini this season, behind only captain Rohit Paudel’s 86 and Niroshan Dickwella’s 75 — and his strike rate of 110.91 is the price of the job he does: he faces the new ball, so his runs come in the powerplay, when the field is up and the bowlers are fresh.

The opener was the innings the signing was made for: 32 off 25 balls, with two fours and two sixes, in a chase of 88 that the Lions finished with seven wickets and more than ten overs to spare. Against Janakpur he made 21 off 21 in a total of 149 for 8, the fourth-highest score of the innings, in the 16-run win. The two small scores since — seven against Pokhara, one against Kathmandu — are the pattern of an opener’s season: the starts are there, the conversion into a fifty is not, yet.

The keeper-opener’s job

Sheikh walks into two jobs for the champions: opening the batting and keeping wicket. That is the whole point of the signing — one player, two roles, and the experience to do both under pressure.

The Lions need stable powerplay starts to justify the spend, and the opening partnership has been the side’s quiet problem this season: the top order has given the side starts, but no Lumbini opener has yet made a fifty. What Sheikh brings that no other Lion can is 69 T20Is of reading new-ball bowling — and the knowledge that a keeper who opens sees the pitch twice, once from behind the stumps and once from the crease. With two league games left and the playoffs in sight, the side needs one big innings from him. The two games against Karnali and Biratnagar are the chance.
