Nine wickets at 6.44 apiece, at an economy of 5.80 — the cheapest wickets in the Chitwan attack, and the third-most wickets in the whole league. The spells tell the story: 4 for 26 against Biratnagar in Match 2, 3 for 7 against Pokhara in Match 17, and 2 for 25 against Kathmandu in Match 20. He has taken wickets in every game he has bowled in. Every Chitwan result is on the NPL Results page.

Only two bowlers in the league have more: his own teammate Rijan Dhakal on 10, and Janakpur’s Harmeet Singh on 11. Airee shares third place with Rahul Yadav of Janakpur and Sompal Kami of Karnali, on nine each — and he has played fewer games than both of them. With the bat, nine runs in two not-out innings, at a strike rate of 150.00, is the tail-ender’s bonus.

Match-winning spell

The opener against Biratnagar Kings set the tone for the whole season. Chitwan made 157 for 3, Biratnagar were bowled out for 149, and Airee’s 4 for 26 in four overs was the difference in an eight-run win — the player-of-the-match performance in the Rhinos’ first game of the campaign.

It was the kind of spell his international record promised: stump-to-stump, wicket-taking rather than containing, the burst that takes them in clusters. Biratnagar never recovered from it, and Chitwan’s season began with a win built on their retained seamer.

International record

Ten T20 internationals, fifteen wickets at 14.80, best of 3 for 15. A right-arm medium-fast bowler who attacks the stumps, he is the retained stability around which the auction-bought seamers must fight for places. The international numbers explain a faith the franchise has now renewed twice: a T20I debut against Malaysia in April 2021, an ODI debut against Oman in February 2020, and a wicket-taking record — not a containing one — across ten T20 internationals.

Three straight campaigns in Rhinos colours make him the domestic pace attack’s retained spine: the 10-lakh Category B buy from 2024 whose price the franchise has never had to reconsider. The 14.80 T20I average is the style in numbers — he bowls to take wickets, and at medium-fast pace the margin for the batter is thin.

Remaining fixtures

Chitwan are fourth after Match 21, with five points from five games and a net run rate of +0.009. The full table is on the NPL Points Table, and the remaining fixtures are on the NPL Schedule. Two league games are left: Janakpur Bolts on Friday 13 November and Karnali Yaks on Sunday 15 November. Win both and the Rhinos reach nine points — enough to guarantee a playoff place.

Between them, Airee and Rijan Dhakal have taken 19 of the 33 wickets Chitwan have claimed in the four matches that produced a result.
