Season 3 summary

Dev Shah's profile is a blank in the public record — no batting hand, no bowling style, no listed role. What he has instead is a record: two matches bowled, two wickets taken. In a squad built around three overseas stars and six retained domestic players, the unlisted signing who does a bit of everything is easy to overlook. Shah has made himself hard to overlook.

His first outing came against Karnali Yaks on 2 November, in the highest-scoring match of Kathmandu's season: 1 for 28 in three overs, as the Gorkhas chased 181 to win by four wickets. His second came against Biratnagar Kings on 5 November: 1 for 15 in two overs, in a match Kathmandu won by nine wickets after bowling Biratnagar out for 83. Two different games, two different jobs — and a wicket in each.

He has not batted in the season. Kathmandu's batting has rarely needed its lower order: the top order has done the work, and in the Biratnagar match only three batters were needed at all. Shah's value so far is entirely with the ball, and entirely in the margins — the overs that hold a total together while the strike bowlers rest.

Season 3 in numbers

Two matches, five overs, 43 runs, two wickets. The table below is current through Match 20 — Shah bowled in Matches 10 and 15, and has not bowled since.

The economy rate of 8.60 is the softest part of the record — it is higher than Shahab Alam's 5.33 or Santosh Yadav's 5.83 — but it comes with a caveat. Shah's three overs against Karnali came in a match where 361 runs were scored, and his 1 for 28 was bowled at a side chasing a big total. Against Biratnagar, in a low-scoring game, he went at 7.50 an over and took a wicket. Two matches is a small sample, and the captain has used him in short spells rather than full four-over sets.

What stands out is the strike rate: a wicket every 15 balls. Only Sahan Arachchige, with a wicket every 7.8 balls, and Santosh Yadav, with one every 12, have taken wickets faster for Kathmandu this season. Shah does not bowl the hard overs — Karan KC takes the new ball and the death — but he has taken a wicket every time he has been asked.

Match by match

Match 10, 2 November, vs Karnali Yaks — 1 for 28 in 3 overs. Kathmandu's highest-scoring game of the season. Karnali made 180 for 6, and Shah's three overs cost 28 with one wicket. The Gorkhas chased it down — 181 for 6 in 19.4 overs — with Rashid Khan's 50 and Karan KC's 44, to win by four wickets. Match 15, 5 November, vs Biratnagar Kings — 1 for 15 in 2 overs. A different kind of match entirely. Biratnagar were bowled out for 83 in 18.3 overs, and Shah's two overs were part of the squeeze: 15 runs, one wicket, and a chase that lasted 9.4 overs. Kathmandu won by nine wickets, with David Warner unbeaten on 44.

Since then, Shah has not bowled — in the Lumbini and Chitwan wins, the captain went with Karan KC, Santosh Yadav, Shahab Alam and Sahan Arachchige. His next chance, if it comes, will likely come the same way his first two did: a short spell in the middle overs, with a wicket expected.
