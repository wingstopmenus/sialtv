Season 3 in numbers

The batting is the headline: 52 not out from 29 balls against Janakpur Bolts, 33 from 17 against Chitwan Rhinos, 29 not out from 10 in the five-over game against Sudurpaschim Royals. Two not outs in five innings lift the average to 48.00, and 14 sixes are second only to Lokesh Bam’s 16 in the whole league.

The bowling has been just as important. His 4 for 45 against Janakpur Bolts won that game — and the award — and his 1 for 8 in two overs in the five-over game against the Royals was the cheapest spell of the match. At 10.00 runs an over he is the most expensive of Biratnagar’s regular bowlers, but he bowls the hard overs, and he takes wickets in them.

The Janakpur game was the complete performance: 52 not out from 29 balls to lift Biratnagar to 174 for 3, then 4 for 45 with the ball as the Bolts fell two runs short. The five-over game against the Royals was the other award — 29 not out from 10 balls, then 1 for 8 in two overs as Biratnagar won by 35 runs.

The two defeats that bookend those wins show the other side. Against Karnali he made 27 from 15 and went wicketless for 24 runs; against Kathmandu he made 3 from 5 and bowled a single over for 14. When Russell fires, Biratnagar win. When he doesn’t, they usually don’t.

03 — The overseas star doing both jobs

The overseas star doing both jobs

Russell arrived as the biggest overseas name in the league — and he has played like it. Born on 29 April 1988 in Jamaica, he played for West Indies from 2010 to 2025, winning the T20 World Cup in 2012 and 2016, before announcing his international retirement in July 2025.

The record behind him is enormous: by October 2026 he had played 611 T20 matches, scoring 9,933 runs with two centuries and taking 539 wickets. With Kolkata Knight Riders he won the IPL in 2014 and 2024, and in the 2019 IPL he was Player of the Tournament with a record season strike rate above 205 and 52 sixes.

Biratnagar announced his signing on 18 October 2026 for the third edition of the NPL, joining a squad captained by Sandeep Lamichhane. He bats in the middle order and bowls right-arm fast — the two-skill package that lets the Kings balance a side built around all-rounders.

04 — Where the Kings stand

Where the Kings stand

Russell has been the Kings’ two-way match-winner, with two Player of the Match awards: Biratnagar Kings are sixth after Match 21 — four points from five games, with a net run rate of −0.820. The full table is on the NPL Points Table, and every result is on the NPL Results page.

Russell’s two Player of the Match awards account for both of Biratnagar’s wins — the 35-run win over Sudurpaschim Royals and the two-run win over Janakpur Bolts. Two more wins, against Pokhara Avengers and Lumbini Lions, would take the Kings to 8 points and almost certainly into the playoffs, and both would almost certainly need a third big game from the West Indian.
