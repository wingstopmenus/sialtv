One wicket in two matches is the whole of the bowling return so far: 1 for 32 in four overs against the Royals in Match 16, and 0 for 20 in a single over against Pokhara in Match 19. Five overs, 52 runs, an economy rate of 10.40 — the numbers are thin because the sample is thin, and the sample is thin because the left-arm overs have gone to Mark Watt first.

With the bat, the return is a single innings: 13 off 8 balls against Pokhara, including a six — a strike rate of 162.50 and the only lower-order hitting Karnali got in their heaviest defeat of the season. A left-hand batter down the order is a point of difference on its own: most late-order batters are right-handers, so his stance alone changes bowling plans.

Price drop

Surya Tamang’s move to Karnali is one of the auction’s quieter stories — and one of its most telling. The left-arm orthodox spinner, born on 30 September 2001, was a Category A pick at the 2025 auction, bought by Biratnagar Kings for NPR 10 lakh. Released after Season 2, he went back into the pool and Karnali signed him this time for NPR 2 lakh — a fifth of his previous price.

That kind of price drop can mean many things: a form dip, a franchise misreading the market, or simply the volatility of T20 auctions. What it guarantees is motivation. A left-hand batter down the order adds a point of difference, and he now competes directly with Bipin Sharma for the domestic left-arm overs.

Bowling style

Tamang is a left-arm orthodox spinner in the classical mould — flight, dip and turn rather than flat dart-throwing. His bowling is built for the middle overs, where his arc invites the drive and his turn punishes it. The 10-lakh Category A price tag Biratnagar paid in 2025 suggests the talent was real enough to excite professional scouts; the question for Season 3 is whether the dip was form or fit.

At 24, he is young enough for this to be a blip rather than a verdict. Kirtipur conditions should suit him: a spinner who flights the ball benefits most from surfaces that grip, and the Yaks’ home ground is exactly that kind of surface. Two games are left to show it.

Remaining fixtures

Karnali sit seventh on four points from five matches, with a net run rate of minus 0.846 — the worst in the league. The full table is on the NPL Points Table, and the remaining fixtures are on the NPL Schedule. Two league games are left: Lumbini Lions on Thursday 12 November and Chitwan Rhinos on Sunday 15 November. Win both and the Yaks reach eight points — enough, probably, unless Biratnagar Kings, who can also reach eight, edge them on net run rate. For Tamang, the run-in is the contest itself: the domestic left-arm overs, against Bipin Sharma, with two must-win games to settle it.
