Sachin Sharma has no batting or bowling entry in the Season 3 stats file — he has not played in any of Karnali’s five matches through Match 21 (10 November 2026). There is nothing to pad that with: he is a batter who has not batted yet, and the page will say so until he does.

What the role demands at NPL level is specific — the ability to rotate strike against spin through the middle overs, find boundaries when the field spreads, and hold a tempo rather than swinging from ball one. Coming through an open talent hunt rather than an age-group production line suggests a self-made game, built on local cricket rather than academy polish. Whether he is a top-order anchor or a middle-order dasher will only become clear when he plays; for now, his listed specialism is simple and non-negotiable: batting.

Three hundred hopefuls, one signing

Sharma is a product of the Yaks’ own pre-season talent hunt in Surkhet and Salyan — the scouting drive in his home province that drew more than 300 hopefuls across the two venues and also unearthed fellow 2-lakh signing Diwan Pun. That pathway is the whole story of his signing: spotted locally, tested by the franchise’s own coaches, and bought at the lowest price bracket as a development pick.

The talent-hunt pathway itself is a data point. The Yaks’ coaches watched him bat in Surkhet and Salyan and chose him from more than 300 hopefuls, which means they saw a repeatable technique rather than one good day. Development picks are bought on what the coaches saw in the nets, not on what the databases say.

What a development pick is for

A 2-lakh signing is the classic low-cost, high-upside auction gamble, and Sharma arrives at franchise level with everything to prove and nothing to lose. Squad listings class him as a pure batting option, and the franchise’s sixth-place finish last season — a finish that exposed the batting as the squad’s weak link — is the reason a development batter was worth a slot at all.

The job, when the chance comes, is the one every young batter faces at this level: score runs, quickly and consistently, against bowling he has never faced before. Until then, his value is in the nets and in the dressing room, learning a franchise system from the inside.

The run-in, and the wait

Karnali sit seventh on four points from five matches, with a net run rate of minus 0.846 — the worst in the league. The full table is on the NPL Points Table, and the remaining fixtures are on the NPL Schedule. Two league games are left: Lumbini Lions on Thursday 12 November and Chitwan Rhinos on Sunday 15 November. Win both and the Yaks reach eight points — enough, probably, unless Biratnagar Kings, who can also reach eight, edge them on net run rate. For Sharma, the run-in is the last window of the season: a debut here would be the payoff for a talent-hunt signing, and the Yaks have nothing to lose by trying a fresh batter.
