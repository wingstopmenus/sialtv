Season 3 in numbers

Two innings, 53 runs, five fours, no sixes — and a strike rate of 108.16 that says more about patience than power. Jora has batted twice in five games, which is the whole story of his season: the talent is not in question, the opportunity has been.

The average of 26.50 is the fifth-best among Lumbini batters this season, and his 53 runs put him fifth on the team’s run chart despite playing only two of the five games.

International record

Jora was retained in June 2026, and his record explains why a side defending a title keeps a batter who has played only twice: he holds the distinction of being the youngest player to score a T20I half-century, and has since played more than 40 T20 internationals with over 500 runs.

His latest statement came at the 2026 Asian Games, where he finished as the third-highest run-scorer of the tournament with 91 runs in two innings, including an unbeaten 57 off 32 balls against Japan. His NPL tallies — 108 runs in 2024, 115 in 2025 — are modest, and that is the gap in his record: the international promise has not yet turned into franchise returns. Season 3, with two games left, is the latest chance to close it.

Middle-order race

Jora is competing for the number five or six slot in the middle order, and the race is the reason he has played only twice. The Lions’ top order has been built around Rohit Paudel, Aasif Sheikh, Niroshan Dickwella and D’Arcy Short, and the middle order has had room for only one of the retained batters at a time.

What the 44 against Kathmandu did was put him first in the queue. No other Lumbini batter has held an innings together this season the way he did that night, and with Karnali and Biratnagar to come — two sides the Lions are expected to beat — the captain has every reason to give him the longer run he has been waiting for. The playoffs, if Lumbini get there, will need exactly that kind of innings.

The 44 against Kathmandu was the highest score by a Lumbini batter that night — the next best was 16 — in a chase of 161 that ended at 124 all out. Jora batted 45 balls for it, three fours and no sixes, holding one end while the rest of the order went around him: the captain made a two-ball duck, Aasif Sheikh made one, Dilip Nath made nought. It was the innings that showed what he is for. Four days earlier, against Janakpur, he had shown the other side of it: nine off four balls, two fours, in a total of 149 for 8 that the Lions defended by 16 runs.
