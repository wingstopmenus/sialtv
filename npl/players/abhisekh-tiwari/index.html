Season 3 summary

Abhisekh Tiwari has scored 51 runs and taken 4 wickets in NPL Season 3 for Janakpur Bolts. The 19-year-old all-rounder, the youngest auction buy in the squad, has played five of the side’s six matches — and his unbeaten 24 off 10 balls against Pokhara Avengers came in a nine-run win.

All-rounder. Auction — NPR 5 lakh. Captain: Harmeet Singh. Head coach: Shivnarine Chanderpaul.

Abhisekh Tiwari was signed by Janakpur Bolts for NPR 5 lakh at the Season 3 mini-auction in Kathmandu on 6 July 2026, listed as an allrounder. He was one of 52 players picked from 155 registered, and his price places him in the mid-range of the club’s auction spending. A right-hand batter and right-arm medium-fast bowler born in Mahottari on 6 September 2007, he is 19 — the youngest auction buy in the squad.

His pedigree is serious: he led Nepal’s Under-16s at the 2023 ACC U16 East Zone Cup and took a five-wicket haul against Malaysia in the final, and his one-day record shows 14 wickets at 15.00 including 5 for 7. The role label says the Bolts expect overs and runs from him — and unlike most of the domestic buys, he has actually been given both.

His season has been bits and pieces, which is what a 19-year-old’s first season usually is. He made 20 off 17 against Karnali Yaks, 24 not out off 10 against Pokhara Avengers, 5 not out off 2 against Biratnagar Kings, 0 off 4 against Lumbini Lions, and 2 off 6 against Sudurpaschim Royals in Match 21. With the ball he has 4 wickets: 2 for 38 against Biratnagar Kings, 1 for 9 against Lumbini Lions, and 1 for 7 against Sudurpaschim Royals.

Season 3 analysis

Fifty-one runs at a strike rate of 130.77, with two not-outs in five innings, and four wickets at an economy of 8.63 — for a 19-year-old in his first NPL season, that is a return the club can build on. The standout is the 24 not out off 10 against Pokhara Avengers: Janakpur won that match by nine runs, and Tiwari’s late hitting was the difference between a total to defend and a total to chase down.

The bowling is the work in progress. Four wickets have cost 69 runs in eight overs, and the 2 for 38 against Biratnagar Kings — his longest spell — shows what happens when batters line him up. But he trains alongside James Neesham, a 349-match T20 seamer, and the club clearly sees him as a long campaign rather than a one-season pick. If Janakpur reach Friday’s shootout against Chitwan Rhinos, Tiwari is the kind of player who either wins it late or learns from losing it — either way, the season has already been worth it for him.
