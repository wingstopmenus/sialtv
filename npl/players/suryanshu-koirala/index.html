Suryanshu Koirala — the batting signing still waiting for a hit

Suryanshu Koirala is a Nepali batter bought by Kathmandu Gorkhas at the base price of NPR 2 lakh for NPL Season 3. Five matches into the season, he has no batting or bowling entry in the stats file — and his first innings has not come yet.

Last updated: 10 November 2026

A development pick, waiting for a debut

Suryanshu Koirala is the youngest kind of signing in franchise cricket: a base-price batter bought on what the scouts saw, not on what the public record shows. He was picked up by Kathmandu Gorkhas for NPR 2 lakh, listed as a batter with no batting hand and no domestic record in the public file — and five matches into Season 3, the record still shows nothing: no runs, no wickets, no overs.

That is the profile of a development pick. A base-price batting signing in a squad with Warner, Bharat and Arachchige is a player the talent scouts rated on domestic performances the public record does not yet show. What his game must offer is adaptability: the modern domestic batter needs a powerplay game and a middle-overs game, strike rotation against spin and boundary options against pace.

Koirala's wait is the same wait as the other squad batters on the edge of the side — and it has been a long one, because Kathmandu's batting has barely changed. The top order has been Warner, Sahil Patel and Rashid Khan, the middle order Karan KC and Sahan Arachchige, and the lower order has rarely been needed. In the Biratnagar win, only three batters were required at all. When a side wins four out of five, the XI does not change.

What a base-price batter is bought to become

Koirala is listed as a batter, and no verified batting hand or domestic record sits in the public file alongside the listing. That is not unusual for a base-price domestic signing: the auction list carries the role, and the detail lives in the franchise's scouting notes rather than in published profiles.

What the listing does tell us is the shape of the job. A development pick in a 16-man squad is bought for what he might become, not only for what he is. He trains daily alongside David Warner — or did, until Warner flew home on 10 November — and alongside Karan KC and Rashid Khan, two of the most productive batters in the side. That education is the point: a season in a winning dressing room, facing franchise bowlers in the nets, learning the tempo of the format.

The comparison inside the squad is Bhim Sharki, a retained domestic batter who top-scored in the opener with 33 and has since played a supporting role. Sharki's path shows the shape of the job — one substantial innings when the side needs it, then the patience to wait for the next one. Koirala's first innings, if it comes, will likely come the same way: a match where the captain wants a fresh batter, or a game that no longer matters for the table.

The side he is waiting to join

The batting order Koirala is trying to break into has barely changed all season. David Warner opened until he flew home on 10 November; Sahil Patel has opened in the last three matches; Rashid Khan and Karan KC bat in the middle. Kathmandu top the table with 8 points from five matches, four wins and one loss, and a side that wins does not change its batting.

Warner's departure is the one thing that changes the equation for a waiting batter. Kathmandu lose their opener and their biggest name, and the runs have to come from elsewhere in the run-in: Sudurpaschim Royals on Friday 13 November, Pokhara Avengers on Sunday 15 November, then the playoffs. Frequently asked questions

What is Suryanshu Koirala's role for Kathmandu Gorkhas?

He is listed as a batter. No verified batting hand or domestic record sits in the public file — he was bought at the base price of NPR 2 lakh in the auction as a batting option for the Season 3 squad, and is best described as a development pick.

Has Suryanshu Koirala played in NPL Season 3?

No. Through five matches the Season 3 stats file has no batting or bowling entry for him at all. He is a verified member of the 16-man squad, and the run-in — Royals on 13 November, Pokhara on 15 November, then the playoffs — is still ahead of him.

How much did Kathmandu pay for Suryanshu Koirala?

NPR 2 lakh — the base price for domestic signings at the Season 3 auction. He was one of several Kathmandu players bought there, alongside Dev Shah, Dayanand Mandal, Krishna Paudel and Sonu Devkota.
