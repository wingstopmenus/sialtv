The runs have come in small pieces: 12 against Janakpur, an unbeaten 28 against Biratnagar, 20 against Kathmandu, 3 against the Royals, 2 against Pokhara. The 28 not out was his best contribution — made in the chase that Karnali won by four wickets — and the 20 against Kathmandu came in the game the Yaks lost by four wickets. A strike rate of 130.00 says the intent is there; the scores say the innings have not gone long enough.

With the ball, the right-arm medium has been a sixth-bowler option: five overs in three matches, no wickets, 67 runs. The 0 for 14 against Janakpur and 0 for 23 against Kathmandu were tidy enough; the 0 for 30 against Pokhara was not. Karnali’s frontline pace comes from Sompal Kami and Nandan Yadav, and Gulshan’s overs are the change-up. The batting is where the side needs him, and two games are left to deliver it.

Career highlight

Gulshan became the youngest cricketer ever to play ODI cricket for Nepal when he debuted at 15, and he has been defying his age ever since. As a teenager he took a five-wicket haul and scored a fifty in the same ODI — a genuine all-round landmark that remains the signature performance of his young career.

His T20I debut followed against the Philippines in February 2022, and an ODI best of 67 not out underlines that the batting is no afterthought. Karnali made him their maximum-bracket buy in the inaugural 2024 auction and retained him for Season 3. Born on 17 February 2006, he is still only 20 — one of two national-team regulars, alongside captain Sompal Kami, anchoring the retained core.

Season 3 summary

Sixty-five runs and no wickets is a quiet season for a player of Gulshan’s standing, and the honest read is that the big innings has not arrived. He has batted in all five matches and bowled in three, so the opportunity has been there — the scores of 12, 28 not out, 20, 3 and 2 tell their own story.

The counterweight is the 28 not out in the Biratnagar chase, the one innings that finished a game. Lumbini Lions on Thursday and Chitwan Rhinos on Sunday are the two chances left to turn a quiet season into a loud finish, and the Yaks need exactly that kind of innings from a national-team regular.

Remaining fixtures

Karnali sit seventh on four points from five matches, with a net run rate of minus 0.846 — the worst in the league. The full table is on the NPL Points Table, and the remaining fixtures are on the NPL Schedule. Two league games are left: Lumbini Lions on Thursday 12 November and Chitwan Rhinos on Sunday 15 November. Win both and the Yaks reach eight points — enough, probably, unless Biratnagar Kings, who can also reach eight, edge them on net run rate. For Gulshan, the run-in is the stage the season has been building towards: a national-team all-rounder, two games, and a side that needs his bat to fire.
