Season 3 in numbers

Season 3 numbers below cover the league through Match 21 (10 November 2026). Karnali’s last game was Match 19, on 9 November, so these figures are complete for the Yaks.

Shubh Kansakar has no batting or bowling entry in the Season 3 stats file — he has not played in any of Karnali’s five matches through Match 21 (10 November 2026). That is a stark line for the franchise’s most expensive purchase, and it is worth stating plainly: the 10-lakh buy has not batted yet.

The context is the batting itself. Karnali’s top order has leaned on Ariful Islam’s 143 and David Miller’s 100 in two games, and the middle order has been quiet — Pawan Sarraf’s 140 is the steadiest return, and nobody else in the verified squad has passed 100. The brief Kansakar was bought for has not gone away. Two games are left for the investment to show a return.

Auction value

Kansakar alone accounted for nearly two-fifths of the Yaks’ NPR 26 lakh auction spend — a financial statement of intent after a sixth-place finish exposed the batting as the squad’s weak link. A Category B batter bought for 10 lakh is expected to bat in the top four and face the most balls in the innings.

That price tag carries its own pressure: in auction cricket, the most expensive buy is expected to be the most important player. Karnali did not spend two-fifths of their purse on a slogger. They bought someone they believe can bat long — and build. The runs have not come yet because the games have not come yet.

Squad role

What the role demands in the NPL is specific: sound technique against the new ball early, rapid scoring against spin through the middle overs, rotating strike from ball one and converting starts into match-defining scores. Without public numbers to dissect, the honest read is role-based — the franchise sees a top-order anchor.

The pressure cuts both ways. A 10-lakh buy who does not play invites questions about the auction itself; a 10-lakh buy who plays and scores settles them in an evening. The run-in is where that question gets answered, one way or the other.

Remaining fixtures

Karnali sit seventh on four points from five matches, with a net run rate of minus 0.846 — the worst in the league. The full table is on the NPL Points Table, and the remaining fixtures are on the NPL Schedule. Two league games are left: Lumbini Lions on Thursday 12 November and Chitwan Rhinos on Sunday 15 November. Win both and the Yaks reach eight points — enough, probably, unless Biratnagar Kings, who can also reach eight, edge them on net run rate. For Kansakar, the run-in is the last window of the season — and the most expensive buy in the squad is exactly the kind of player a side turns to when two must-win games arrive.
