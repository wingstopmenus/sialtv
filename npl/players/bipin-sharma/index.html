Thirteen wickets at sixteen

In the inaugural 2024 season, Bipin was Karnali’s Local Icon Player at NPR 1 lakh — and he repaid the faith by finishing as the franchise’s leading wicket-taker with 13 scalps, carrying the Yaks to the playoffs. The slow left-arm orthodox spinner had announced himself as one of the NPL’s genuine homegrown finds before he could legally vote.

His 13-wicket debut season showed where he is most dangerous — through the middle overs at Kirtipur, where surfaces can grip and his changes of pace turn dot balls into scoreboard pressure. He is also a natural powerplay option, left-arm spin being a proven T20 weapon against right-hand-dominant top orders. A right-handed bat down the order completes a package that was, at 16, already a franchise cornerstone.

The left-arm contest

Karnali released Bipin in June 2026, then paid NPR 5 lakh — five times his original fee — to buy him back at the Season 3 auction. Born on 25 October 2008, he is still only 17, returning to a squad that now also holds Scotland’s Mark Watt and fellow domestic left-armer Surya Tamang.

The contest is real: Watt has five wickets in five matches, Tamang has played twice, and Bipin has not played yet. The franchise believes in the talent — the rebid says so — and now wants to see who wins the left-arm overs. Two games are left, and a 17-year-old who led an attack at 16 does not stay on the bench by accident.

The run-in, and the wait

Karnali sit seventh on four points from five matches, with a net run rate of minus 0.846 — the worst in the league. The full table is on the NPL Points Table, and the remaining fixtures are on the NPL Schedule. For Bipin, the run-in is the last window of the season — and the left-arm contest with Surya Tamang is exactly the kind of selection call a must-win game forces.
