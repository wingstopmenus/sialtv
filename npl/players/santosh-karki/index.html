Wicketkeeper. Auction — NPR 3 lakh. Captain: Harmeet Singh. Head coach: Shivnarine Chanderpaul.

Santosh Karki was signed by Janakpur Bolts for NPR 3 lakh at the Season 3 mini-auction in Kathmandu on 6 July 2026, listed as a wicketkeeper. He was one of 52 players picked from 155 registered, and the only keeper among the club’s auction buys: a specialist acquisition in a squad that needed the position filled. A right-hand batter who also bowls right-arm offbreak, he came through Nepal’s Under-19s.

He was born in Parsa on 20 July 2003. His recent T20 batting — 143 runs at a strike rate of 102.14 — marks him as a grafter rather than a dasher: the kind of keeper-batter who holds an end while others hit around him.

His Season 3 campaign is a straight duel: his gloves against Anil Kumar Sah’s legacy. Sah captained this franchise to its first title and is also waiting for a game, so the keeping job has gone to neither of them so far. If Karki wins the gloves for the last league match against Chitwan Rhinos on Friday, he plays every ball of it — a shootout is no place to blood a keeper, which is exactly why the decision matters.

Squad depth

A side that has been bowled out for 93 and has crossed 150 only twice in six matches does not usually leave a specialist keeper-batter unused — but Janakpur’s XI has been settled from the start, and the keeping duties have been covered without him. The case for Karki is simple: he was bought to fill a position, the position is his by trade, and the side’s batting has been short of runs all season.

The case against is that Friday is a must-win game, and a debutant keeper in a shootout is a risk. What counts in his favour is the price of the alternative: leaving him out again means Janakpur end the season having spent NPR 3 lakh on a specialist they never used. His recent T20 record says he can bat — 143 runs at just over a run a ball — and if the top order fails again on Friday, the side will wish it had another batter who keeps.
