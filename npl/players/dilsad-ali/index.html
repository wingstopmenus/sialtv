Season 3 in numbers

No matches, no runs, no wickets. Ali has not played in NPL Season 3 so far — and with Bam keeping in all five games, the second-choice keeper’s chance depends on an injury, a rest, or a dead rubber.

That is the reality of the second-choice keeper’s job. The role exists so the side is covered if the first-choice keeper is unavailable, and most seasons the cover is never needed. Ali cost NPR 5 lakh at the July auction — joint-second among the Kings’ domestic picks, level with Naren Saud and behind only Rupesh Singh’s 15 lakh — which says the franchise rates him as more than a name on a list.

His record below the NPL level is age-group cricket and domestic one-day cricket: national Under-19 selection, a place in the 2024 Asia Cup qualification squad and the 2023 ACC Emerging Teams Asia Cup squad, and a 2023 Prime Minister Cup campaign for Madhesh Province. The numbers are thin; the selection is the evidence.

Match by match

Lokesh Bam has kept wicket in all five games — and Bam is also the league’s top run-scorer with 194 runs, so there is no scenario in which the captain rests him for a keeper swap. The Kings have had no reason to rotate the gloves, and Ali has had no game.

The last two league games are the only realistic window. If the Kings win on Thursday and qualify before Saturday, the final game against Lumbini Lions could be a dead rubber — the one match in which a second-choice keeper might get a game.

Player profile

Ali is a right-handed wicketkeeper-batter from Birgunj, Parsa, bought by Biratnagar Kings for NPR 5 lakh at the July auction. He came through Nepal’s age-group system and was named in the national Under-19 setup before moving into domestic one-day cricket.

The path ran through the youth system: national Under-19 selection, then a place in the 2024 Asia Cup qualification squad and the 2023 ACC Emerging Teams Asia Cup squad, and a 2023 Prime Minister Cup campaign for Madhesh Province. Each step was a vote of confidence from the selectors.

As a keeper he is the cover for Bam, and as a batter he is a right-handed option in a squad whose senior batters are mostly right-handed too. His value is depth: the franchise paid 5 lakh for a keeper who can step in without the side changing its shape.
