It is the kind of spell that says the bowler did his job while the game went the other way — and it is why the captain keeps coming back to him.

Auction value

In a squad coming off a two-win Season 2, every retained name is expected to justify the faith, and Kumar’s three-season survival says the franchise keeps finding a use for him. The defined job without fuss is itself the credential: reliability, three years running.

In a fifteen-player squad with only three overseas signings, the domestics who survive every rebuild are rarely passengers — they are the flexible pieces, the players who train at match intensity, cover multiple selection scenarios, and step into the XI without the combination needing surgery.

Notable spell

Against the Royals on 2 November, Chitwan made 129 for 6 and watched it chased down with 20 balls to spare. Kumar’s 3 for 28 in four overs was the one part of the defence that worked — three wickets in a game where the rest of the attack managed two between them. It was the spell that kept a one-sided chase from becoming a rout.

Left-arm orthodox is the angle he brings: the ball turning away from right-hand batters, the dot-ball squeeze that builds pressure for the quicks at the other end. It is not a glamorous skill, but in a side whose strike bowlers do the headline work, it is the support act that makes the headlines possible.
