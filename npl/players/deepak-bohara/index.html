Rescue innings

The 42 from 36 was not a blaze but a rescue, played at barely above a run a ball while the innings was falling apart: timing and placement over power, the innings rebuilt in singles while the required rate stayed manageable. In at 29 for 2 chasing 167 against Karnali Yaks in Season 2, Bohara did the job the scoreboard rarely celebrates — he stopped the collapse.

It is the innings the franchise keeps paying for. Three squad-building exercises, one conclusion: few domestic players survive every rebuild a franchise runs, and Bohara has survived all of Chitwan’s.

Career journey

Bohara’s game is the middle-order stabiliser’s craft, and the Karnali innings is the whole syllabus: in at 29 for 2, he played 36 balls for 42 — strike rotation over boundary-hunting, the innings rebuilt while the required rate stayed manageable. In T20, the stabiliser is the least glamorous and most structurally important batting role: he is the reason collapses stop at two wickets instead of six, the player who lets the power-hitters bat around him.

That skill set shapes his tempo and his method. He is not the batter you pick for the powerplay fireworks; he is the batter you pick for the morning after the fireworks fail. The franchise has now paid for that skill three times — 5 lakh in 2024, then two retentions.
