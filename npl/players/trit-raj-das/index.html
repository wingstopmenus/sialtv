Season 3 summary

Trit Raj Das has scored 112 runs in six matches in NPL Season 3 — the most by any batter in Janakpur Bolts’ verified squad. The NPR 15 lakh auction buy, the club’s most expensive domestic signing, made 61 against Pokhara Avengers and top-scored with 21 in the Match 21 defeat to Sudurpaschim Royals.

Batter. Auction — NPR 15 lakh. Captain: Harmeet Singh. Head coach: Shivnarine Chanderpaul.

Trit Raj Das was Janakpur Bolts’ most expensive domestic auction buy for Season 3: NPR 15 lakh, Category A, listed as a batter. He was one of 52 players picked from 155 registered at the Season 3 mini-auction in Kathmandu on 6 July 2026. A right-hand batter who bowls right-arm medium, he represents Nepal A and plays domestic cricket for the Army club — in March 2026 he fired Army to a seven-wicket win over Gandaki alongside Binod Bhandari and Bhim Sharki. He was born in Kalaiya on 18 October 2002.

The price was a statement: the franchise wanted this batter specifically, and it leaves no middle ground — he was bought for runs, immediately. He projects as an opener or No. 3, the right-handed anchor who lets the overseas hitters bat around a stable base rather than rebuild after early wickets.

The runs have come in two good innings and four quiet ones. He made 1 off 3 against Karnali Yaks, 5 off 5 against Kathmandu Gorkhas, then 61 off 55 against Pokhara Avengers — his only fifty, in the nine-run win — followed by 6 off 9 against Biratnagar Kings, 18 off 18 against Lumbini Lions, and 21 off 29 against Sudurpaschim Royals in Match 21, when he top-scored in a total of 93.

Season 3 analysis

One hundred and twelve runs at an average of 18.67 and a strike rate of 94.12 is not what NPR 15 lakh buys on paper — but the shape of it matters more than the total. The 61 against Pokhara was the innings that won Janakpur one of their two matches: he held the top of the order for 55 balls while the overseas hitters came and went. And the 21 in Match 21, slow as it was, was still the top score in a side bowled out for 93. When Janakpur bat well, Das is the reason; when they collapse, he is usually the last man standing.

The problem is the strike rate. At 94.12 he is the slowest of Janakpur’s regular batters — Moeen Ali strikes at 104.85, Harmeet Singh at 130 — and in a side that has crossed 150 only twice, an anchor who scores at under a run a ball puts pressure on everyone else to hit. The counter is that Janakpur’s collapses have come when the anchor goes early: his 1 and 5 in the first two matches were followed by totals of 113 and 116. Friday against Chitwan Rhinos needs the Pokhara version of Das, not the Karnali one.
