Season 3 summary

Aaditya Mahata has scored 40 runs and taken 1 wicket in three matches in NPL Season 3 for Janakpur Bolts. The retained slow left-arm spinner made an unbeaten 30 off 24 balls against Lumbini Lions, and took his first wicket of the season against Sudurpaschim Royals in Match 21.

Bowler. Retained. Captain: Harmeet Singh. Head coach: Shivnarine Chanderpaul.

Aaditya Mahata is a retained Janakpur Bolts player for NPL Season 3, a slow left-arm orthodox spinner who bats left-handed in the lower order. He entered the franchise at the Season 2 auction and earned his retention the hard way: against Karnali Yaks he took two wickets in a single over, both batters bowled — the dismissals credited “b Aaditya Mahata” — to announce himself as a genuine wicket-taking spinner.

Season 2 scorecards show both halves of his listed role in action: he was picked as a bowler in the XI against Karnali and batted at No. 6 against Sudurpaschim Royals, striking 15 from 13 balls. He fits a deliberate spin strategy under new captain Harmeet Singh, who is himself a left-arm spinner.

In Season 3 he is the high-upside second left-arm option behind Lalit Rajbanshi: the bowler the captain turns to when the match-up demands a second angle or Rajbanshi needs a rest across a long tournament. His season so far has been three games: 0 off 1 against Karnali Yaks in the opener, then an unbeaten 30 off 24 against Lumbini Lions — his highest score of the season — and 10 off 11 against Sudurpaschim Royals in Match 21, where he also took 1 for 19 in three overs, his first wicket of the season.

Season 3 analysis

Forty runs at an average of 20 and a strike rate of 111.11, with one wicket at an economy of 7.00, is a thin return for three games — but the 30 not out against Lumbini Lions is the innings that matters. Janakpur made 133 for 8 in that match and lost by 16 runs; Mahata’s unbeaten 30 off 24 was the highest score from the lower order and the reason the total got as far as it did. A spinner who can bat at No. 6 and hold an end is worth more than his bowling figures say.

The bowling is the open question. One wicket in five overs is not the return of a bowler who took two wickets in a single over to earn his retention — but he has bowled only five overs in three matches, and one of them, the 1 for 19 in Match 21, came against the side that bowled Janakpur out for 93. The role is clear: second left-arm option, used when the match-up demands it. Friday against Chitwan Rhinos is a shootout on a ground where spinners have done the damage all season, and a second left-armer who can also bat is exactly the kind of player a captain wants in a must-win game.
