Season 3 in numbers

Four matches, 86 runs at an average of 43, and two wickets at under six an over. Paudel is the only Lion contributing in both disciplines every game, and his numbers show it: no other Lumbini batter has passed 80 runs this season, and no other captain in the league is doing this much with bat and ball at once.

There is no fifty yet — the 47 not out against Pokhara is the closest he has come — but two not outs in four innings lift the average, and a strike rate of 128.36 is the best of any Lumbini batter to have faced more than 30 balls this season. With the ball, his one for two in a single over against Pokhara is the cheapest spell of his season.

Captaincy

Paudel is Nepal’s national captain, the man who led the country at the 2024 ICC Men’s T20 World Cup, and Nepal’s all-time leading ODI run-scorer with more than 2,200 runs. Lumbini kept him as their marquee player before Season 3, and the 2025 title run explains why.

In that campaign he finished as the franchise’s leading run-scorer with 276 runs and took 10 wickets — and then, in the final against the Royals, produced the spell of the tournament: three for 12 in four overs, including a hat-trick, as Sudurpaschim were bowled out for 85. He bats at three or four and bowls his off-break through the middle overs, which gives the Lions a third spin option alongside Sher Malla and Abhishesh Gautam and lets the captain attack from both ends without weakening the batting. That balance is the reason the title defence is built around him, and the reason the side looks to him first whenever a game drifts.

The 47 not out against Pokhara was the knock that defined his season so far. Chasing 98, the Lions were two down early; Paudel walked in, hit three fours and three sixes, and finished the game with more than eight overs to spare. A week later against Janakpur he made 34 in a total of 149 for 8 — the highest score of that match for Lumbini — and was named Player of the Match as the Lions won by 16 runs. The one blank was the Kathmandu game, a two-ball duck on the night the whole batting order failed and Lumbini were bowled out for 124. The opening night told the other side of his game: five not out to finish a chase of 88, and one for 10 with the ball as the Royals were kept to 87.
