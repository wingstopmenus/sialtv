Scouting discovery

Pun’s signing starts with the talent hunt. Karnali ran a pre-season scouting drive in Surkhet and Salyan that drew more than 300 hopefuls, and Pun was one of two players it produced — the other is Sachin Sharma. That pathway matters: he was watched by the franchise’s own coaches, in his home province, before the auction ever started.

He has bowled for Karnali Province in the PM Cup, the domestic 50-over competition. Fifty-over cricket demands longer spells and repeated discipline, the exact temperament that translates into economical T20 middle overs. It is a thin public record, but it is a real one — and it is the record the Yaks bought.

Squad role

Pun is listed as an all-rounder, but his primary identity is with the ball: a bowler who can bat a bit. His calling card is economical middle-overs bowling — the unsung T20 discipline of landing six balls in the same area and letting scoreboard pressure do the damage.

He does not have the headline variations of a death-overs specialist, and at this stage of his career that is fine. His job is the quiet overs between the strike bowlers’ spells, the 7th-to-14th-over phase where games drift. With the bat he is a capable lower-order contributor rather than a finisher, useful for eking out ten or fifteen runs when the tail needs stretching.
