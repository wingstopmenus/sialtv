Mayan Yadav is a retained Janakpur Bolts player for NPL Season 3, a 20-year-old right-hand batter who bowls right-arm offbreak and came through Nepal’s Under-19s. Born in Sarlahi on 7 May 2006, his breakout innings came in May 2026, when his 72 helped Nepal A chase 263 to beat the United States at Mulpani — a genuine match-winning knock at representative level.

Season 2 scorecards show his Janakpur utility in action: he batted as high as No. 3 against Sudurpaschim Royals and No. 4 against Karnali Yaks, bowled a one-over spell in that Karnali game, and took two wickets against Lumbini Lions. The club kept him while rebuilding its overseas group: the retention, at 20, is a bet on development as much as on what he has already done.

In Season 3 he is the development asset in a win-now squad: back-up top-order batter and part-time off-spin option, the first reserve Harmeet Singh can plug into multiple XI shapes. His season so far is two games: 14 off 11 balls against Karnali Yaks in the opener, and 9 off 10 against Kathmandu Gorkhas in the eight-wicket win. No playing role is listed for him in the Season 3 squad record, so it is shown as TBC here.

Season 3 analysis

Twenty-three runs at an average of 11.50 and a strike rate of 109.52 is a small return, but the two innings say more than the total. Fourteen off 11 in the opener, batting in the top order, and 9 off 10 in the win over Kathmandu — both at better than a run a ball, in a side where the established batters have struck far slower. Trit Raj Das, the side’s big domestic buy, strikes at 94.12. Yadav, at 20, is already scoring faster than the anchor the club paid NPR 15 lakh for.

The question is why two games and not six. The answer is the same as for most of the domestic buys: the XI was settled early, and the overseas all-rounders plus the retained core filled the batting slots. But the side has lost four of six, has crossed 150 only twice, and was bowled out for 93 in Match 21. Friday against Chitwan Rhinos is a shootout, and a 20-year-old who has already won a representative match with a 72 is not the worst bet a captain can make when the season is on the line.
