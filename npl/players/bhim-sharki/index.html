Season 3 summary

Kathmandu Gorkhas have won four of their five matches in NPL Season 3, and Bhim Sharki's season reads like a small version of the team's: one bright innings at the start, then a supporting role while others took the headlines. In the opener against Janakpur Bolts on 29 October, Kathmandu were bowled out for 115. Sharki made 33 of them — the highest score in the innings, struck at better than a run a ball, with four fours.

That 33 is still his top score of the season. He made 5 against Karnali Yaks on 2 November, then finished unbeaten without scoring in the two most recent wins — 0 not out against Lumbini Lions on 7 November and 0 not out against Chitwan Rhinos on 9 November, both times at the crease when the innings closed. He did not bat in the nine-wicket win over Biratnagar Kings on 5 November, when Kathmandu needed only three batters to chase 84.

The numbers add up to 38 runs in four innings at a strike rate of 108.57, with two not-outs. It is a modest return, but it comes with a clear context: Sharki bats in the middle order of a side whose top order — David Warner, then Rashid Khan and Karan KC — has done most of the scoring, and two of Kathmandu's wins were finished so quickly that the middle order never got a proper hit.

Season 3 in numbers

Batting first, because that is where Sharki's season lives. The table below covers Kathmandu's five league matches — the opener against Janakpur, then Karnali, Biratnagar, Lumbini and Chitwan — and it is current through Match 20.

He has also bowled two balls in the season — 0 for 7 in the opener against Janakpur, when he was given the last over of a match already lost. Sharki bowls right-arm off-break, and it is a genuine second skill rather than a last resort, but Kathmandu have had little need for it: their front-line attack of Karan KC, Santosh Yadav, Shahab Alam and Sahan Arachchige has taken the wickets so far.

What the numbers say is simple. Sharki's one substantial innings came when Kathmandu needed it most, against the best bowling attack they have faced. Since then he has been the man at the other end when wins were sealed, not the man sealing them. With two league matches left — Sudurpaschim Royals on 13 November and Pokhara Avengers on 15 November — and David Warner gone home, there is a gap at the top of the order and a chance for the middle order to bat longer.

Match by match

Match 5, 29 October, vs Janakpur Bolts — 33 off 29 balls. Kathmandu were bowled out for 115, and Sharki's 33 was the only score above 20 in the innings. He hit four fours and held the innings together while wickets fell around him. Janakpur chased 116 with eight wickets to spare, and the defeat remains Kathmandu's only loss of the season. Match 10, 2 November, vs Karnali Yaks — 5 off 4 balls. A short stay in a high-scoring game. Kathmandu chased 181 to win by four wickets, and the runs came from Warner, Rashid Khan and Karan KC — Sharki was in and out before the chase took shape. Match 18, 7 November, vs Lumbini Lions — 0 not out off 0 balls. Kathmandu batted first and made 160 for 8. Sharki came to the crease at the very end of the innings and did not face a ball. The total was enough: Lumbini were bowled out for 124 and Kathmandu won by 36 runs. Match 20, 9 November, vs Chitwan Rhinos — 0 not out off 2 balls. The same story. Kathmandu made 180 for 8, Sharki was unbeaten at the close, and Chitwan fell 10 runs short. Two not-outs in two matches, two runs not scored — and two wins banked.
