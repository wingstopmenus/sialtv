Squad role

There is no match to describe yet, so the honest picture is the squad around him.

A second keeper waits on one thing: the first keeper’s fitness or form. Binod Bhandari has kept in the Royals’ matches all season and batted in five of them, scoring 61 runs at a strike rate of 124.5, so the gloves have not changed hands. Dipak Bohara’s Rs 10 lakh auction price shows the Royals rated him — no other squad player in the side was listed higher — but a price does not pick the eleven, and a settled winning side rarely changes its keeper.

His route in is narrow but real. Keepers get injured, and a long tournament with a playoff run can force a change late. The last league match, against Kathmandu Gorkhas on Friday 13 November, is the Royals’ final chance to rotate before the playoffs, though the top-two race means they are unlikely to rest a fit player. If he does play, it will be his first NPL appearance of the season — and his first chance to show why the Royals paid the side’s highest auction price for a keeper.
