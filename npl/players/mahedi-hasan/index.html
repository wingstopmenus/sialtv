Season 3 summary

Mahedi Hasan has taken five wickets in NPL Season 3, joint-most for Pokhara Avengers alongside Bipin Khatri. He has also scored 65 runs in five matches, and bowled the only maiden by a Pokhara bowler this season.

All-rounder. Overseas signing. Captain: Kushal Bhurtel. Head coach: Ajantha Mendis.

Mahedi Hasan is an all-rounder from Bangladesh. Born on 12 December 1994 in Khulna, he is a right-handed batter and right-arm off-break bowler. He made his T20I debut for Bangladesh against Sri Lanka on 18 February 2018 and his ODI debut against New Zealand on 20 March 2021. At the 2021 T20 World Cup he took 8 wickets in 7 innings at an average of 18.75, and he was also in Bangladesh’s squad for the 2024 T20 World Cup. He won a gold medal with Bangladesh in the men’s cricket tournament at the 2019 South Asian Games in Kathmandu and Pokhara.

His season has been five matches of two disciplines, every game played. The bowling: 1 for 31, 1 for 24, 0 for 27, 0 for 32 and 3 for 24 — the last of them in the only win. The batting: 32, 8, 13, 7 and 5 — the 32 against Sudurpaschim Royals in the opener was the second-highest score in Pokhara’s 145. He is the only Pokhara player to have played all five matches and taken a wicket in three of them.

Season 3 analysis

The bowling is the most reliable in the side: five wickets in 20 overs at an average of 27.60 and an economy of 6.90, with a maiden — the only one bowled by a Pokhara bowler this season. The 3 for 24 against Karnali Yaks in Match 19 is the best bowling performance in Pokhara’s only win, and it came after four games of 1 for 31, 1 for 24, 0 for 27 and 0 for 32. His economy of 6.90 is the second-tightest among Pokhara’s regular bowlers, behind only Shakib Al Hasan’s 4.50.

The batting is honest rather than decisive: 65 runs in five innings at an average of 13.00 and a strike rate of 89.0. The 32 off 21 against Sudurpaschim Royals in the opener, with four fours and a six, was his best — and it came in a game Pokhara lost by six wickets. He has not passed 13 since, which is the story of a bowler who bats rather than a batter who bowls. Two must-win matches remain, and Pokhara need his bowling more than his batting now.
