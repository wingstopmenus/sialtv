Season so far

Paktia picked last in the Platinum round, and it worked out. Wanindu Hasaranga, Sri Lanka’s leg-spinning all-rounder, was still there, and Paktia took him. Then came Noor Ahmad and Bangladesh fast bowler Nahid Rana in Diamond, and Ijaz Ahmadzai, Colin Munro, David Wiese and Hashmatullah Shahidi in Gold. Two frontline wrist-spinners in one squad is a luxury in any T20 league; in the UAE, it is a plan.

The 2018 side from the city was Paktia Panthers, and it had the tournament’s top scorer — Mohammad Shahzad, with 344 runs — and Isuru Udana’s 17 wickets. That club is not this club, and its record stays with it. Shahzad, the 2018 top scorer, now plays for Kandahar Warriors. Paktia start 2026-27 with a blank record: played 0, won 0.

The captain is the man the city already knows. Rahmanullah Gurbaz opens the batting and keeps wicket for Afghanistan, and he leads a squad that bats deep: Munro, Shahidi, Wiese and Gerhard Erasmus — who captains Namibia — have all batted in the top six for their countries, and Olly Stone’s pace leads the seam attack. Azhar Mahmood coaches the side, with Waqar Younis as mentor.

What to watch

The spin is the story. Hasaranga and Noor Ahmad are two of the best wrist-spinners in world cricket, and they bowl in the same team. In the UAE, where wrist spin has decided tournament after tournament, Paktia can take wickets in the middle overs while other sides are trying not to go for runs. Behind them, Farmanullah Safi and the Emerging picks fill out the attack.

The batting is just as deep. Gurbaz, Munro, Shahidi, Wiese and Erasmus have all batted in the top six for their countries, and Nahid Rana and Olly Stone give Mahmood genuine pace at both ends. The question is the same one every side faces — nineteen players who have never played together, with eight league matches to become a team — and whether two wrist-spinners are enough cover when the pitches flatten out.

Watch Nahid Rana. The Bangladesh fast bowler is the quickest in this squad, and on UAE pitches that have something in them early, he is the man who can take the top order out before the spinners even start. If Paktia’s plan works, Rana takes the wickets up front and Hasaranga and Noor finish the job.

Sixteen points is the maximum; four wins should be enough to stay in the race, and net run rate decides the ties.
