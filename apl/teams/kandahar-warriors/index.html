Season so far

Kandahar Warriors opened the APL draft, and they opened it with a statement. Muhammad Amir, the first pick of the whole draft and the only Pakistani picked among the 95 players, went to Kandahar in Platinum. Then came Mohammad Nabi, the first pick of the Diamond round, and England keeper-batter Joe Clarke. Three rounds in, the Warriors had the new-ball bowler, the most experienced Afghan all-rounder there is and an overseas keeper — and the rest of the league was catching up.

The captain is the biggest name of all. Rashid Khan is the franchise’s Afghan Icon, and he held Afghanistan’s T20I captaincy before Ibrahim Zadran took it over. He is joined by two more men who played the 2018 season — Nabi and Mohammad Shahzad — though none of that season’s numbers carry over to this club. The 2018 side from Kandahar was Kandahar Knights, a different club, and its record stays with it.

Ravi Bopara coaches the side, with Irfan Pathan as mentor, and the squad they were handed has an obvious shape: spin through the middle, pace at both ends, and batting that runs deep. Karim Janat, Kusal Perera and Tymal Mills came in Gold; Shahzad, Dwaine Pretorius and George Munsey in Silver. On paper, no side in the league has more match-winners.

What to watch

The spin attack is the best in the league on paper, and it is not close. Rashid Khan and Mohammad Nabi have bowled together for Afghanistan for a decade, and behind them are Karim Janat’s off-spin and the left-arm pace of Farid Ahmad Malik. In the UAE, where the ball has gripped for spinners in tournament after tournament, Kandahar can squeeze a game in the middle overs like no other side.

The new ball is just as strong. Amir’s left-arm swing, Tymal Mills’ pace and Farid Malik’s angle give Bopara three different ways to start an innings, and Dwaine Pretorius covers the death. The batting has power — Shahzad, Perera, Clarke, Munsey — but it also has a question: Shahzad and Perera are keepers by trade, and only one pair of gloves is available.

Watch Rashid the captain. He has captained Afghanistan and franchises around the world, but this is his first APL season as a franchise captain in a new league, with a new squad, and the whole competition will be judged against him. If Kandahar’s spin wins games, it will be because their captain set the fields for it.

Points table

Five teams chase four playoff places, so one side goes home after the league stage. Each team plays eight league matches — each opponent twice — and a win is worth 2 points. Sixteen points is the maximum; four wins should be enough to stay in the race, and net run rate decides the ties. The table below is empty because the season has not started: every team begins on 0 points, and the first update comes after Match 1 on 27 December.

For Kandahar, the maths should be the easy part. A side with this much spin and this much pace does not need favours from other results — it needs to win the games it is expected to win, and let the table take care of itself.
