A West Indian keeper, at the peak of his powers

Amir Jangoo is one of Nangarhar’s five Silver picks, alongside Zahir Khan, Ismatullah Alam, Mohammad Yamin Ahmadzai and Shafiqullah Ghafari, at a reported $20,000. The season starts on 27 December 2026 in the UAE, and it will be his APL debut: he was twenty-one and uncapped when the only previous APL season was played in October 2018, and there is no record of him in any of the five squads that season.

Jangoo is an established West Indies international in all three formats — Test cap 342, ODI cap 227, T20I cap 106 — and his last twelve months have been the best of his career. The headline is a Test double hundred of 233, the kind of innings that changes how a player is seen. He is a left-hand batter who bowls right-arm off-spin, born in St James, Trinidad, on 14 July 1997, and his full name is Amir Anthony Jangoo.

For Cosmic, he gives the side a second wicketkeeper-batter alongside Ikram Alikhil, and a left-hand option in the middle order. Two keepers in a nineteen-man squad is a luxury, and it means Radford can pick the gloves on form and conditions rather than on availability alone.

Where Nangarhar stand

Nangarhar Cosmic’s overseas group is five strong — Hales, Shakib, Kamindu Mendis, Junaid Siddique and Jangoo — and Jangoo is the one arriving in the best form. Captain Rahmat Shah leads the side, Toby Radford coaches it, and Mark Boucher mentors it.

Cosmic play eight league matches, two against each of the other four teams, with four playoff places on offer.
