A domestic batter’s first big stage

Allah Noor Naseri was born on 22 April 2003 in Nangarhar, Afghanistan. He is a right-handed batter who bowls right-arm off-break, and he joins Kandahar Warriors for APL 2026-27 as a Silver pick from the player draft in Dubai on 3 November 2026.

His cricket so far has been played entirely in Afghan domestic and age-group cricket — and the APL is the first place he will be asked to play Twenty20 cricket alongside senior international players. His List A debut came in August 2019, ten months after the first APL season ended, so he did not play in 2018.

What he offers Kandahar is a right-handed top-order batter in form. His best List A score is 109, a hundred that shows he can build an innings as well as hit through the line, and his off-break gives Ravi Bopara a part-time option if a match needs one.

He is one of five Silver picks for Kandahar, alongside Mohammad Shahzad, Muslim Musa, Dwaine Pretorius and George Munsey, and one of twenty-five Silver players picked across the five franchises. The Silver round is where franchises fill out their squads with established domestic cricketers — and Naseri is the young domestic batter in a room full of internationals.

Learning from the best in the room

Naseri joins a squad captained by Rashid Khan, coached by Ravi Bopara and mentored by Irfan Pathan, with overseas names in Mohammad Amir, Joe Clarke, Kusal Perera, Tymal Mills and Dwaine Pretorius, and an Afghan core of Mohammad Nabi, Karim Janat, Hassan Eisakhil, Farid Ahmad Malik and Mohammad Shahzad. For a young domestic batter, that dressing room is a finishing school.

His route into the XI is through the top order, where Kandahar have power but also questions — three keepers, two openers, and a middle order that will change from game to game. A right-handed batter who can bat anywhere from one to five is the kind of player a coach keeps close.

The Afghanistan Premier League 2026 season starts on 27 December 2026, and Kandahar play eight league matches. Naseri may not play all eight — but in a five-team league with four playoff places, the domestic players who take their chances are often the ones who decide the table.
