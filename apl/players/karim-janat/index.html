Seventy-seven T20Is of bowling all-round craft

Karim Janat was born on 11 August 1998 in Kabul, Afghanistan. He is a bowling all-rounder who has played 77 T20Is for Afghanistan, and he joins Kandahar Warriors for APL 2026-27 as a Gold pick from the player draft in Dubai on 3 November 2026, for a reported US$40,000.

His best day in international cricket came in Lucknow in November 2019, when he took 5 for 11 against West Indies — the kind of spell that wins a T20 match on its own. With the bat he holds a Shpageeza League record: a highest T20 score of 120, proof that the “bowling” in his job title only tells half the story.

At 28 he is in the middle of his career, not at the start of it, and Kandahar bought him for exactly that: a proven international who can bowl in the powerplay or at the death and bat in the lower middle order. He did not play the 2018 APL season, so this is his APL debut.

He is also part of the Afghan core of the squad — alongside Rashid Khan, Mohammad Nabi, Hassan Eisakhil, Farid Ahmad Malik and Mohammad Shahzad — which matters in a dressing room with five overseas signings. The local players set the tone; Janat is one of the ones who does.

Karim Janat in numbers

The season has not started, so there are no APL 2026-27 numbers for Karim Janat yet. The first ball is on Sunday 27 December 2026, and his figures for this season will be added here after Match 1.

His international record is the benchmark: 77 T20Is for Afghanistan, best figures of 5 for 11 against West Indies, and a highest T20 score of 120 in the Shpageeza League. Kandahar will measure his season against those marks.

The third spinner, the second seamer

Janat’s value is that he fills two gaps at once. Behind Rashid Khan and Mohammad Nabi, his off-spin gives Kandahar a third spin option for the middle overs; with the ball in hand as a seamer, he gives Ravi Bopara a bowler who can take the hard overs that neither spinner wants.

That flexibility is what a five-team league rewards. Kandahar play eight league matches, two against each opponent, and the same XI will not suit every game — a spin-heavy pitch one night, a flat one the next. Janat is the player who lets Bopara change the balance without changing the team.

His batting, meanwhile, lengthens a lower order that already has Nabi, Pretorius and Rashid in it. In a league where four of five teams reach the playoffs, the difference between first and fourth is often one lower-order partnership — and Janat is built for exactly that job.

The Afghanistan Premier League 2026 season starts on 27 December 2026, and Janat’s two-way game is built for a short league.
