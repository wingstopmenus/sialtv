Not a highlight reel — a captain

Gerhard Erasmus was born in Windhoek, Namibia, on 11 April 1995. He bats right-handed, bowls right-arm off-break, and he has led Namibia since 2019 — through two T20 World Cups as skipper, a first ODI series win over a Full Member, and an ICC Associate Cricketer of the Year award.

Paktia picked him in the Silver category at the Dubai draft on 3 November 2026, for a reported $20,000. It is the kind of signing that tells you what a franchise values: not a highlight reel, but a captain. Paktia have bought the man who holds a dressing room together, and who happens to bat in the middle order and bowl four overs of off-spin that have troubled India.

He plays under Rahmanullah Gurbaz, with Azhar Mahmood as head coach and Waqar Younis as mentor, alongside a second Namibian all-rounder in David Wiese — the two have shared a dressing room at three World Cups.

In form, and already a winner

Erasmus has been the face of Namibian cricket for most of his adult life. He first played at senior level for Namibia in February 2011, aged fifteen, against a touring Marylebone Cricket Club side, and made his first-class and international debut against Ireland in September 2011 in the ICC Intercontinental Cup, at sixteen.

He arrives in form that few overseas signings can match. A month before the draft, he carried Namibia to a 3–2 ODI series win over Ireland — their first bilateral ODI series win over a Full Member — as Player of the Series with 263 runs and four wickets, including a career-best unbeaten 156 in Windhoek. Nine months earlier, at the 2026 T20 World Cup, he had taken four for 20 against India in Delhi.

He has played 92 T20I matches for Namibia. Paktia have not signed a player hoping he finds form in the UAE; they have signed one who is already in it.

Steady the innings, bowl for control, make the call

Erasmus’s job is the one he has done for Namibia for seven years: steady the innings when wickets fall, bowl when the captain needs control rather than pace, and make the right call in the field when the game tightens.

He is also the second captain in Paktia’s squad, after Gurbaz himself, and the second Namibian all-rounder alongside Wiese. A new franchise of nineteen players who have never played together needs more than talent — it needs people who know how a team works, and Erasmus has been doing that job since 2019.

A new club, a blank record

Twenty league matches, three playoffs and a final: that is the shape of APL 2026-27. Five teams play each other twice, eight matches apiece, and four of them reach the playoffs, so one side is out after the league stage. A win is worth two points. The table is still blank — the season has not started, and the first update comes after Match 1 on 27 December 2026, first ball 5:30 PM Afghanistan time (5:00 PM Dubai time) in the UAE, with the final on Wednesday 20 January 2027.

Paktia are a new club for 2026-27, not a continuation of the 2018 Paktia Panthers. Erasmus was 23 and a year into the Namibia captaincy when that first season was played, and he was not among its overseas cast. The Afghanistan Premier League season starts on 27 December 2026 in the UAE.
