Rashid Khan — the captain carrying Kandahar

Rashid Khan captains Kandahar Warriors in APL 2026-27. The leg-spinner is the biggest name in Afghan cricket, the first bowler ever to 200 T20I wickets, and he returns to the APL eight years after being named Player of the Series in 2018.

The biggest name in the league, in Kandahar colours

Rashid Khan was born on 20 September 1998 in Nangarhar Province, and he bowls right-arm leg-break googlies and bats right-handed. He captains Kandahar Warriors in APL 2026-27, picked in the Afghan Icon category at the draft in Dubai on 3 November 2026, with Ravi Bopara as head coach and Irfan Pathan as mentor.

His first APL season is the reason this one carries so much weight. In 2018 he captained Kabul Zwanan at Sharjah and was named Player of the Series even though his side lost the final to Balkh Legends. The semi-final settled it: 35 not out off 19 balls against Paktia Panthers, then 4 for 20 as Paktia were bowled out for 102 and Kabul won by 90 runs.

That season he finished with 173 runs and 10 wickets, and the numbers only tell half of it — he shaped games with bat and ball in the same night, which is exactly what Kandahar bought him to do again. Eight years on he returns in different colours, and with more to carry: this time he is the franchise captain of a new club, not the captain of a side that already existed.

Beyond the APL he is the standard-bearer for Afghan cricket. He made his ODI debut on 18 October 2015 and his T20I debut on 26 October 2015, both against Zimbabwe, and his Test debut on 14 June 2018 against India. He held Afghanistan’s T20I captaincy before Ibrahim Zadran took it over, and he has captained franchises around the world.

Rashid Khan in numbers

The season has not started, so there are no APL 2026-27 numbers for Rashid Khan yet. The first ball is on Sunday 27 December 2026, and his figures for this season will be added here after Match 1.

What exists so far is his 2018 record, and it is the best single-season return by any player in that tournament: 173 runs at a strike rate of 198.85 and 10 wickets at an economy of 6.59, the third-best economy in the tournament. His best night was the semi-final against Paktia Panthers — 35 not out off 19, then 4 for 20 in 3.5 overs.

What Rashid means to this Kandahar side

Kandahar built their whole draft around their captain. They held the first pick in both the Platinum and Diamond rounds, used the first overall pick on Muhammad Amir, then added Mohammad Nabi, Joe Clarke, Kusal Perera and Tymal Mills around Rashid — spin through the middle, pace at both ends, and batting that runs deep.

The shape of the side suits him. With Nabi’s off-spin beside him and Karim Janat’s off-spin behind them, Rashid can attack from one end while the other holds, and in the UAE, where the ball has gripped for spinners in tournament after tournament, that middle-overs squeeze is Kandahar’s clearest route to wins. Five teams chase four playoff places, so one side goes home after the league stage — Kandahar’s job is to make sure it is not them.

Watch Rashid the captain as much as Rashid the bowler. He has captained Afghanistan and franchises around the world, but this is his first APL season as a franchise captain in a new league, with a new squad, and the whole competition will be judged against him. If Kandahar’s spin wins games, it will be because their captain set the fields for it.

The Afghanistan Premier League returns after eight years, and Kandahar’s captain is the man the whole season will be measured against.
