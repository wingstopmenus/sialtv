A rare bowler, in a squad full of spin

Noor Ahmad Lakanwal was born in Khost on 3 January 2005, the youngest of eight children, and he bowls left-arm wrist spin. He bats right-handed, and he is the bowler in Paktia’s squad that opposition batters will have faced the least — because there are so few like him.

Paktia took him in the Diamond round at the Dubai draft on 3 November 2026, alongside Bangladesh fast bowler Nahid Rana. He plays under captain Rahmanullah Gurbaz, with Azhar Mahmood as head coach and Waqar Younis as mentor, and he will share the spin duties with Platinum pick Wanindu Hasaranga — a right-arm leg-spinner at one end, a left-arm wrist-spinner at the other.

He was thirteen when the first APL season was played in 2018, and he was not part of that league. The 2026-27 season is his APL debut.

Fourteen at first-class level, nineteen on T20I debut

Noor Ahmad made his first-class debut for Kabul Region on 29 April 2019, in the Ahmad Shah Abdali 4-day Tournament, at just fourteen years old. His franchise career took off in July 2020, when he was signed by the St Lucia Zouks for the Caribbean Premier League, and his IPL breakthrough came in February 2022, when Gujarat Titans acquired him at the auction.

He made his T20I debut against Zimbabwe at Harare on 14 June 2022 and took four wickets, earning the Player of the Match award. His ODI debut followed on 30 November 2022 against Sri Lanka. Since then he has played in franchise leagues around the world — the BBL, where he was the youngest bowler ever to play in the competition, the CPL, the PSL and the IPL.

The 2025 IPL season was his best yet. Playing for Chennai Super Kings, he finished as the team’s most successful bowler, with 24 wickets in 14 matches, and he was named in Afghanistan’s squad for the 2026 ICC Men’s T20 World Cup.

A different angle, from the same hand

Left-arm wrist spin is rare enough that most batters face it a handful of times a year. Noor Ahmad turns the ball away from right-handers, bowls a googly they have to pick, and does it at an age — twenty-one — when most spinners are still learning their craft.

For Gurbaz, the value is the pairing. Hasaranga’s leg spin and Noor’s chinaman attack from opposite ends, and a batter who has just survived one has to reset against the other. In the UAE, where the middle overs decide most T20 games, that is the whole point of Paktia’s squad.

A new club, a blank record

Twenty league matches, three playoffs and a final: that is the shape of APL 2026-27. Five teams play each other twice, eight matches apiece, and four of them reach the playoffs, so one side is out after the league stage. A win is worth two points. The table is still blank — the season has not started, and the first update comes after Match 1 on 27 December 2026, first ball 5:30 PM Afghanistan time (5:00 PM Dubai time) in the UAE, with the final on Wednesday 20 January 2027.

Paktia are a new club for 2026-27, not a continuation of the 2018 Paktia Panthers. Do not confuse Noor Ahmad with Hasibullah Lakanwal, another Paktia squad member who shares the surname — they are different players. The APL T20 season starts on 27 December 2026 in the UAE.
