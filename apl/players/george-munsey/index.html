Big, fast, at the top of the order

Henry George Munsey was born on 21 February 1993 in Oxford, England, and he plays for Scotland. He is a left-handed opening batter who bowls right-arm medium-fast, and he joins Kandahar Warriors for APL 2026-27 as a Silver pick from the player draft in Dubai on 3 November 2026, for a reported US$20,000.

His record is the one Kandahar bought. He owns Scotland’s highest scores in both white-ball formats: 191 in ODIs against Netherlands at Forthill in 2025, and 132 in T20Is against Austria at Edinburgh in 2023. He has three T20I hundreds — 127 not out, 132 and 108 — and in 88 T20Is he has scored 2,598 runs at a strike rate of 143.93.

He made his T20I debut on 18 June 2015 against Ireland at Bready, and his ODI debut on 22 January 2017 against Hong Kong in Abu Dhabi. A decade later he is still Scotland’s first name on the team sheet, and the only Scottish player in the 2026 APL squads.

He was not there the first time round. He was 25 when the inaugural APL season was played in Sharjah in October 2018, and his name appears in none of the five squads of that season. The 2026 season is his APL debut, eight years later.

George Munsey in numbers

The season has not started, so there are no APL 2026-27 numbers for George Munsey yet. The first ball is on Sunday 27 December 2026, and his figures for this season will be added here after Match 1.

His T20I record is the benchmark: 88 matches, 2,598 runs, a strike rate of 143.93, with 278 fours and 118 sixes, and three hundreds. He opens the batting, so he is the first name the opposition plans for — and the first wicket they want.

The left-hander at the top

Munsey’s job is the simplest in the side and the hardest to do: score big, fast, at the top of the order. Kandahar’s powerplay batting has been built around him and Hassan Eisakhil opening together — a left-hander and a right-hander, one with a decade of international cricket behind him and one with a BPL hundred in January.

His left hand matters against the new ball, and his strike rate of 143.93 across 88 T20Is says he does not need time to settle. In a short season of eight league matches, one Munsey hundred could be the difference between second place and fourth — and with four playoff places for five teams, that difference is everything.

The Afghanistan Premier League is his first franchise league in Asia, and it puts him in a dressing room with Rashid Khan, Muhammad Amir and Mohammad Nabi. For a Scottish opener, that is the biggest stage he has played on outside international cricket.
