Defending champions — nine years later

Peshawar Zalmi won their first PSL title in 2017, beating Quetta Gladiators by 58 runs in Lahore. Then came the long wait: four lost finals, and nine years without a trophy, before the 2026 final brought it back. They chased 185 against Hyderabad Kingsmen at Gaddafi Stadium on 3 May 2026 and won by five wickets, with two balls to spare.

That win changed what Peshawar Zalmi are. For nine years they were the league’s nearly team — always there, always short. Now they are the champions, and PSL 12 is the first season they start as the team everyone else is trying to beat. No team has defended the PSL except Lahore Qalandars in 2022 and 2023, so the history Zalmi are chasing is a short list.

Babar Azam captained the side in 2026, steadying the chase in the final and leading from the front all season. His captaincy is the first thing to settle for 2027: a defending champion that keeps its captain starts the season with its identity intact.

The champions have to do it again

Peshawar’s strength is the one thing no other team in PSL 12 has: they know how this season ends, because they just lived it. The 2026 final was won from a chase, under pressure, with two balls to spare — the kind of win that settles a dressing room. A defending champion does not need to learn how to win a final. It needs to remember how.

What to watch is the retention list. Champions usually keep their core, and Zalmi’s core is built around Babar Azam. If the batting group that chased 185 in the final stays together, Peshawar start 2027 as favourites. If the auction breaks it up, the title defence starts with a rebuild, and rebuilds rarely defend titles.

The other watch point is the target on their back. Every team raises its game against the champions, and the league has a new franchise and a runners-up side with a point to prove. Zalmi will not get an easy match in PSL 12 — that is what being champions costs.

The table and the top four

The points table is empty — PSL 12 starts on Thursday 18 March 2027, and the first standings appear after Match 1. Eight teams play ten league matches each, and the top four go through to the playoffs. With eight teams instead of six, the middle of the table is tighter: a side can win half its games and still miss out, so dropped points against the bottom teams hurt more than they used to.

For Peshawar, the maths is the same as for everyone else, but the expectation is different. Champions do not aim for fourth place. The realistic target is a top-two finish, which brings two chances at the final; fourth place means one bad day ends the defence.
