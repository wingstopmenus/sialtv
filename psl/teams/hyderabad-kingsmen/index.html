Runners-up in year one

Hyderabad Kingsmen joined the PSL in 2026 as an expansion franchise, and did what no new team in the league had done before: they reached the final in their first season. Peshawar Zalmi beat them by five wickets at Gaddafi Stadium on 3 May 2026, chasing 185 with two balls to spare — close, but a loss all the same.

That debut matters more than the defeat. Expansion teams are supposed to take time. Hyderabad took a season to reach a final, which means they start PSL 12 not as newcomers finding their feet but as a proven side with a point to prove. The teams that beat them in 2026 will not be caught out twice; the Kingsmen have to find a second way to win.

Marnus Labuschagne captained the side in 2026, an unusual pick for a T20 franchise and a deliberate one — a calm, methodical leader for a brand-new team. Whether he keeps the job in 2027 is the first decision the franchise has to make, and it will say a lot about whether they see 2026 as a beginning or a one-off.

The second season is the real test

Hyderabad’s strength is that they have nothing to unlearn. They were built from scratch in 2026, so there is no old core to protect and no losing habit to break. The final run proved the build worked — and a franchise that reaches a final in year one has earned the right to be taken seriously in year two.

What to watch is the retention list. A debut finalist usually keeps most of its side, and Hyderabad’s choices will show which players the franchise believes were the reason for the run. The auction is where they add the pieces a finalist needs: a finisher, a death bowler, the two roles that decide close finals.

The other watch point is the rematch with Peshawar Zalmi. The champions beat them in the 2026 final, and every meeting between the two in PSL 12 will carry that history. If Hyderabad can beat the champions in the league stage, the rest of the league will know the debut was not a fluke.

The table and the top four

The points table is empty — PSL 12 starts on Thursday 18 March 2027, and the first standings appear after Match 1. Eight teams play ten league matches each, and the top four go through to the playoffs. With eight teams instead of six, the middle of the table is tighter: a side can win half its games and still miss out, so dropped points against the bottom teams hurt more than they used to.

For Hyderabad, the maths is the same as for everyone else, but the expectation has changed. A debut finalist does not aim for fourth place. The realistic target is a top-two finish, which brings two chances at the final — and this time, a second chance at the team that beat them.
