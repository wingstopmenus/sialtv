A name returns, under new owners

Multan Sultans won their only PSL title in 2021, beating Peshawar Zalmi by 47 runs in Abu Dhabi. What followed is the hardest record in the league to carry: three straight final defeats in 2022, 2023 and 2024, each time to a different champion. They reach finals more often than almost anyone — and win them less often than anyone would like.

The 2027 season brings a bigger change than any result. The franchise behind the name has changed hands: the Sialkot franchise, picked as the eighth team in January 2026 by OZ Developers for Rs 1.85bn, was sold to CD Ventures, which revived the Multan Sultans name for it. The old Multan Sultans franchise went the other way — bought by Walee Technologies, moved to Rawalpindi and renamed Rawalpindiz.

So the Multan Sultans of PSL 12 are, in ownership terms, a new team carrying an old name — and an old record. Ashton Turner captained the side in 2026. Who leads it in 2027, and who plays in it, will be settled by the retentions and the auction on 23 January 2027.

Finals are the habit — winning them is the job

Multan’s strength is getting there. Four finals in four seasons from 2021 to 2024 is a record of consistency no other franchise in that period can match. The problem is the other side of the same record: three of those finals were lost, and a team that keeps reaching finals and losing them starts to carry that history into the next one.

What to watch is how much of the old side survives the ownership change. A new owner usually means new decisions — new captain, new overseas picks, new priorities at the auction. If CD Ventures keep the core that kept reaching finals, Multan start 2027 as contenders. If they rebuild, the first job is not winning a title but building a side that can reach a final at all.

The other watch point is the captaincy. Ashton Turner led in 2026; a new name in 2027 would be the clearest sign that this is a fresh start, not a continuation.

The table and the top four

The points table is empty — PSL 12 starts on Thursday 18 March 2027, and the first standings appear after Match 1. Eight teams play ten league matches each, and the top four go through to the playoffs. With eight teams instead of six, the middle of the table is tighter: a side can win half its games and still miss out, so dropped points against the bottom teams hurt more than they used to.

For Multan, the maths is simple and unforgiving. Fourth place is the minimum, and fourth place means one bad day ends the season. The realistic target is a top-two finish, which brings two chances at the final — and a team with three lost finals knows better than most what a second chance is worth.
